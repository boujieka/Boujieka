# Hydropower Development and Finance: Evidence Base for Developer and Financier Chapters

**Scope:** How hydropower projects, especially 5-150 MW private IPPs and run-of-river schemes in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA), are developed and financed.
**Prepared:** 2026-10-04
**Status:** Research note for book authors and the development-model defaults. It does not replace a legal or financial opinion.

---

## Methodology

1. **Source hierarchy.** Sources are ranked in this order: (a) primary institutional documents (regulator publications, DFI project disclosures, programme briefs, policy texts); (b) institutional guides (IFC/World Bank); (c) DFI press releases; (d) trade press and law-firm guides quoting primary sources; (e) secondary aggregators. Wikipedia appears only as a cross-check and is flagged.
2. **Verification marks.**
   - Figures with no mark were read on the cited page or in the downloaded PDF during this session. PDFs were downloaded and text-extracted where possible: the IFC hydro guide, the ERA GET FiT brief, the World Bank GET FiT knowledge note, the Kenya FiT Policy 2012, the ALN Uganda/Kenya/Tanzania power guides and the KfW GET FiT factsheet.
   - **(snippet)** means the figure was seen only in a search-engine result summary and the page itself was not opened, or could not be opened because of 403/404/Cloudflare.
   - **(derived)** means this note computed the number from verified inputs. The arithmetic is shown.
   - **PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND** means a targeted search did not find a citable public figure.
3. **Access limits.** Several key primary sources could not be read: the GET FiT Uganda annual reports (simplebooklet links now return 404), getfit-uganda.org (503), the IEA Uganda case study (403), the McKinsey PDF (503), the ICA project-preparation report (403/Cloudflare), the AfDB SEFA pages (403), the ppp.worldbank.org PDFs (403) and the full Eberhard et al. (2016) book. Where these matter, the figure is marked (snippet) or replaced by a verified proxy.
4. **No invention.** No number, name or URL in this file was invented. Where sources disagree (for example, Achwa 2 project cost), all figures are shown.

## 1. Development stages and typical durations

### 1.1 Stage durations (IFC/World Bank guide, Fichtner data)

The IFC "Hydroelectric Power: A Guide for Developers and Investors" (2015) is still the most specific institutional benchmark [S1].

| Stage | Small HPP (SHPP) | Medium/large HPP | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site identification / first-phase assessment | 1-6 months | 4 months - 1 year | [S1] Table 5-1 |
| Pre-feasibility study (PFS) | ~1 month (report ~30 pages) | 3-6 months (up to 100 pages) | [S1] s.5.2 |
| Feasibility study (FS) | 2-6 months | 9-18 months (most FS are 300+ pages) | [S1] Table 5-2 |
| Financing and contractual arrangements | "can still take up to six months" | EPC tender docs and procurement "up to 18 months"; BOO tender "up to two years" | [S1] s.5.4 |
| Detailed design | "a few months" | "more than a year" | [S1] s.5.5 |
| Construction | 9-18 months | up to 4 years | [S1] s.5.6 |
| Trial operation / reliability run | 3-10 days | ~30 days | [S1] s.5.7 |

Other points from the IFC guide [S1]:
- Lenders first engage during the FS. A mandate letter is usually signed before lender due diligence.
- Financial close "might be achieved during this phase or in the detailed design phase".
- Developers sometimes start design and even construction before financial close (FC), and then "a higher equity contribution is required".

These durations are engineering-study times. They leave out permitting, land, PPA negotiation and financing queues, which usually set the critical path in SSA (see 1.3).

### 1.2 Total development time: institutional statements

- **IFC InfraVentures (2015):** "development + construction can be 8-10 yrs (hydros)" [S2]. This is the strongest institutional statement of total hydro lead time. It covers all hydro sizes and is not SSA-specific.
- **Long-delayed examples:**
  - **Ngonye Falls** (Zambia; InfraCo Africa is a shareholder) has been "under development for more than a decade". It began as a 45 MW design and became 180 MW. As of 19 June 2026 it had not reached FC, although it had completed feasibility studies, environmental approvals, water rights and a ZESCO PPA [S3].
  - **Nyagak III** (Uganda, 6.6 MW new units) was launched in 2015 and commissioned on 1 August 2025, about a decade later. The delay came after "an international financier withdrew funding citing land acquisition issues" [S4].
- **Uganda generation licence:** ERA's maximum processing time is "180 days". Licence validity is "usually 40 years". The application must include an EIA approval, an off-take agreement and financing documentation [S5]. This source is a secondary procedure wiki, so confidence is medium.
- **GET FiT Uganda solar auction:** the full process from RfP to winner selection took "approximately 10 months" [S6]. This is a useful benchmark for a structured procurement window.
- **Generic SSA hydro lead time from conception to FC:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND as a statistical average. The Eberhard et al. (2016) World Bank book [S7] could not be text-extracted. A search snippet (source not opened) said "lead times associated with the development of new hydro plants in Uganda exceed 5 years from financial close" (snippet; source not identified, so do not use).

### 1.3 Permits, water rights, land, grid, PPA and IA (Uganda reference case)

- REFiT projects must sign the ERA-approved standardised PPA. Projects >20 MW negotiate with UETCL using the standard PPA as the base [S8].
- A standardised PPA and Implementation Agreement (IA) were first drafted for small hydro SPPs and later adapted for solar [S6].
- GET FiT eligibility required: a valid ERA development permit, a concluded feasibility study, and compliance with the IFC Performance Standards (2012) [S9].
- Individual durations for water-abstraction permit, land acquisition and grid-connection study in SSA: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. The ESIA duration for small hydro in SSA was not found in a citable primary source either.

### 2.2 Development cost as a percentage of CAPEX

- **PIDA/NEPAD-IPPF planning ratio:** project preparation resources were estimated at 2-5% of investment costs (snippet [S19]; AU/PIDA document, not opened).
- **IFC InfraVentures implied development budget:** IFC funds up to US$8m, "avg. $4M for 20-30% share" of development expenses, for projects with construction financing needs ideally >US$200m [S2]. (derived) A US$4m share at 20-30% implies a total development budget of about US$13-20m. For a US$200m project that is about 6.7-10% of CAPEX. This is an inference for large projects, not a published ratio.
- **Small hydro, illustrative (derived):** Castalia's US$1-2.5m [S18] against verified small-hydro CAPEX of US$13.5m (Muvumbe) to US$87m (Kikagati) (Section 8) gives a range of about 1% to 19%. The ratio rises steeply as project size falls. This is an analytical range, not a published benchmark.
- A published split of development cost by stage, as a share of total development budget: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.

### 2.3 Who funds each stage

- **Site identification:** "Typically, the developer funds these investigations but an alternative source of funding could be venture capital" [S1].
- **Feasibility to FC:** sponsor equity, plus co-development capital (IFC InfraVentures cost-sharing convertible to equity [S2][S20]; Africa50 Project Development minority stakes [S21]; InfraCo Africa development equity, for example Ngonye [S3]) and grants (SEFA [S17][S22]; EU-ACP Energy Facility; Sida [S16]).
- **Construction:** equity plus senior and subordinated DFI debt (Section 8). Results-based grants such as the GET FiT premium are paid only from COD onward [S9].

### 3.2 Reasons for failure (institutional lists and cases)

- **IFC InfraVentures list** of why RE projects do not reach FC [S2]:
  - political and/or regulatory uncertainty;
  - off-taker credit concerns;
  - risk allocation in project documents;
  - E&S concerns;
  - risky financial structure;
  - limited technical experience of the sponsor;
  - limited access to capital by the sponsor.
- **McKinsey via Brookings:** governments and developers lack capabilities and budgets for project preparation [S23].
- **Case evidence:**
  - Land acquisition caused a financier to withdraw (Nyagak III) [S4].
  - Scale-up, re-design and a decade-long search for financing (Ngonye Falls) [S3].
  - Lenders perceived Zambia as a weaker market. Sovereign guarantees and tariff/PPA structuring were sticking points (IHA interview with the Lunsemfwa founder) [S27].
- **Hydrology:** the IFC guide warns that merging the PFS and FS for small HPPs "may appear to save time and money but is often the reverse". It cites "overestimating hydrology, underestimating CAPEX, sub-optimal turbine design" [S1].

### 5.1 GET FiT Uganda: verified design and results

- **Instruments:** (1) premium payment mechanism, a results-based per-kWh top-up on REFiT; (2) a guarantee facility (World Bank PRG, MIGA likely); (3) a private financing mechanism through Deutsche Bank [S9].
- **Premium levels (ERA brief and World Bank note):**

| Technology | REFiT (US$/kWh) | GET FiT premium (US$/kWh) | Max. capacity factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydro 9-20 MW | 0.085 | 0.014 | 60% |
| Hydro 1-8.9 MW | 0.115-0.085 | 0.014 | 60% |
| Hydro 0.5-1 MW | 0.109 | not included | n/a |

Sources: [S9][S6]

- **Payment profile:** the premium is calculated on 20 years of expected output but paid within the first 5 years. 50% is paid at COD after an independent performance test; the remaining 50% is paid in annual 10% tranches over 5 years against energy delivered. The formula shares part of the hydrological risk [S6][S9].
- **Selection:** non-price competition scored on financial/economic, technical/organisational and E&S criteria, with IFC PS compliance. An independent Investment Committee made the decisions [S6].
- **Project-level premiums:** Siti 1 (5 MW) €2.9m [S38]; Nyamagasani 1 and 2 together €11.6m [S39]; Nyamwamba US$5.8m (snippet) [S40].
- **Leverage:**
  - KfW (May 2017): about €94m of public funds for €450m of private investment; 17 projects, 156 MW [S35].
  - ERA: about US$375m private finance; grant ≈7% of 20-year cash flow [S31].
  - KfW SDG portal (snippet): "some USD 428 million has been leveraged on USD 104 million of top-up grants" [S41].
- **Portfolio:** 17 projects / 158 MW (snippet) [S25]. The KfW 2017 factsheet reported 1 bagasse, 1 solar and 2 hydro plants complete [S35]. A later snippet reports 11 hydro plants complete [S41]. The exact final count of hydro projects is not verified (annual reports offline).

## 7. Construction contracting for small and medium hydro

- **EPC vs multi-contract.** Under turnkey EPC, "Risk is transferred to the prime contractor, which raises the overall contract price compared to a lot-by-lot contracting. Most financial institutions prefer turnkey EPC contracts." Under multi-lot contracting (civil, E&M, grid, penstock) the developer manages the interfaces, usually through an owner's engineer [S1].
- **Payment terms (IFC):**
  - Lump-sum EPC: 10-30% down payment and 10-20% retention, released on completion and acceptance.
  - E&M supply: 30% advance, 50% on delivery of equipment, 20% on acceptance.
  Source: [S1]
- **Contingencies.** Across Fichtner's sample, contingencies were min 5.4%, median 9.8%, average 9.1%, max 12.6% of total plant cost. Industry practice is 15% for civil works, 7.5-10% for E&M and 7.5-10% for grid connection. Contingencies are higher in earlier phases, and risks are "even higher if a tunnel must be built" [S1].
- **CAPEX and O&M ranges (IFC):** small HPP US$1,300-8,000/kW; large greenfield US$1,000-3,500/kW. Annual O&M is 1-4% of investment (IEA assumes 2.2-3% for small HPP), about US$52/kW/yr for small HPP [S1].
- **Geological risk and FIDIC Emerald Book (2019).**
  - Launched 7 May 2019 at the World Tunnel Congress (Naples) with ITA-AITES. It takes the 2017 Yellow Book as its starting point [S47].
  - The Geotechnical Baseline Report (GBR) is "the sole source or contractual document that describes the anticipated subsurface conditions".
  - The Employer bears unforeseeable ground-condition risk beyond the baseline and benefits if ground is better. Time for Completion is adjusted for ground conditions (sub-clause 13.8.3 uses contractor production rates). Remuneration is flexible according to ground conditions [S47].
- **Turnkey EPC in practice:** Achwa 2 is described as a "turnkey" run-of-river plant (snippet, Wikipedia) [S48].
- Share of SSA small-hydro deals using EPC vs multi-contract: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.

## 8. Lender requirements at financial close and debt terms

### 8.1 Requirements

- **Hydrology:** "data for at least 15 years are required". Shorter records can shift energy estimates by 10% or more [S1].
- **DSCR:** must exceed 1 "under the worst-case hydrology scenario such as dry years". The typical SHPP range is 1.2-1.5. A Debt Service Reserve covers shortfalls [S1].
- **Independent advisers:** lenders conduct "intensive technical, environmental and social, financial and legal due diligence ... using a team of independent advisors". An independent engineer reports to lenders during construction and commissioning [S1].
- **E&S standards:**
  - Equator Principles (EP4, effective 1 October 2020) apply to project finance with total capital cost ≥US$10m. Projects in non-designated countries (all SSA) must comply with IFC PS and the World Bank EHS Guidelines [S49][S50].
  - GET FiT required IFC PS (2012) compliance [S9].
  - FMO classified Kikagati as E&S Category A [S51]. Siti and Nyamwamba were B+ [S52][S40b].
- **Conditions precedent checklist:** a public SSA-hydro CP list was not found (PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND). The IFC guide defines FC as the point when "all required conditions have been met and all project and financing agreements have been signed" [S1].
