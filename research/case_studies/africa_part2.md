# Bankable Hydro — African Case-Study Library, Part 2

**Research date:** 2026-10-03
**Scope:** Nachtigal (Cameroon); Lesotho Highlands Water Project (Muela, Phase II); Rusumo Falls; Ruzizi I/II/III; Mpatamanga (Malawi); Julius Nyerere/Rufiji (Tanzania); Isimba, Karuma, Kiira/Nalubaale, Nyagak, Bujagali (Uganda); Batoka Gorge (Zambia/Zimbabwe).

## Methodology note

- Sources were found with web search and, where possible, retrieved directly (WebFetch or PDF download and text extraction). The order of preference was: World Bank Group documents (Chair Summary, Project Completion Note, Restructuring Paper, ESRS, MIGA/IFC disclosure pages), AfDB, EIB and NDB releases, government and utility bodies (LHDA, TCTA, UEGCL), then trade and business press (Engineering News/Reuters, Water Power Magazine, Enerdata) and national press (Daily Monitor, The Citizen, The Independent, Eagle Online).
- Each figure is followed by `[S#]`, which points to the numbered source list at the end. Some sources are marked **(search-result only)**. For these, the URL and claim appeared in search results but the page could not be fetched because of a 403 or 503 error. Treat them as lower confidence and check them before quoting externally.
- Wikipedia was used only to find leads. It is never cited as the source of a number. Where only Wikipedia gave a figure, the field says so and is marked unverified.
- Some sources aggregate data from others, such as the Early Warning System (EWS, rightsindevelopment.org) mirror of World Bank data. These are labelled as mirrors.
- `PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND` means the figure was not located within this pass, which allowed about 2–5 searches per project. It does not mean the data does not exist. World Bank PADs, ICRs and AfDB appraisal reports would fill many of these gaps.
- **Analyst inference** labels interpretation, including all of field AD.
- Currency and year are given as stated in each source. No currency conversion or inflation adjustment was done.

## 1. Nachtigal Hydropower (Cameroon) — 420 MW IPP

- **A Project size:** Large run-of-river IPP on the Sanaga River, about 65 km NE of Yaoundé [S2][S4]. Expected to add about 30% to Cameroon's installed capacity [S2].
- **B Installed capacity:** 420 MW [S2][S3]. The units are 7 × 60 MW GE turbines (search-result only) [S7].
- **C Generation:** "Nearly 3 TWh" per year [S4].
- **D Hydrology notes:** Sanaga River. Cameroon's 2015 installed capacity was 1,286 MW, about 60% of it hydro [S2]. Upstream regulation by Lom Pangar is widely cited, but no primary source was retrieved in this pass. PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (primary).
- **E CAPEX:** EUR 1.2 billion [S1][S4]. The World Bank PAD gives EUR 1,184 million (about US$1,383 million) including EUR 114 million of funded contingencies [S63]. Business in Cameroon reports a total investment of CFA 786 billion [S5].
- **F Construction period:** Construction began at end-2018 with financial close (signing on 8 Nov 2018) [S4][S6]. The plan was 5 years [S1], with COD originally 2023 [S4] and later a planned COD of September 2024 [S5]. Full activation was postponed to December 2024 (search-result headline) [S8]. Cameroon Tribune (27 Mar 2025) reports the plant reached full capacity on 27 Feb 2025 with the coupling of the seventh and last unit [S64]. The PAD planned a 57-month construction period [S63].
- **G Cost overruns:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. In August 2023 the regulator ARSEL found construction defects: spillway cracks, water infiltration and early concrete degradation [S5].
- **H Financing structure:** About 25% equity and 75% debt [S4]; the PAD gives 76:24 debt:equity, with the whole debt package under IFC's Global Coordinator mandate [S63]. The debt came from 11 DFIs plus local banks, coordinated by IFC [S6].
- **I Debt:** Lenders were AfDB, AFC, AFD, CDC, DEG, EAIF, EIB, FMO, IFC, OFID and Proparco, plus the local banks Attijariwafa SCB Cameroon, BICEC, Société Générale Cameroun and Standard Chartered Cameroon (search-result list) [S6]. The IFC A loan was up to EUR 130 million [S3]. The local-currency tranche was EUR 171 million, about one-fifth of total debt, with a 21-year tenor [S1].
- **J Equity:** At close: EDF 40%, IFC 30%, Republic of Cameroon 30% [S4]. Later: EDF 40%, IFC 20%, Cameroon 15%, Africa50 15%, STOA 10% (search-result only) [S9]. IFC equity was up to EUR 60 million [S3].
- **K Concessional finance:** Of the DFI debt, the amount on concessional terms is PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. IBRD guarantees are non-concessional; see M.
- **L Government contribution:** Equity stake of 30%, later reported as 15% [S4][S9]. The state also backs the IBRD guarantees through a single Indemnity Agreement covering both guarantees [S63] (the Chair Summary [S3] does not mention the indemnity).
- **M Guarantees:** IBRD payment guarantee up to EUR 86 million (US$100 million). IBRD loan guarantee up to EUR 171 million (US$200 million). MIGA guarantees up to EUR 224.8 million (US$262.5 million) were approved [S3]. MIGA issued EUR 164.5 million of breach-of-contract cover for 15 years to EDF International and STOA [S2].
- **N PPA structure:** Long-term PPA under a 35-year BOOT concession [S2][S4]. Capacity-style payments of CFA 10 billion per month from COD are "payable by Eneo … or Treasury" regardless of consumption [S5].
- **O Offtaker:** ENEO (Energy of Cameroon), the privately owned distribution utility [S2].
- **P Utility financial condition:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (primary). The World Bank Board noted the operation's role in "improving Cameroon's power sector's financial sustainability" and flagged governance risks [S3].
- **Q Transmission requirements:** A 50 km transmission line [S4]. Who was responsible for it is PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **R Grid integration:** Injects into the Southern Interconnected Grid (RIS) [S5].
- **S Demand assumptions:** Peak demand projected to quadruple by 2035. Cameroon's 2015 generation was 6,426 GWh [S2].
- **T Tariff:** EUR 0.061/kWh [S2]. The World Bank blog prints "€0.6/kWh" [S1], which is almost certainly a typo for EUR 0.06. Retail tariff is more than EUR 0.12/kWh [S1].
- **U Regulation:** ARSEL, the electricity regulator, inspected the works and requested remediation schedules [S5].
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** BOOT, with a 35-year concession between NHPC and the Republic of Cameroon [S2].
- **W FX risk:** Mostly EUR-denominated debt with a FCFA-pegged economy, so EUR/XAF peg risk is low. The CFA local-currency tranche of EUR 171 million reduced the mismatch [S1]. IFC client risk-management swaps were up to US$10 million exposure [S3].
- **X Political risk:** Mitigated by MIGA breach-of-contract cover [S2][S3].
- **Y E&S risk:** The World Bank Board highlighted the prevalence of gender-based violence and asked for E&S lessons to be documented [S3]. Construction defects were also found [S5].
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** CFA 10 billion per month falls to ENEO, or to the Treasury if ENEO cannot pay [S5]. The state also carries its IBRD guarantee indemnity obligations [S3].
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** Up to EUR 86 million payment guarantee and EUR 171 million loan guarantee, both counter-guaranteed by Cameroon through an Indemnity Agreement [S63]. MIGA breach-of-contract cover is a contingent claim on the state's performance [S2].
- **AB Delays:** About 1.5–2 years against the 2023 COD [S4][S5][S8].
- **AC Restructuring:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (shareholding changes only) [S9].
- **AD Lessons for financial close (Analyst inference):** This was the "full WBG stack": IBRD payment and loan guarantees, MIGA cover, and IFC equity and debt. Together they let 11+ DFIs and local banks lend at a EUR 0.061 tariff. A 21-year local-currency tranche is replicable. However, take-or-pay obligations backstopped by the Treasury turn a private IPP into a sovereign contingent liability, so the guarantee and indemnity totals should be modelled as fiscal exposure.

## 2. Lesotho Highlands Water Project (Muela hydro, Phase II incl. Oxbow)

- **A Project size:** A binational water-transfer and hydropower programme under a 1986 Treaty, with a further 2011 Agreement for Phase II [S10]. Phase I was completed in 2003/2004 [S11][S10]. Phase II includes the Polihali Dam (165–166 m), a 38–38.5 km transfer tunnel and the Oxbow hydropower scheme [S11][S12].
- **B Installed capacity:** Muela is 72 MW [S10][S11]. Oxbow, in Phase II, is 80 MW [S10][S12]. A planned 1,200 MW Kobong pumped storage scheme [S11] was deferred because of "unfavourable economic conditions" [S10].
- **C Generation:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (primary). Oxbow will run as a peaking plant for about 7 hours a day [S10].
- **D Hydrology notes:** Water transfer rises from about 780 million to 1,260–1,270 million m³/yr after Phase II [S12][S13]. Oxbow tailwater is routed back into 'Muela reservoir so the water remains in the transfer system [S10].
- **E CAPEX:** Phase I cost R16.4 billion [S11]. Phase II was estimated at R32.6 billion [S11]. The current projection is R53 billion, re-baselined in May 2023 and up from R8 billion in 2008 [S12].
- **F Construction period:** Phase II launched on 13 Nov 2020 [S11]. Polihali impoundment is planned for the 2027/28 rainy season, water-transfer completion for 2029, and hydropower "shortly after 2031" [S12].
- **G Cost overruns:** Phase II rose from R8 billion (2008) to R53 billion (2026 projection) [S12]. This partly reflects scope and escalation, and is not a like-for-like overrun (Analyst inference).
- **H Financing structure:** South Africa (TCTA) funds the water-transfer costs off-budget through commercial and DFI borrowing, repaid from IVRS water tariffs [S11]. Lesotho is responsible for the hydropower component, for which LHDA references IPP/PPP models [S10].
- **I Debt:** TCTA signed R15.4 billion of facilities in May 2021 with ABSA, Investec, Nedbank, RMB, Standard Bank and DBSA [S11]. AfDB lent US$86.72 million, about ZAR 1.3 billion, and NDB lent ZAR 3.2 billion, pari passu for Polihali Dam and tunnel (search-result only) [S13][S14].
- **J Equity:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. Oxbow equity structure is PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **K Concessional finance:** The AfDB and NDB loans to TCTA are sovereign-guaranteed [S14]. Their concessionality is PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **L Government contribution:** The Government of the Republic of South Africa (GoRSA) bears the full water-transfer cost [S11]. Lesotho's contribution to hydropower is PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **M Guarantees:** GoRSA guarantees the AfDB loan to TCTA (search-result only) [S14].
- **N PPA structure:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (Muela/Oxbow).
- **O Offtaker:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (primary). Muela supplies Lesotho's national grid [S10].
- **P Utility financial condition:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Q Transmission requirements:** Oxbow needs a 132 kV line to the 'Muela substation [S10].
- **R Grid integration:** Oxbow will reduce Lesotho's dependence on electricity imports [S10].
- **S Demand assumptions:** The pumped storage scheme was deferred after a market survey and transmission study [S10].
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (power). Water royalties are paid to Lesotho, but the royalty amounts were not verified from a primary source.
- **U Regulation:** Bi-national governance under the Treaty. LHDA (Lesotho) and TCTA (South Africa) are the implementing authorities [S10][S11].
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** Public. Hydropower is considered under IPP/PPP models [S10].
- **W FX risk:** Rand-denominated domestic debt [S11], so the water-transfer component carries little FX mismatch (Analyst inference).
- **X Political risk:** A treaty-based framework between two states [S10]. Phase I had major corruption: Lesotho courts convicted Lahmeyer International and Acres International of bribing the LHDA chief executive (search-result only) [S15].
- **Y E&S risk:** Resettlement and livelihoods for the Polihali reservoir. The AfDB has published an ESIA summary, but details were not retrieved. PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (quantified).
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** South Africa's exposure goes through TCTA off-budget debt plus the GoRSA guarantee [S11][S14].
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** GoRSA guarantee on AfDB/NDB debt [S14]. Contingent liabilities for Oxbow are PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AB Delays:** Phase II was agreed in 2011 and launched in 2020, and hydropower is now expected after 2031 [S10][S11][S12].
- **AC Restructuring:** Cost re-baselined in May 2023 [S12]. Hydropower was rescoped from 1,200 MW pumped storage to 80 MW conventional [S10][S11].
- **AD Lessons (Analyst inference):** A ring-fenced revenue stream (raw-water tariffs) lets a creditworthy state agency raise rand debt off-budget. Hydropower riding on a water-transfer project gets the dam "for free", but it can be deprioritised and rescoped when power economics are weak. Procurement integrity also matters: the bribery convictions became a precedent in international anti-corruption enforcement.

## 3. Rusumo Falls (Rwanda / Tanzania / Burundi) — 80 MW regional public project

- **A Project size:** Run-of-river plant on the Kagera River on the Tanzania–Rwanda border [S16].
- **B Installed capacity:** 80 MW [S16]. Each country is allocated about 27 MW (26.67 MW) [S18].
- **C Generation:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (design). From Nov 2023 to Jan 2024 the plant delivered 66 million kWh in total (Tanzania 21, Rwanda 21, Burundi 22) [S18].
- **D Hydrology notes:** Organic material and sediment build up in the cooling-water system, causing abnormal shaft-seal wear and outages [S16].
- **E CAPEX:** Project cost US$468.90 million (EWS mirror of World Bank data) [S17]. World Bank financing totals US$347.50 million [S16]. The World Bank-financed component contracts were: CP1 civil works US$155 million, CP2 electromechanical US$126 million, E&S US$22 million, Owner's Engineer US$37 million [S16].
- **F Construction period:** World Bank approval 6 Aug 2013, effectiveness 14 Jul 2014. Supply to the grids began in Nov 2023 and COD was declared in mid-Feb 2025 [S16].
- **G Cost overruns:** The CP1 civil contract rose 47%, from US$75.1 million to US$110.4 million. LRP/LADP costs rose from US$18 million to about US$38 million. Owner's Engineer costs rose from US$24.36 million to about US$36.7 million. The financing category was overspent (US$188 million allocation vs US$213.7 million spent) [S16].
- **H Financing structure:** Generation is financed by IDA grants and credits to the three states and on-lent or assigned to RPCL. Transmission is parallel-financed by AfDB and the EU [S16].
- **I Debt:** IDA: Tanzania credit 5296; Rwanda grant H873 plus credit 5295; Burundi grant [S16]. IDA disbursed US$295.39 million (92%) by 28 Feb 2025 [S16]. AfDB committed US$113 million, of which US$97.3 million came from ADF and NTF plus a US$16 million EU-AITF grant for transmission (search-result only) [S19].
- **J Equity:** RPCL is owned equally by the three countries [S16].
- **K Concessional finance:** Entirely IDA and ADF concessional, plus grants [S16][S17][S19]. The EWS mirror lists US$116 million of grants [S17].
- **L Government contribution:** Government contributions are referenced but not quantified [S16]. PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (amount).
- **M Guarantees:** None identified. PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **N PPA structure:** PPAs between RPCL and the three utilities, with capacity charges after COD. Before COD, RPCL was paid a "significantly lower" energy charge [S16]. RPCL must meet a guaranteed availability [S16]. PPA signing dates: REGIDESO 21 Jun 2023, TANESCO 30 Sep 2023, REG-EUCL 1 Dec 2023 [S18].
- **O Offtaker:** TANESCO, REG/EUCL and REGIDESO [S18].
- **P Utility financial condition:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (primary).
- **Q Transmission requirements:** 220 kV lines: 118 km in Rwanda, 94 km in Tanzania and 160 km for Burundi, of which 56 km run through Tanzania [S16]. The Citizen gives the Burundi line as 16 km against a 372 km total, which looks like a typo; the World Bank figure is preferred [S18].
- **R Grid integration:** Expected to improve voltage stability in NW Tanzania [S18].
- **S Demand assumptions:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** Implemented by NELSAP-RPIU (Nile Basin Initiative) for RPCL [S16].
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** Public, tri-national SPV. Not a PPP [S16].
- **W FX risk:** USD/SDR-denominated IDA debt carried by the sovereigns [S16].
- **X Political risk:** Coordination across three states, and Rwanda's relocation decision came late (2023) [S16].
- **Y E&S risk:** Blasting in 2018 damaged houses, and 580 structures in Tanzania needed repair. In Rwanda, 80 households had to be relocated [S16]. The safeguards rating was later raised to Moderately Satisfactory [S16].
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** All three sovereigns carry IDA debt service, with overruns covered by reallocation [S16].
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. The long-term cooling-system redesign is financed by RPCL [S16].
- **AB Delays:** The original World Bank closing date of 31 Dec 2020 was extended to 30 Jun 2025 through three restructurings [S16]. COD came in Feb 2025, against an early-2020s target [S16].
- **AC Restructuring:** Three closing-date extensions, plus a 2025 reallocation of about US$59.8 million in savings to the overspent category [S16].
- **AD Lessons (Analyst inference):** Regional public hydro on IDA grants avoids financial-close risk but not execution risk. Variation orders made up 58% of the CP1 increase [S16], so geotechnical and design maturity before tender matters. Specifying cooling water for a sediment- and organics-laden river should be a design check. COD-linked capacity charges create a strong incentive to reach availability targets.

## 4. Ruzizi I / II / III (DRC / Rwanda / Burundi) — Ruzizi III regional PPP

- **A Project size:** Ruzizi III is a regional IPP/PPP with a 51 m dam, 3.8 km headrace and compact reservoir (about 46 ha) [S20].
- **B Installed capacity:** Ruzizi I is 29.2 MW [S20] (29.8 MW in another source, search-result only [S25]). Ruzizi II is 43.8 MW [S20]. Ruzizi III is 206 MW [S20][S22]. An EIB 2020 release said 147 MW [S21], so earlier configurations differed.
- **C Generation:** Ruzizi III: 1,140 GWh/yr [S20].
- **D Hydrology notes:** Lake Kivu outflow to Lake Tanganyika. Ruzizi I, 3 km downstream of the lake outlet, controls Lake Kivu's discharge [S20].
- **E CAPEX:** Ruzizi III is US$760 million [S20][S22]. The EIB's 2020 release gave EUR 728 million for the earlier 147 MW configuration [S21].
- **F Construction period:** Target operation was 2030 before the conflict pause [S22]. Ruzizi I and II were commissioned in 1958 and 1989 [S20].
- **G Cost overruns:** Not applicable (pre-construction).
- **H Financing structure:** IFIs (World Bank, EU, EIB, AfDB, KfW, AFD) were expected to fund about 60% (search-result only) [S23]. EIB is lead arranger [S22]. IDA support for Ruzizi 3 was listed as a US$200 million credit and US$100 million grant (EWS mirror; a search snippet gave US$168 million and US$92 million, so verify) [S24].
- **I Debt:** AfDB authorised a US$138 million loan in Jan 2016 (search-result only) [S23]. Other amounts: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **J Equity:** Sponsors are IPS (AKFED) and SN Power, now TotalEnergies [S21][S22]. Shareholding split: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **K Concessional finance:** EU-AITF grant of EUR 9.1 million signed 20 Feb 2020 [S21]. IDA credit and grant as in H [S24].
- **L Government contribution:** The three governments are borrowers of the IDA funds, through Ruzizi III Energy Limited (REL), an SPV registered in Rwanda [S20][S24].
- **M Guarantees:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **N PPA structure:** PPAs signed in Jul 2019 [S21]. Terms: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **O Offtaker:** National utilities of the three countries. Named counterparties: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (primary).
- **P Utility financial condition:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Q Transmission requirements:** A 7 km 220 kV double-circuit line to Kamanyola substation (DRC). The associated Kamanyola–Buhandahanda 220 kV line is developed by EGL with KfW support [S20].
- **R Grid integration:** Would double Burundi's capacity and raise Rwanda's by about one-third [S21][S22].
- **S Demand assumptions:** Electricity access in the region was about 6% [S21].
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** Regional body Energie des Grands Lacs (EGL) is involved [S20].
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** Described as "Africa's first regional PPP hydropower project" [S21]. A 25-year concession (search-result only) [S23].
- **W FX risk:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **X Political risk:** Extreme. The site lies within the M23 conflict area. EIB adopted a "wait and see" approach in Mar 2025, and Germany suspended new commitments to Rwanda [S22].
- **Y E&S risk:** World Bank Category A, including environmental flow and biodiversity offset requirements [S20][S24].
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** Three sovereigns borrowing IDA funds for on-lending or equity [S24].
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AB Delays:** EIB has been involved since 2009. Financial close was expected mid-2021 [S21], then later in 2025 [S22], and remains pending. A World Bank Board vote was listed for 24 Sep 2026 [S24]; the outcome was not verified.
- **AC Restructuring:** Capacity was rescoped from 147 MW to 206 MW [S20][S21].
- **AD Lessons (Analyst inference):** Multi-sovereign PPPs need offtake and payment-security arrangements that work across three utilities of differing credit quality. Conflict and political risk, rather than structuring, can block close for over a decade. Rehabilitating the existing Ruzizi I and II assets is a lower-risk parallel track.

## 5. Mpatamanga Hydropower Storage (Malawi) — about 358.5 MW PPP

- **A Project size:** A main peaking dam plus a regulating dam on the Shire River (part of the Zambezi system) [S26]. Land requirement is 5,863 ha [S26].
- **B Installed capacity:** 358.5 MW: a 301 MW peaking plant plus a 57.5 MW baseload plant [S26][S28]. Earlier reports said 350 MW [S29].
- **C Generation:** 1,544 GWh/yr (EWS mirror) [S27].
- **D Hydrology notes:** International waterway (Zambezi). Location is between the existing Tedzani and Kapichira plants [S28][S29].
- **E CAPEX:** More than US$1.5 billion [S28]. US$1,636 million (EWS mirror) [S27]. An earlier estimate was about US$1 billion (search-result only) [S29].
- **F Construction period:** A 35-year PPA covers 5 years of construction and 30 of operation [S27]. Construction start date: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (primary).
- **G Cost overruns:** Not applicable, though the estimate grew from about US$1 billion to US$1.5–1.64 billion [S27][S28][S29].
- **H Financing structure:** Debt up to US$900 million. This comprises an IFC loan of US$150 million, an IDA Private Sector Window loan of US$150 million, and parallel loans up to US$600 million [S26]. There is also a US$350 million IDA grant to the government [S26][S28].
- **I Debt:** As in H [S26].
- **J Equity:** Expected after financial close: EDF 27.5%, SN Power 27.5%, EGENCO 30%, IFC 15% [S26].
- **K Concessional finance:** US$350 million IDA grant, approved 23 May 2025 [S28]. US$150 million IDA PSW [S26].
- **L Government contribution:** IDA grant passed through the Government of Malawi, plus EGENCO's 30% equity [S26][S28].
- **M Guarantees:** IDA Partial Risk Guarantee of US$100 million [S26][S27]. MIGA political risk insurance of US$180 million [S26].
- **N PPA structure:** 35-year PPA under a BOOT structure [S27].
- **O Offtaker:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (primary). ESCOM is reported in secondary sources.
- **P Utility financial condition:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Q Transmission requirements:** 64 km of 400 kV and 11 km of 132 kV lines [S26].
- **R Grid integration:** Expected to roughly double Malawi's current generation [S26]. A stated objective is regional trade [S27].
- **S Demand assumptions:** More than 1 million new households to be served [S28].
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** BOOT PPP [S27].
- **W FX risk:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **X Political risk:** Covered by MIGA PRI and the IDA PRG [S26].
- **Y E&S risk:** IFC Category A. 160–200 households physically displaced and 1,600–2,200 economically displaced [S26].
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** The IDA PRG requires a sovereign indemnity. The government holds equity through EGENCO [S26].
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** IDA PRG of US$100 million [S26].
- **AB Delays:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AC Restructuring:** Sponsor changes over time (Scatec, SN Power, TotalEnergies) [S26][S28].
- **AD Lessons (Analyst inference):** This is the "Nachtigal template" for an IDA-only country. A large IDA grant buys down CAPEX and so the tariff, while the IDA PRG and MIGA wrap the private debt. Watch the fiscal cost of the indemnity and the PPA's capacity charges for a single-buyer market.

## 6. Julius Nyerere Hydropower (Rufiji, Tanzania) — 2,115 MW state-owned

- **A Project size:** A 131 m main dam, four auxiliary dams and a reservoir of about 34 billion m³ [S31].
- **B Installed capacity:** 2,115 MW, from 9 × 235 MW units [S30][S31].
- **C Generation:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (primary). Wikipedia gives 5,920 GWh/yr (unverified). The plant is expected to provide about 60% of Tanzania's generation [S30].
- **D Hydrology notes:** Rufiji River (Stiegler's Gorge). Large storage gives flood regulation [S31].
- **E CAPEX:** TZS 6.5 trillion (search-result only) [S32] to TZS 7.45 trillion (US$2.8 billion) [S30]. Water Power Magazine gives US$2.9 billion [S31].
- **F Construction period:** EPC awarded Dec 2018 with a contractual 42 months [S31]. Construction June 2019 to March 2025 [S30]. Commissioned 22 Aug 2026 [S30][S31].
- **G Cost overruns:** Cost estimates rose from TZS 6.5 trillion to TZS 7.45 trillion [S30][S32]. The original contract value was not verified.
- **H Financing structure:** 100% government budget (domestic revenue), with no project finance (search-result only) [S32]. The Citizen (5 Apr 2025) reports that 99.5 percent of the Sh6.5 trillion had been paid by the government and came from domestic revenue [S65].
- **I Debt:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (no project debt identified).
- **J Equity:** TANESCO and the Government of Tanzania. Owner: TANESCO (Wikipedia lead, unverified).
- **K Concessional finance:** None identified. PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **L Government contribution:** About 100%, from the budget [S32].
- **M Guarantees:** Not applicable.
- **N PPA structure:** Not applicable (utility-owned).
- **O Offtaker:** TANESCO (integrated).
- **P Utility financial condition:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Q Transmission requirements:** 400 kV connection to the national grid [S31].
- **R Grid integration:** Installed capacity reaches 4.6 GW against a 2.27 GW peak, a surplus of 2.38 GW; exports to Kenya and Zambia are being discussed [S30].
- **S Demand assumptions:** Export-dependent absorption of the surplus [S30] (Analyst inference: demand risk shifts to TANESCO and the regional market).
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** EWURA. PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (project-specific).
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** Public EPC, by the Egyptian JV of Arab Contractors and Elsewedy Electric [S31].
- **W FX risk:** Borne by the budget; the EPC currency was not found.
- **X Political risk:** Low for lenders (no lenders). A government-to-government contracting model with Egypt [S31].

## 11. Bujagali (Uganda) — 250 MW IPP with IDA PRG ★ fiscal / contingent-liability case

- **A Project size:** Run-of-river on the Victoria Nile, about 8 km downstream of Kiira [S48].
- **B Installed capacity:** 250 MW [S49][S50]. The 2001 version was 200–250 MW [S48].
- **C Generation:** About 45% of Uganda's annual generation (2018) [S50]. Eagle Online says more than 25% [S51]. GWh: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **D Hydrology notes:** Downstream of the Lake Victoria-controlled Kiira/Nalubaale [S48]. The 2004–06 drought and over-release years caused load-shedding, which raised the value of new capacity [S48][S44].
- **E CAPEX:** In 2001 (AES Nile Power) the estimate was US$582 million [S48]. In 2007 it was US$799 million (MIGA) [S49]. Sithe Global stated US$862 million "to date" in 2012 (press) [S52].
- **F Construction period:** About 2007–2012. The PRG indemnity agreement was dated 18 Jul 2007 [S53] and the plant was commissioned in 2012 [S50].
- **G Cost overruns:** From US$582 million (2001 design) to US$799 million (2007) to US$862 million (2012) [S48][S49][S52]. The changes reflect re-procurement and escalation (Analyst inference).
- **H Financing structure:** In 2007 the World Bank Group package was US$360 million: a US$130 million IFC loan, US$115 million IDA PRG and US$115 million MIGA guarantee (search-result only) [S54][S49]. Other lenders were EIB, AfDB, Standard Chartered, FMO, Proparco and DEG/KfW (press) [S52].
- **I Debt:** Senior plus subordinated debt with a 20-year term and 5-year grace (press) [S52]. US$402 million outstanding was refinanced in July 2018 [S51] ("more than US$400 million" [S50]) by IFC, AfDB, FMO, Proparco, DEG, CDC, Absa and Nedbank [S50]. Maturity was extended from 2023 (senior) and 2027 (sub) to 2032 [S51].
- **J Equity:** Sponsors IPS (AKFED) and Sithe Global, selected Apr 2005 [S48]. The original return on equity was 19%, with 15% proposed in the refinancing [S51]. The government's US$20 million equity was reported in press [S52].
- **K Concessional finance:** AfDB, EIB and the bilateral DFIs. The concessional share is PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **L Government contribution:** Implementation Agreement of 13 Dec 2005 [S53]. A tax waiver: the 30% corporation tax was waived in exchange for the loan re-profiling [S51].
- **M Guarantees:** IDA PRG up to US$115 million for commercial lenders, with a Uganda–IDA Indemnity Agreement of 18 Jul 2007 [S53]. MIGA US$115 million breach-of-contract cover for up to 20 years to World Power Holdings (Sithe) [S49]. A Liquidity Facility Agreement also exists [S51].
- **N PPA structure:** Take-or-pay with monthly capacity payments to BEL for availability (≥96% of 250 MW) (search-result only) [S55]. The PPA, IA and LFA were amended in 2018 [S51].
- **O Offtaker:** UETCL [S49][S51].
- **P Utility financial condition:** In 2020, 9% of UETCL purchases were "deemed energy" (Shs 104.4 billion), paid for but not evacuated (search-result only) [S55]. In 2005 the World Bank said a "financially viable distribution business" was key to investor risk [S48].
- **Q Transmission requirements:** About 100 km of transmission line was in the 2001 scope [S48].
- **R Grid integration:** Displaced more than 100 MW of diesel. Marginal cost fell by more than 65%, against tariff subsidies of about US$180 million/yr (IEA, search-result only) [S56].
- **S Demand assumptions:** Least-cost expansion. Load-shedding before 2012 justified the plant [S48].
- **T Tariff:** About US$0.11/kWh (2018 press) [S51]. IEA: US$0.113/kWh in 2017, due to rise to US$0.133 in 2018 and US$0.147 by 2023 before refinancing (search-result only) [S56]. Press reported a 2012 debate over 10–12 US cents vs about 2 cents for Kiira/Nalubaale [S52].
- **U Regulation:** ERA. Parliament imposed conditions on the tax waiver (search-result headline) [S61].
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** BOOT IPP under the Implementation Agreement [S53].
- **W FX risk:** USD capacity charges paid by a UGX-revenue offtaker. The FX risk sits with UETCL and the state (Analyst inference).
- **X Political risk:** First attempt (AES) collapsed. The ECAs withdrew in Jan 2002, one month after Board approval, leaving a gap of about US$234 million. Corruption probes involved an EPC contractor, and AES withdrew in Aug 2003 [S48].
- **Y E&S risk:** World Bank Inspection Panel report (May 2002) [S48]. CAO complaints (search-result only) [S62].
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** The state pays through UETCL capacity charges, tax waivers, deemed energy and tariff subsidies [S51][S55][S56]. The 2018 refinancing saved UETCL about US$40 million/yr in capacity payments (search-result only) [S55].
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** IDA PRG indemnity of US$115 million [S53]. MIGA breach-of-contract cover of US$115 million [S49]. Termination and buy-out obligations under the IA and the liquidity facility: amounts PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AB Delays:** About 6 years between the cancelled 2001 deal and the 2007 close [S48][S53].
- **AC Restructuring:** The 2018 refinancing extended tenor about 10 years to 2032, in exchange for a tax waiver and lower ROE [S50][S51].
- **AD Lessons (Analyst inference):**
  1. A PRG mobilises commercial debt, but only by moving payment and termination risk onto the sovereign through the indemnity. It should be counted as a contingent liability.
  2. Short debt tenors front-load capacity charges, producing high early tariffs and later political pressure. Tenor matched to asset life is the cheapest tariff lever.
  3. Take-or-pay with a weak single buyer causes deemed-energy leakage when the grid lags generation.
  4. Financing plans need to be robust to ECA withdrawal (lesson stated by the World Bank [S48]).
  5. Refinancing an operating hydro asset can be engineered for tariff relief, but the state pays for it with tax expenditure.
