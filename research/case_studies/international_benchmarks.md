# International Hydropower Benchmark Cases: Lessons for African Structuring

**Product:** Bankable Hydro (book, Excel bankability model, manual, course)
**Research date:** 2026-10-03
**Analyst:** desk research (web), compiled for internal product development

## Methodology

1. Seven non-African benchmark projects were chosen to cover the main structuring archetypes: state-led megaproject (Three Gorges), binational treaty entity (Itaipu), state-bank-led concession (Belo Monte), MDB-guaranteed export IPP (Nam Theun 2), commercial-bank export IPP (Xayaburi), DFI-led domestic IPP (Upper Trishuli-1), and utility-sponsored trust/lease financing (Reventazón).
2. Data come only from web pages and PDFs I actually opened, or from search-result snippets where the page could not be opened. Snippet-only facts are marked **(snippet)**. Each fact carries a [S#] reference to the source list at the end of the file, which gives the exact URL.
3. Fields follow the A–AD template only where data exists. If the reviewed sources gave no figure, the field says **PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND**. Nothing is estimated or extrapolated.
4. When sources disagree, all values are shown with their sources. They are not reconciled.
5. Sections headed "Lessons for African structuring (Analyst inference)" are my judgement, not sourced fact.
6. Currency is nominal and as reported. No inflation or FX conversions were made.
7. Not covered for lack of time or verifiable sources: Theun-Hinboun, Tala, Punatsangchhu, Teesta III and Chaglla. Each is a candidate for a later pass.

## 1. Three Gorges (China): state-financed megaproject

| Field | Data |
|---|---|
| A. Capacity | 22,500 MW (32×700 MW + 2×50 MW) [S1] |
| B. Generation | ~100 TWh/yr when complete (snippet; Wikipedia mirror) [S1] |
| C. CAPEX | 1992 approved budget: 57 bn CNY (US$8.35 bn). 1994 revision: 90 bn CNY, excluding grid. By end-2008: ~180 bn CNY (>US$26 bn). "Dynamic investment" up to 205 bn CNY including interest [S2]. Government estimate cited as 180 bn CNY [S1] |
| D. Financing | Three Gorges Construction Fund, Gezhouba Dam profits, China Development Bank loans, domestic and foreign commercial bank loans, corporate bonds, and pre- and post-completion revenue. Provinces paid a surcharge of ¥7/MWh (beneficiary provinces) or ¥4/MWh (other provinces) [S1] |
| E. Guarantees | Implicit sovereign; no project-finance ring-fencing identified |
| F. Offtaker | PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (provincial grids, specifics not verified) |
| G. Overrun/delay | Nominal cost grew more than 3× from the 1992 approval [S2]. Officials reportedly said a further 100 bn CNY was needed to address dam-induced problems [S2] (critical source: Probe International) |
| H. Fiscal exposure | Funded largely through a national electricity surcharge, so cost fell on consumers nationwide (inferred from [S1]) |

**Lessons for African structuring (Analyst inference)**
- A national levy or construction fund can finance a megaproject without a PPA. The cost is socialised on all consumers, and that only works where the tariff base is large and collection is strong, which is rare in Sub-Saharan Africa.
- The headline budget depends on its scope: including or excluding transmission, resettlement and interest moved it by 57 → 205 bn CNY. The Bankable Hydro model should therefore report "all-in" cost including IDC, transmission and E&S, and should not treat EPC price as CAPEX.

## 2. Itaipu (Brazil–Paraguay): binational treaty entity, 2023 debt payoff, Annex C

| Field | Data |
|---|---|
| A. Capacity | 14,000 MW, 20 units × 700 MW (snippet) [S5] |
| B. Generation | Record 103,098,366 MWh in 2016. Design basis 75 TWh/yr (snippet) [S5]. Cumulative 2.9 bn MWh; supplies >13% of Brazil's and ~90% of Paraguay's electricity [S3] |
| C. CAPEX | Construction cost: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND in reviewed sources |
| D. Financing | >300 financing contracts with ~70 creditors (Eletrobras, BNDES, Finame, Banco do Brasil, Citibank, Dresdner, Deutsche Bank, Swiss Bank Corp., JPMorgan) [S3]. Total paid US$63.5 bn = US$35.6 bn principal + US$27.9 bn financial charges [S3] |
| E. Guarantees | Sovereign treaty structure (details not verified) |
| F. Offtake / tariff | Each country owns 50% of output and sells unused energy to the other at preferential prices under Annex C (snippet) [S6]. Revenue began in March 1985 [S3] |
| G. Debt payoff | Final instalments of US$115 m paid to Eletrobras and BNDES on 28 March 2023 [S3] |
| H. Annex C renegotiation | Annex C, the treaty's financial basis, became reviewable once the debt was retired. Talks were expected from August 2023 [S3]. May 2024 agreement: tariff of **US$19.28/kW-month for 2024–2026**, up from US$17.66 [S4][S6]. Brazil kept its consumer rate at US$16.71. From 2027 the tariff is expected to fall to roughly US$10–12, reflecting operating costs only, and Paraguayan surplus energy can be sold in Brazil's market [S4]. Paraguay's foreign ministry estimated ~US$1.25 bn/yr in revenue to Paraguay (snippet) [S6]. Final Annex C text and ratification status as of 2026-10: **PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND** |
| I. Fiscal exposure | Post-debt, the tariff no longer carries debt service. The fight is now over how to split the "debt-free rent" between consumer tariff relief and government or social spending [S4][S6] |

**Lessons for African structuring (Analyst inference)**
- A cost-plus tariff that includes debt service creates a cliff when the debt is repaid. Treaties and PPAs should state from day one what happens to the post-debt rent. This matters for shared-river projects such as Ruzizi III and Rusumo, and for Grand Inga.
- About 44% of total repayments (US$27.9 bn of 63.5 bn) were financial charges. Financing terms can matter as much as construction cost, and the model's levelised tariff decomposition should show this split.
- Bilateral tariff disputes went on for decades. Binational projects need a dispute and renegotiation clause with fixed review dates.

## 3. Belo Monte (Brazil): state-bank-funded concession, large overrun

| Field | Data |
|---|---|
| A. Capacity | 11,233 MW installed; 4,571 MW assured energy (~41% of nameplate) [S8] |
| C. CAPEX | Initial estimate R$19 bn (US$4.8 bn); final R$31 bn (US$7.8 bn) [S7]. R$38.6 bn spent to 2017 [S8]. Sources differ, so no single figure is given here |
| D. Financing | BNDES loan of R$22.5 bn approved Nov 2012, cited as ~80% of the project [S8]. Shareholders contributed R$12 bn (31.1%) [S8]. Mongabay cites an initial BNDES figure of R$15.2 bn [S7] |
| E. Guarantees / shareholders | Public-sector shareholders: Eletronorte 19.98%, Eletrobras 15%, Chesf 15%, plus pension funds Petros and Funcef at 10% each, and others [S8]. Treasury support of R$5 bn via Medida Provisória 511 to enable BNDES [S7] |
| F. Offtake | Winning auction bid R$78/MWh for 70% of output (regulated market). Free-market contracts at R$112–130/MWh. The 30-year terms BNDES required for the free-market share were described as unachievable [S8] |
| G. Overrun/delay | Cost rose about 63% from R$19 bn to R$31 bn [S7]. Final turbine was expected in July 2020; BNDES disbursements stopped in 2016–17 [S8] |
| H. Fiscal exposure | Treasury capitalisation of BNDES and state-owned shareholders carry the downside. One study projected lifetime losses of R$28 bn because of Xingu seasonal hydrology [S7] |

**Lessons for African structuring (Analyst inference)**
- Assured energy was ~41% of nameplate. A tariff bid against an optimistic hydrology or capacity factor shifts losses to the public shareholders and lenders. The model needs P50/P90 hydrology gates before tariff sign-off.
- A state development bank plus state-owned equity is not risk transfer: the sovereign stays the residual risk-bearer. Bankable Hydro should label this structure "public project with private wrapper" in its PPP structure comparison.

## 4. Nam Theun 2 (Lao PDR): MDB-guaranteed export IPP

| Field | Data |
|---|---|
| A. Capacity | 1,070 MW: 995 MW to EGAT, 75 MW to EDL [S10] |
| B. Generation | ~6,000 GWh/yr average [S10]. EGAT contract: up to 995 MW and 5,636 GWh/yr, take-or-pay [S9] |
| C. CAPEX | Base cost US$1.25 bn (72% debt, 28% equity) plus US$200 m contingency (funded 50:50 debt:equity, pari passu) [S9]. Total commitments US$1.58 bn including bonding facilities [S10] |
| D. Financing | Base 72:28 debt:equity: debt US$900 m, equity US$350 m [S11]. Debt including contingency ~US$1 bn, 50% USD and 50% THB, from MDBs, bilaterals, ECAs (COFACE, EKN, GIEK), 9 international and 7 Thai commercial banks [S9][S10]. Financial close 10 June 2005 [S10] |
| E. Guarantees | IDA PRG up to US$50 m; ADB PRG up to US$50 m; MIGA up to US$50 m per CAREC [S11]. The MIGA brochure reports US$91 m MIGA cover (US$86 m on Fortis-led loan; balance for EDF International transfer risk) [S9]. IDA, ADB and MIGA together covered ~US$186 m of debt [S9]. Political-risk cover was "a pre-requisite" for dollar lenders [S9] |
| F. Government equity | The Government of Lao PDR (GOL) funded its EDL stake (via NTPI) with an IDA grant of US$20 m, an ADB public loan of US$20 m, and AFD and EIB support [S11] |
| G. PPA | EGAT PPA signed 2003, 25 years from COD; concession 25 years from COD, BOOT [S9]. COD 30 April 2010 (snippet) [S12]; EIB states the plant was commissioned in April 2010 [S24] |
| H. Revenue management | Government revenues are ring-fenced for eligible poverty-reduction spending (education, health, rural roads, environment) under World Bank and ADB oversight. More than US$2 bn of government revenue projected over 25 years. First receipt was ~US$600,000 in June 2010 (snippet) [S12]. The MIGA brochure projects US$1.9 bn in FX earnings over 25 years [S9] |
| I. Cost overrun/delay | Turnkey, price-capped EPC with EDF [S9]. Material overrun: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND |
| J. Fiscal exposure (country context) | Lao PDR is now in debt distress (World Bank LIC-DSF). Public and publicly guaranteed debt was 112% of GDP at end-2022, and the energy sector (mainly EDL) was ~43% of it, with substantial further contingent liabilities [S13]. NT2 itself was export-backed; the later EDL-backed take-or-pay IPP wave is the cautionary part |

**Lessons for African structuring (Analyst inference)**
- NT2 is the template for a hard-currency, creditworthy offtaker (EGAT) combined with MDB political-risk cover and a ring-fenced government revenue framework. The NT2 model works because the offtaker is not the host-country utility. African analogues would be regional power-pool export PPAs with an investment-grade buyer.
- A small grant or concessional loan for the government's equity (IDA US$20 m, ADB US$20 m) let the host take an equity stake without budget stress. The Bankable Hydro model should treat government equity funding as a separate line, not an implicit sovereign loan.
- The later Lao experience shows the reverse: many take-or-pay PPAs signed by a weak domestic utility (EDL) built up quasi-sovereign debt and pushed the country into distress [S13]. The fiscal and contingent-liability module should aggregate the whole PPA portfolio, not assess one project at a time.

## 5. Xayaburi (Lao PDR, Mekong mainstream): commercial-bank export IPP

| Field | Data |
|---|---|
| A. Capacity | 1,285 MW (7×175 MW + 1×60 MW) [S14] |
| B. Generation | ~7,370 GWh/yr, base-load run-of-river [S14] |
| C. CAPEX | Total: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND in verified sources. Debt US$2.67 bn from six Thai banks: Exim Thailand, SCB, KTB, BBL, BAY, TBank [S14] |
| D. Sponsors | CK Power 42.5%, Global Power Synergy 25%, EGCO 12.5%, others [S14] |
| F. PPA | 1,220 MW to EGAT for 29 years at constant price; 31-year concession [S14] |
| G. Delay | COD 29 October 2019 [S14]. Original schedule: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND |
| H. Fiscal exposure | Export PPA, so limited direct Lao fiscal exposure (inference). Mekong River Commission consultation led to redesign of fish passage and sediment features. A 2022 MRC report found "moderate" impacts [S14] (secondary aggregator site) |

**Lessons for African structuring (Analyst inference)**
- A deep regional bank market plus a creditworthy cross-border offtaker can finance a >US$2.5 bn hydro without MDB guarantees. Few African markets have this today; South African banks with an Eskom or SAPP buyer are the closest analogue.
- Transboundary consultation can force redesign and add cost. Shared-basin projects such as those on the Nile, Zambezi and Congo need a budget line for this.

## 6. Upper Trishuli-1 (Nepal): DFI-led domestic IPP

| Field | Data |
|---|---|
| A. Capacity | 216 MW run-of-river, 3×72 MW [S15][S16] |
| B. Generation | ~1,427 GWh/yr [S18]; ~1,456 GWh/yr [S19] |
| C. CAPEX | US$647.4 m: equity US$194.2 m, debt US$453.2 m (~70:30) [S17] |
| D. Financing | IFC-led US$453 m package with 9 lenders. IFC US$190 m (US$95 m own account + US$95 m as implementing entity). Other lenders: KEXIM, ADB, AIIB (US$90 m), KDB, CDC/BII, FMO (US$15.38 m), OPEC Fund, Proparco [S15][S16][S18]. Announced Nov 2019 [S15] |
| E. Guarantees | MIGA US$135 m political risk cover for sponsors [S15] |
| F. Sponsors | Korea South-East Power 50%, Daelim 15%, Kyeryong 10%, IFC 15%, local partner 10% [S18] |
| G. PPA | With Nepal Electricity Authority, signed 28 Jan 2018, 30 years, take-or-pay. Tariff based on Nepal's standard run-of-river feed-in tariff with separate wet- and dry-season rates [S16][S17]. FMO cites a levelised tariff of US¢5.1/kWh [S18]. Reported as a USD-denominated PPA (headline only; page not opened) [S19b] |
| H. Delay | Expected COD 2024 at financing announcement [S15]. Completion now reported for December 2026 [S19] |
| I. Fiscal exposure | NEA, a state utility, carries take-or-pay and, if the PPA is in USD, FX risk (inference). The project PDA and PPA are expected to serve as model agreements for Nepal [S17] |

**Lessons for African structuring (Analyst inference)**
- This is the closest analogue to a typical African mid-size IPP: a state utility offtaker, DFI club, MIGA cover and a standardised tariff. The delay from the planned 2024 COD to a reported Dec 2026 shows that the model's construction-delay stress tests must flow through to the government's take-or-pay and deemed-energy exposure.
- A USD-indexed take-or-pay with a local-currency utility moves FX risk onto the public balance sheet. Bankable Hydro's contingent-liability register should value it explicitly.

## 7. Reventazón (Costa Rica): utility-sponsored trust and lease, project bond

| Field | Data |
|---|---|
| A. Capacity | 305.5 MW, 5 units [S20][S23] |
| B. Generation | ~10,000 GWh in first decade, ~9% of national consumption (article 23 Sep 2026) [S21] |
| C. CAPEX | US$1.4 bn [S20][S21] |
| D. Financing | ICE equity US$475 m. Debt US$904 m: IDB A-loan US$200 m; IFC US$100 m; US$135 m 4(a)(2)/Reg-S private placement at 8.1% coupon, rated investment grade (Fitch BBB-), 20-yr tenor; local banks US$469 m in colones, 20 yrs with 3.5-yr grace. Debt:equity 66:34. Financial close Jan 2014 [S20] |
| E. Guarantees / structure | The project is owned by Fideicomiso PH Reventazón, a Scotiabank-administered trust. ICE leases the plant and passes lease costs into tariffs. Construction, operating and cost-overrun risk pass through to ICE, so the deal is "lightly structured ICE risk" [S20] |
| G. Overrun/delay | First unit March 2016; inaugurated September 2016 (snippet) [S23]. Post-COD spillway foundation repairs (water infiltration, rock movement) estimated at US$15 m, 71% complete in May 2018 [S22] |
| H. Fiscal exposure | Sits on ICE (state utility) via the lease; recovered through regulated tariffs [S20] |

**Lessons for African structuring (Analyst inference)**
- A trust and lease structure gives off-balance-sheet form, but the risk is still sovereign or utility risk. The Bankable Hydro PPP comparison module should test substance over form: who bears overrun, hydrology and demand risk.
- Pairing local-currency bank debt (US$469 m) with USD DFI and bond debt reduced FX mismatch for a utility with local-currency revenue. This is a useful pattern for Kenya, Côte d'Ivoire and Nigeria.
- Geotechnical problems can emerge after COD (the spillway repair). Maintenance reserve and DSRA sizing should allow for early civil remediation.

## Cross-case lessons for the Bankable Hydro bankability gates (Analyst inference)

| Gate | Evidence from cases |
|---|---|
| Hydrology realism (P50/P90, assured vs nameplate) | Belo Monte 4,571/11,233 MW [S8]; Xingu seasonality loss estimate [S7] |
| Offtaker credit | NT2/Xayaburi relied on EGAT [S9][S14]; Lao EDL take-or-pay build-up became a fiscal problem [S13] |
| FX and currency matching | NT2 50/50 USD/THB debt [S9][S10]; Reventazón local-currency tranche [S20]; Upper Trishuli USD PPA [S19b] |
| Political-risk cover | NT2 IDA/ADB/MIGA as a pre-requisite [S9]; Upper Trishuli MIGA US$135 m [S15] |
| Government equity funding | NT2 IDA grant + ADB loan for GOL equity [S11] |
| Post-debt tariff rules | Itaipu Annex C, from US$19.28 down to ~US$10–12 [S3][S4] |
| Portfolio-level contingent liabilities | Lao PPG 112% of GDP, energy ~43% [S13] |
| Cost-scope discipline | Three Gorges budget 57 → 205 bn CNY depending on scope [S2] |
