# Bankable Hydro: African Hydropower Case Studies (Part 1)

## Methodology note

- **Date of research:** 2026-10-03.
- **Scope:** GERD, Inga I/II, Inga 3 and Grand Inga, Cahora Bassa, Kariba (including KDRP), Akosombo, Bui, Merowe, Gilgel Gibe I/II/III and Kafue Gorge Lower.
- **Approach:** I ran targeted web searches, about 2 to 5 per project. Where I could, I opened primary or institutional documents: World Bank project and restructuring papers, the WB/IMF Debt Sustainability Analysis, EIB project pages, EU (EEAS) pages, AidData's Chinese development finance records, the Arab and Abu Dhabi development funds, and US State Department FRUS archives. I added trade press for financing events (ENR, Water Power Magazine, Global Trade Review, African Energy, Renewable Energy World, Ghana News Agency).
- **Wikipedia:** not used as a source.
- **Source tags:**
  - **[S#]** means I opened the page with a fetch tool and checked the figure on it.
  - **[S#, snippet]** means the figure came from a search-result excerpt. The URL and title are real and were returned by the search engine, but I could not open the full page (often HTTP 403 or 404). Check these before using them in client work.
- **Missing data:** "PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND" means I found nothing in the time available. It does not mean no data exists.
- **Analyst inference:** analytic or interpretive statements are labelled "Analyst inference".
- **Currency and year:** amounts are nominal, in the currency and year the source gives. I have not deflated anything.

## 1. Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD), Ethiopia (Blue Nile)

- **A Project size:** RCC gravity dam about 145 m above the riverbed and 1,780 m long [S1]. Another source gives about 170 m high and about 1,800 m long, plus a saddle dam of about 5 km [S2]. Reservoir is 74 bn m³ [S1][S2].
- **B Installed capacity (MW):** 5,150 MW from 13 Francis units [S1][S2]. Design figures changed over time: 5,250 MW originally, raised to 6,450 MW in March 2017 [S4], then the current 5,150 MW [S1].
- **C Generation (GWh/yr):** about 15,700 GWh/yr expected [S1][S2]. Actual: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **D Hydrology notes:** Blue Nile, filled in stages from 2020 [S1]. Sediment storage is about 100 years under current conditions [S2]. There is no binding operating agreement with Egypt or Sudan [S1].
- **E CAPEX:** about USD 5 bn at completion, against an initial budget of USD 4.8 bn [S1]. One press headline gives USD 4 bn [S6, snippet].
- **F Construction period:** 2011 to inauguration on 9 Sep 2025 (14 years) [S1]. First unit (375 MW) ran in Feb 2022 [S2].
- **G Cost overruns:** PM Abiy said in Feb 2019 that the project had a 60% cost overrun because of delays [S5, snippet]. Final cost was about USD 5 bn against USD 4.8 bn initially [S1]; the sources do not reconcile these two figures.
- **H Financing structure:** government states 91% was financed domestically through bonds, payroll contributions and public fundraising [S1]. Central bank financing is also mentioned [S2].
- **I Debt:** about USD 1 bn from China Exim Bank for turbines and electromechanical equipment [S1]. Terms: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **J Equity:** 100% state-owned (Analyst inference; developed by the state utility). Amount: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **K Concessional finance:** built largely without multilateral bank funding [S1].
- **L Government contribution:** 91% of about USD 5 bn financed domestically [S1].
- **M Guarantees:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **N PPA structure:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. Export agreements with Sudan, Djibouti and Kenya are reported [S1].
- **O Offtaker:** Ethiopian Electric Power (state) and export buyers [S1]. Contract terms: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **P Utility financial condition:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Q Transmission requirements:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. Export links to Sudan, Djibouti and Kenya are referenced [S1].
- **R Grid integration:** units commissioned in phases between 2022 and 2024 [S2]. 6 units were operating at inauguration [S1].
- **S Demand assumptions:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** none; public EPC model with Webuild (formerly Salini Impregilo) on civil works [S1][S3].
- **W FX risk:** Analyst inference: FX exposure was small relative to cost because most funding was in local currency. The China Exim component (about USD 1 bn [S1]) carries USD/CNY risk.
- **X Political risk:** high transboundary risk. Egypt "will not accept measures that endanger" its water security, and Sudan raised safety concerns [S1].
- **Y Environmental/social risk:** downstream water-security and coordination concerns [S1]. Resettlement figures: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** about 91% of about USD 5 bn carried on the domestic balance sheet and by citizens [S1].
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AB Delays:**
  - Originally a 5-year build [S4].
  - Zero units running after 8 years, with 65% complete as of Dec 2018 [S4].
  - METEC's electromechanical contract was cancelled in Aug 2018 [S3][S4].
  - Contracts followed with China Gezhouba (USD 40.1 m) and Voith Hydro Shanghai (USD 113 m) [S5, snippet].
- **AC Restructuring:** contractor restructuring only (METEC removed in 2018) [S3]. Debt restructuring: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AD Lessons for financial close (Analyst inference):** Domestic mobilization can stand in for financial close when multilaterals stay away because of transboundary disputes. The cost of that route was a long schedule, a large overrun and weak electromechanical capability at a state contractor. Without a riparian agreement, a project like this is unlikely to be bankable on non-recourse terms.

## 2. Inga I/II, Inga 3 and Grand Inga, DRC (Congo River)

- **A Project size:** Grand Inga is envisaged at about 40,000 MW in seven stages [S7]. Inga 3 is the next stage.
- **B Installed capacity (MW):**
  - Inga 1 and 2: 2,132 MW combined [S7]. A separate report gives 350 MW for Inga 1 and 1,424 MW for Inga 2 [S11, snippet], so the range is about 1,774 to 2,132 MW.
  - Inga 3: 4,800 MW [S7]. The World Bank describes 2 to 11 GW of potential [S8].
  - A 7 GW variant was proposed in 2024 [S10, snippet].
- **C Generation (GWh/yr):** Inga 1 and 2 ran at only about 40% of capacity as of 2013 [S7]. GWh figures: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **D Hydrology notes:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **E CAPEX:** Inga 3 estimated at about USD 14 bn [S7]. Inga I/II rehabilitation: an IDA grant of about USD 300 m funded rehabilitation and market development [S12, snippet].
- **F Construction period:** Inga 1 and 2 were built in 1972 and 1982 (search summary of the International Rivers sheet [S9, snippet]). Inga 3 has not started. The World Bank says preparation and construction take about a decade [S8].
- **G Cost overruns:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. Inga 3 has not been built.
- **H Financing structure:**
  - Inga 3 technical assistance of USD 106.5 m was announced in March 2014: World Bank USD 73.1 m and AfDB USD 33.4 m [S7].
  - In 2025 the World Bank approved a USD 1 bn multiphase programme. Phase 1 is a USD 250 m IDA credit approved on 3 Jun 2025 [S8].
- **I Debt:** none for construction. Concessional only (see H) [S7][S8].
- **J Equity:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
  - Developer selection has changed repeatedly.
  - Fortescue was removed in May 2024 [S10, snippet].
  - Natural Oilfield Services signed a preliminary deal for feasibility work on a 7 GW scheme [S10, snippet].
- **K Concessional finance:** World Bank TA of USD 73.1 m, of which only 6% was disbursed before suspension on 25 Jul 2016 [S7]. IDA programme of USD 1 bn, with USD 250 m in phase 1 [S8].
- **L Government contribution:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **M Guarantees:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **N PPA structure:** a 2013 SA–DRC treaty gives South Africa 2,500 MW of Inga 3's 4,800 MW [S7][S13, snippet]. Tariff and terms: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **O Offtaker:** Eskom/South Africa (anchor) [S7][S13, snippet]. Domestic mining offtake: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **P Utility financial condition:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. Context: DRC electricity access is 21%, with a target of 62% by 2030 [S8].
- **Q Transmission requirements:** a long-distance line to South Africa is implied by the 2,500 MW export treaty [S7]. Specifications: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **R Grid integration:** Inga 1 and 2 "provide most of DRC's electricity" [S8].
- **S Demand assumptions:** about 7 m people in the Kinshasa area to receive power directly from Inga 3, plus 2 m from three mid-size projects [S7].
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** the planned development agency (ADEPI) was to be set up by end-2014 but was never established [S7]. ADPI-DRC is now named as the lead agency [S8].
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** intended as a private-developer PPP with an export PPA [S7][S10, snippet]. Final structure: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **W FX risk:** Analyst inference: a rand-denominated export PPA would leave USD debt exposed to the rand.
- **X Political risk:** very high.
  - The World Bank suspended disbursement in July 2016 over governance and strategic direction [S7].
  - The developer was replaced in 2024 [S10, snippet].
- **Y Environmental/social risk:** about 100 communities and 1.2 m people in Kongo Central are targeted by the local-development phase [S8].
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AB Delays:** multi-decade. Tenders launched before the studies were finished triggered the 2016 World Bank exit [S7].
- **AC Restructuring:** the 2016 TA suspension, then a 2025 restart as a phased IDA programme [S7][S8].
- **AD Lessons for financial close (Analyst inference):**
  - Institutions (the development agency) and completed studies come before developer selection.
  - An anchor export PPA (South Africa) alone did not deliver close when governance and procurement were contested.
  - The 2025 approach starts with local benefits and phased tranches, which de-risks the social licence before committing capital.

## 3. Cahora Bassa (HCB), Mozambique (Zambezi)

- **A Project size:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (dam dimensions).
- **B Installed capacity (MW):** 2,075 MW, as 5 units of 415 MW after the Alstom refurbishment [S22][S27].
- **C Generation (GWh/yr):** 2023 expected production was 15,753.5 GWh, against a 14,291.7 GWh target [S28]. 12,351 GWh was delivered in 2024 [S27]. Design: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **D Hydrology notes:** Zambezi. A drought affected recent output; 2024 deliveries were lower [S27].
- **E CAPEX:** original construction cost: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. In 2007 Mozambique paid Portugal about USD 950 m to take control [S22].
- **F Construction period:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (original build). Ownership transferred on 27 Nov 2007 [S25, snippet].
- **G Cost overruns:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **H Financing structure:**
  - The 2007 acquisition was financed by a USD 800 m electricity prepayment facility signed in Nov 2007.
  - Borrower was "Renascer", a Mauritius SPV. Mandated lead arrangers were Banco BPI and Calyon.
  - Tenor was 10 years. Margins were 190 bp in years 1–2, 240 bp in years 3–6 and 280 bp in years 7–10 [S23].
- **I Debt:**
  - USD 800 m prepayment facility (BPI and Calyon) [S23].
  - Earlier reports: USD 700 m from Calyon and BPI, about USD 300 m each, with DBSA USD 200 m as sub-underwriter [S24, snippet].
  - Range: USD 700–800 m [S23][S24].
- **J Equity:** ownership moved from Mozambique 18% / Portugal 82% to Mozambique 85% [S22]. In 2019 an IPO floated 4.0% of shares on the Bolsa de Valores de Moçambique, raised from 2.5% because of demand [S26, snippet].
- **K Concessional finance:** none. This was "Mozambique's first big ticket commercial transaction executed without political risk insurance or the support of multilaterals" [S23].
- **L Government contribution:** about USD 250 m was paid in 2006 as the first stage of the transfer [S24, snippet].
- **M Guarantees:** no political risk insurance or multilateral support [S23]. The IMF urged non-recourse finance to limit government liabilities [S22].
- **N PPA structure:**
  - Long-term Eskom supply agreement in place since 1979, expiring in 2030 [S27].
  - EDM receives 300 MW firm plus 380 MW variable [S27].
  - Mozal contract ends in 2026 [S27].
- **O Offtaker:** 2024 split of deliveries [S27]:
  - Eskom: 8,319 GWh (66%)
  - EDM: 3,451 GWh (30%)
  - ZESA: 499 GWh (4%)
- **P Utility financial condition:** HCB is described as "financially healthy" [S28]. 2023 revenue to the state was MZN 13.06 bn (USD 204.7 m) through November [S28].
- **Q Transmission requirements:** long-distance export line to South Africa (Analyst inference from the Eskom offtake [S27]). Transmission losses were 6.76% in 2024 and 7.60% in 2023 [S27].
- **R Grid integration:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (beyond S27).
- **S Demand assumptions:** the government targets recovering the "8–10 TWh" now exported to South Africa by 2031 [S27].
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (verified source).
- **U Regulation:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** state-majority corporatised concessionaire, with a 4.0% public float [S26, snippet].
- **W FX risk:** Eskom pays in rand. The 2007 structure was designed to "neutralise the foreign exchange risk" for HCB and lenders [S23].
- **X Political risk:** a decades-long Portugal–Mozambique control dispute, resolved by a Dec 2006 MoU and the 2007 transfer [S22][S24, snippet].
- **Y Environmental/social risk:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** the IMF stressed non-recourse structuring to "keep down the government's liabilities" [S22].
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AB Delays:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AC Restructuring:** ownership restructuring in 2007 [S22][S23].
- **AD Lessons for financial close (Analyst inference):** An existing hydro asset with a creditworthy hard-currency-linked offtaker (Eskom) can raise large commercial, non-sovereign financing secured on export receivables, without multilaterals or PRI. PPA expiry in 2030 is the key refinancing and valuation risk for any new HCB-level debt.

## 4. Kariba (Zambia/Zimbabwe) and the Kariba Dam Rehabilitation Project (KDRP)

- **A Project size:**
  - Double-curvature arch dam on the Zambezi, owned by the bi-national Zambezi River Authority (ZRA) [S14][S16].
  - Drainage basin is about 663,848 km² [S16].
  - A scour hole about 80 m deep had formed below the dam [S16].
- **B Installed capacity (MW):** 2,130 MW of installed generation that KDRP protects [S14]. Split by Kariba North and South: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **C Generation (GWh/yr):** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. The 2024 water allocation for generation was cut to 16 bn m³, split equally between Zambia and Zimbabwe [S19, snippet].
- **D Hydrology notes:**
  - Severe drought in 2023/24.
  - The 2024 allocation of 16 bn m³ was the lowest since 2016 [S19, snippet].
  - Plunge-pool deterioration forced the operating rule curve down by 3.5 m [S15].
- **E CAPEX:** KDRP was USD 294.2 m at appraisal (2014) and revised to USD 273.1 m in the 2022 restructuring (a net reduction after exchange losses) [S15][S16].
- **F Construction period:** IDA approved on 8 Dec 2014 [S18, snippet]. Plunge-pool works started in Mar 2017 and spillway works in Nov 2019 [S15]. Reported as 94% complete in May 2026 and targeting year-end 2026 [S21, snippet (headline)].
- **G Cost overruns:** about USD 20 m of overruns on the plunge pool from unforeseen geotechnical conditions and extra seepage-reduction works [S15].
- **H Financing structure:** grants and loans totalling USD 294.2 m [S15][S16]:
  - IDA USD 75 m
  - AfDB USD 75 m
  - EU-EDF USD 100 m
  - Sweden/SIDA grant USD 25 m
  - ZRA/borrower USD 19.2 m
- **I Debt:** IDA credit of USD 75 m to Zambia, 38-year maturity and 6-year grace [S18, snippet]. AfDB USD 75 m [S15]. Loan/grant split: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **J Equity:** n/a. ZRA is jointly owned by the two governments [S14].
- **K Concessional finance:** effectively all external funding (IDA, AfDB, EU-EDF, SIDA) [S15]. The EU states its contribution as about EUR 114 m [S14].
- **L Government contribution:** USD 19.2 m via ZRA/borrower [S15][S16].
- **M Guarantees:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **N PPA structure:** ZRA sells water, not power. The utilities pay ZRA for water. End-2021 arrears to ZRA [S15]:
  - ZESCO: USD 45.0 m
  - ZPC/KHPC: USD 24.4 m
  - Together, more than 4x ZRA's annual operating expenses.
- **O Offtaker:** ZESCO (Zambia) and the ZESA/ZPC-linked company (Zimbabwe) [S15][S19, snippet].
- **P Utility financial condition:** weak. The arrears to ZRA are above [S15]. ZESCO's broader condition is covered under KGL (§9) [S61].
- **Q Transmission requirements:** n/a (rehabilitation).
- **R Grid integration:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **S Demand assumptions:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. The economic re-analysis gives a 30.6% IRR [S15].
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** bi-national treaty authority (ZRA) [S14].
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** none.
- **W FX risk:** exchange losses contributed to the 2022 revision of project cost [S15].
- **X Political risk:** two-sovereign governance. Zimbabwe's arrears constrain access to IDA (Analyst inference; not confirmed in sources).
- **Y Environmental/social risk:** Category A, full assessment [S16]. Dam-failure risk was the project rationale [S14].
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** Analyst inference: dam-safety failure is a large implicit liability on both sovereigns.
- **AB Delays:** about 3 years of cumulative delay from procurement, geotechnical surprises and COVID-19 [S15]. The SIDA grant was extended from 30 Jun 2022 to 28 Feb 2025 [S15].
- **AC Restructuring:** 2022 restructuring (RES50183) covering cost and timeline revisions [S15]. 35.4% (USD 93 m) was disbursed by 15 May 2022 [S15].
- **AD Lessons for financial close (Analyst inference):**
  - Multi-donor grant-heavy blends can fund shared-asset safety works that no lender would finance commercially.
  - Geotechnical contingency for plunge pools and foundations must be large.
  - Hydrology (2024 drought) and utility arrears to the river authority are the binding risks, not construction.

## 5. Akosombo (Volta River Project), Ghana (VRA)

- **A Project size:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (dam dimensions or reservoir).
- **B Installed capacity (MW):**
  - The Loan 310-GH initial stage had 4 units totalling 588 MW [S32, snippet].
  - Another source gives 912 MW original, raised to 1,020 MW after the 2006 modifications [S34, snippet].
- **C Generation (GWh/yr):** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **D Hydrology notes:** controlled spillage from 15 Sep 2023 due to high inflows, followed by downstream flooding [S35, snippet].
- **E CAPEX:** two figures:
  - USD 168 m total, per a 1961 US State Department record [S31, snippet].
  - USD 258 m (about USD 2.1 bn today), per ICE [S34, snippet].
  - Range: USD 168–258 m (1960s USD).
- **F Construction period:** 1962–1965 [S32, snippet].
- **G Cost overruns:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND. Analyst inference: the gap between USD 168 m and USD 258 m may reflect scope differences (smelter or transmission) rather than an overrun.
- **H Financing structure (1961 plan):** Ghana about 50%, World Bank USD 40 m, DLF and US Exim USD 30 m, UK USD 14 m [S31, snippet]. Foreign financing was conditional on financing arrangements for the VALCO smelter [S31, snippet].
- **I Debt:** World Bank Loan 310-GH of USD 47 m. Board approved 7 Sep 1961; agreement dated 8 Feb 1962 [S33, snippet][S32, snippet].
- **J Equity:** state (VRA, created by the Volta River Development Act 1961) [S32, snippet].
- **K Concessional finance:** World Bank loan, DLF and US Exim, and UK government loan [S31, snippet].
- **L Government contribution:** about 50% of USD 168 m [S31, snippet].
- **M Guarantees:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **N PPA structure:** anchor long-term bulk power contract with VALCO, the Kaiser-led aluminium smelter. It was a precondition for foreign financing [S31, snippet][S34, snippet]. Terms: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **O Offtaker:** VALCO, large mines and the Electricity Corporation of Ghana [S32, snippet].
- **P Utility financial condition:** weak.
  - ECG pays about 50–60% of its monthly VRA bill despite the Cash Waterfall Mechanism [S36, snippet].
  - Calls have been made to resolve VRA–ECG–VALCO debts [S37, snippet].
- **Q Transmission requirements:** transmission to VALCO, mines and ECG was financed under Loan 310-GH [S32, snippet].
- **R Grid integration:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **S Demand assumptions:** smelter-anchored demand [S31, snippet][S32, snippet].
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** public utility (VRA) with a private industrial anchor offtaker (VALCO) [S31, snippet].
- **W FX risk:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **X Political risk:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Y Environmental/social risk:** about 80,000 people from 700 villages were relocated [S34, snippet]. The 2023 spillage caused flooding, and VRA was criticised [S35, snippet].
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** Analyst inference: the circular debt chain (ECG–VRA–VALCO) is an implicit fiscal liability.
- **AB Delays:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AC Restructuring:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AD Lessons for financial close (Analyst inference):**
  - The classic "anchor industrial offtaker" model: lenders required the smelter deal before closing.
  - Sixty years on, distribution-company non-payment, not hydrology, drives the generator's credit risk.

## 6. Bui, Ghana (Black Volta; Bui Power Authority)

- **A Project size:** dam 108 m above foundation, 12.57 bn m³ reservoir, 444 km² inundated [S38]. The total project included irrigation, fisheries and transmission [S39, snippet].
- **B Installed capacity (MW):** 400 MW, described by BPA as 404 MW [S38][S40]. 5 MW floating solar and a 50 MWp solar PV farm were added [S43, snippet][S40].
- **C Generation (GWh/yr):** average annual production of 969 GWh [S42, snippet]. About 1,554 GWh in 2022, BPA's record year [S40].
- **D Hydrology notes:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **E CAPEX:** USD 622 m initially; raised by about USD 168 m to USD 790 m in a 2012 cost review [S38].
- **F Construction period:** commissioned Dec 2013 [S38]. Start date: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **G Cost overruns:** about USD 168 m, roughly 27% [S38].
- **H Financing structure:** China Exim concessional loan USD 270 m, China Exim commercial loan USD 272 m, Government of Ghana USD 60 m [S38]. EPC by Sinohydro at USD 594 m [S39, snippet].
- **I Debt:** China Exim, USD 542 m in total; 5-year grace and 20-year amortisation [S38].
- **J Equity:** state (BPA).
- **K Concessional finance:** China Exim concessional tranche of USD 270 m [S38].
- **L Government contribution:** USD 60 m [S38].
- **M Guarantees and security:**
  - 85% of electricity sales proceeds go to an escrow account [S38].
  - Proceeds of 30,000 tonnes a year of cocoa exports to China serve as loan collateral [S38].
- **N PPA structure:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND (terms). Almost 99% of output is sold to ECG [S40].
- **O Offtaker:** ECG [S40].
- **P Utility financial condition:**
  - ECG owes BPA USD 612 m (Mar 2023) [S40].
  - Another report puts it at USD 614.4 m at end-2022, up from USD 386 m at end-2019 [S41, snippet].
  - ECG pays about 30% of the value of power generated [S40].
- **Q Transmission requirements:** the transmission line was part of the USD 622 m project scope [S39, snippet].
- **R Grid integration:** hydro–solar hybrid strategy, with a 250 MW solar target [S40].
- **S Demand assumptions:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** state authority with Chinese EPC plus finance (Analyst inference from S38, S39).
- **W FX risk:** USD debt service against cedi-paying ECG. This is mitigated by the cocoa-export escrow (Analyst inference from S38).
- **X Political risk:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Y Environmental/social risk:** 1,216 people resettled; 444 km² inundated [S38].
- **Z Fiscal exposure:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AA Contingent liabilities:** Analyst inference: ECG arrears to BPA (USD 612 m [S40]) are a quasi-fiscal liability.
- **AB Delays:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AC Restructuring:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **AD Lessons for financial close (Analyst inference):**
  - Commodity-backed (cocoa) escrow plus a revenue escrow let Ghana close Chinese financing quickly.
  - Collateral outside the power sector shields the lender from offtaker non-payment, while BPA itself absorbs the ECG shortfall.
  - BPA posted a USD 74 m profit in 2022 [S40] while still owed USD 612 m. Accounting profit is not cash.

## 7. Merowe, Sudan (Nile, 4th Cataract)

- **A Project size:** dam about 9 km long and up to 67 m high, with an 8.3 km³ reservoir [S46].
- **B Installed capacity (MW):** 1,250 MW as 10 units of 125 MW [S45][S46].
- **C Generation (GWh/yr):** "over 6,500 GWh" per year [S46]. Actual: PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **D Hydrology notes:** silting; the dam had lost 1.7% of storage by 2020 [S44].
- **E CAPEX:**
  - USD 2.4 bn total project cost, per ADFD [S46].
  - AidData gives a USD 1–3 bn range [S44].
  - A Kuwait Fund statement implies its ~USD 217 m equals 7.95% of cost [S45]; Analyst inference: that implies about USD 2.7 bn.
- **F Construction period:** started 8 Jul 2003 [S44]. Planned completion was 30 Jul 2008; the 10th unit connected on 8 Apr 2010 [S44]. ADFD says built 2003–2009 [S46].
- **G Cost overruns:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **H Financing structure:** Government of Sudan about 40%; the rest from Arab funds and Chinese long-term loans [S45].
- **I Debt:**
  - China Exim USD 608 m at 3.5% fixed, 20-year maturity and 6.5-year grace, committed 23 Dec 2003 [S44].
  - Abu Dhabi Fund USD 200 m [S46].
  - Kuwait Fund about USD 164.5 m, plus KWD 15 m (USD 52.5 m) in 2008 [S45].
  - Arab Fund, Saudi Fund and others are listed by AidData. Amounts differ by source [S44].
- **J Equity:** state (Dams Implementation Unit) (Analyst inference).
- **K Concessional finance:** Arab and Gulf development funds and China Exim at 3.5% [S44][S45][S46].
- **L Government contribution:** about 40% [S45].
- **M Guarantees:** the China Exim loan was collateralised with oil revenues. That collateral was compromised after South Sudan's secession in 2011 [S44].
- **N PPA structure:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **O Offtaker:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **P Utility financial condition:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **Q Transmission requirements:** about 500 km of new 500 kV line to Khartoum and about 1,000 km of 220 kV lines [S46]. Harbin built the lines and substations [S45].
- **R Grid integration:** the plant roughly doubled Sudan's capacity, from about 1,000 MW in 2006 [S46].
- **S Demand assumptions:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **T Tariff:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **U Regulation:** PUBLIC DATA NOT FOUND.
- **V PPP/IPP structure:** none. Public works with Chinese contractors (CWE, Sinohydro, Harbin) and Alstom electromechanical [S44][S45].

## Cross-cutting lessons (Analyst inference)

1. **Offtaker credit dominates.**
   - VRA, BPA and ZRA are all owed hundreds of millions by distribution companies or utilities [S15][S36][S40][S61].
   - HCB's rand-linked Eskom export is the counter-example that financed commercially without multilaterals [S23].
2. **Procurement and governance determine the financing pool.**
   - Direct awards (Gibe III) and contested tenders (Inga 3) pushed out the World Bank, AfDB and EIB [S7][S53].
   - The financing then moved to Chinese policy banks, or did not happen at all.
3. **Commodity and receivable escrows substitute for credit-worthy offtake.**
   - Examples: Bui cocoa escrow [S38], the HCB electricity prepayment [S23] and Merowe oil collateral [S44].
   - Collateral can be impaired by political events, as South Sudan's secession showed [S44].
4. **Schedule risk is cost risk.**
   - GERD had a reported 60% overrun [S5, snippet].
   - At KGL, interest and insurance were about 21% of cost [S58].
   - Bui ran 27% over [S38]. Kariba lost about 3 years to geotechnical surprises [S15].
5. **Hydrology and transboundary risk sit outside the lender's control.**
   - Kariba's 2024 allocation fell to 16 bn m³ [S19, snippet].
   - GERD has no riparian agreement [S1], and Gibe III affects Lake Turkana [S53].
   - These risks need regional institutions such as ZRA, plus drought-stress testing of debt service.
