# Competitor Review: Books and Book-Length Guides on Hydropower Development and Finance

**Research date:** 2026-10-04
**Prepared for:** "Hydropower Development and Finance (Africa Energy Finance, Book 7)". The repository README calls this product *Bankable Hydro*.
**Scope:** Books and book-length guides on hydropower **project development and finance**, plus general project-finance books that compete for the same reader. General hydraulic-engineering texts are included only where they claim to cover development or economics.
**Companion file:** `research/competitive_intelligence.md` (2026-10-03) benchmarks 33 products, including Excel models and courses. This file goes deeper on **books and guides only** and adds full-text checks of the free institutional guides.

---

## 1. Methodology

1. **Search.** I ran web searches for every title named in the brief, then broader searches for hydro development and finance books (Springer, Elsevier, Wiley, CRC/Routledge, ICE, Palgrave, Practical Action), Africa-focused hydro books, and any "book + companion Excel model + case" format for hydro.
2. **Full-text checks.** Five free guides were downloaded and read as PDF text: IFC 2015, ESHA 2004, Penche/ESHA 1998 (Italian edition), World Bank/ESMAP 2024, Head/World Bank 2000, and Addleshaw Goddard/IHA 2021. For these I extracted the table of contents and counted key terms: PPA, premium, financial close, transmission, credit, guarantee, development cost/budget, concession. The counts appear in the profiles. They show only whether a topic is there, not how good the treatment is.
3. **Publisher pages.** I opened publisher and bookseller pages where they loaded. Springer, World Bank OKR, ESMAP and Amazon pages returned 403 errors or login redirects to the fetch tool. Facts from those sources come from search-result text only and are marked **(snippet)**.
4. **No invention.** If I could not find a fact, the entry says **not found**. "n/v" in the matrix means not verified: I could not see the table of contents, so I make no rating claim.
5. **Ratings.** Amazon pages could not be fetched, so no Amazon ratings are reported. Goodreads ratings appear only where I opened the page.
6. **Titles in the brief that I could not locate.** I could not find a hydropower project-management or development book by **Ottens**, by **Bhattacharya** (only P.K. Bhattacharya's *Water Power Engineering*, Khanna 1984, an engineering text, turned up (snippet)), by **Nag**, or by **Kaldellis**. For Kaldellis only a 2005 *Energy Policy* paper on small-hydro techno-economics turned up [S27]. I also could not find a Wiley book titled **"Small Hydro Power Plants"**, or a **Böttcher** book titled "Financing Renewable Energy" or "Handbook of Project Finance" from Wiley. The closest Böttcher match is *Green Banking* (De Gruyter, 2020) [S24]. Where the brief named these titles, I substituted the nearest real books that do exist.
7. **Analyst judgement** is labelled as such. Ratings of what a book "lacks" rest on its table of contents and description (or full text, where read), not on reading every page.

## 3. Title profiles

### 3.1 Hydro-specific development and finance guides (closest competitors)

**1. *Hydroelectric Power: A Guide for Developers and Investors***
- **Author/publisher:** International Finance Corporation (IFC). Material developed by Fichtner Management Consulting; managed by Stratos Tavoulareas (IFC) [S1 full text].
- **Year:** 2015 [S2]. **Pages:** 120 (PDF) [S1]. **Price:** free, CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 IGO (snippet) [S2].
- **Audience:** "IFC and World Bank clients and stakeholders planning and implementing hydro projects" (Foreword) [S1].
- **TOC highlights** [S1]:
  - Overview
  - Design and Typical Layouts
  - Project Development (7 phases, Figure 5-1; Tables 5-1 and 5-2 on time required)
  - Site Selection
  - Hydrology and Energy Calculations
  - Permits and Licensing
  - Construction
  - Commissioning
  - O&M
  - E&S Impact Mitigation (Equator Principles)
  - Capital and O&M Costs
  - Economics and Financial Analyses
  - Financing HPP Projects (project vs. corporate finance, key players, risks and mitigation; Box 15-1 on financing small hydro)
  - Annex 17.1, a comprehensive HPP checklist (Table 17-1)
- **Does well:** It is the only free, hydro-specific, lifecycle guide that ties development phases to bank involvement. Financing institutions come in at Phase 3. The checklist annex is practical, and the guide gives a $/kW cost range for greenfield large hydro.
- **Lacks (for an African IPP developer or financier):**
  - The Foreword says outright that the permitting and financing sections are "a high-level review", while the technical sections are the detailed part [S1].
  - Nothing on development budget by stage, developer returns or development premium. "Premium" appears once, about peak prices, not developer economics.
  - "Financial close" does not appear at all (0 hits).
  - The guide says financial analysis is done in "complex custom spreadsheet models" but supplies none.
  - The worked example is the Ma River project in Vietnam, not Africa. Lessons are drawn from the Balkan Renewable Energy Program.
  - No analysis of utility credit risk or fiscal exposure.
- **Ratings:** not found.

**2. *Leveraging Private Sector Solutions in Large Hydropower Projects***
- **Author/publisher:** World Bank (ESMAP and PPIAF support). Task team led by Nicolas Sans and Patrice Caporossi; research by Mott MacDonald and CrossBoundary Group [S3 full text].
- **Year:** 2024; ESMAP page dated 16 Oct 2024 (snippet) [S4]. **Pages:** 52 (PDF) [S3]. **Price:** free, CC BY 3.0 IGO [S3].
- **Audience:** regulators, policymakers, government agencies, developers, banks (snippet) [S4].
- **TOC highlights** [S3]:
  - Private Sector Participation in Hydropower
  - Survey of 23 stakeholders (risks, interest, preferred geographies, private development approaches)
  - Capital Recycling (green bonds, YieldCos)
  - DFI Role (enabling environment, de-risking project preparation, de-risking financing, scaling mechanisms)
  - Figures: "Typical Hydropower Project Timeline, Risk, Stages and CAPEX" (Fig. 9) and "Generic PPP Project Sequence" (Fig. 13)
  - The survey questionnaire is annexed
- **Does well:** It is current (interviews July–October 2023), market-sounding based, and frank about why the private sector stays away from large hydro. It covers liquidity extension guarantees (LEG appears 19 times with "liquidity") and capital recycling.
- **Lacks:**
  - A policy report, not a how-to book.
  - Nothing on development premium (0 hits).
  - PPA gets only 11 mentions and transmission 7.
  - No model, no worked case and no stage-gate tools.
  - It addresses DFIs rather than developers.
- **Ratings:** not found.

**3. *Guide on How to Develop a Small Hydropower Plant***
- **Author/publisher:** European Small Hydropower Association (ESHA), Brussels; distributed by SKAT [S5, S6].
- **Year:** 2004 [S5]. **Pages:** 151 (Part 1 PDF) [S7]. **Price:** free download [S6].
- **Audience:** practitioners and potential local developers of small hydro (snippet) [S5].
- **TOC** [S7]:
  1. Introduction
  2. Fundamentals of Hydraulic Engineering
  3. Evaluating Stream Flow
  4. Site Evaluation Methodologies
  5. Hydraulic Structures
  6. Electromechanical Equipment
  7. Environmental Impact
  8. Economic Analysis
  9. Administrative Procedures
- **Does well:** A clear step-by-step path for evaluating a small scheme before a full feasibility study. The economic analysis chapter covers IRR (13 mentions).
- **Lacks:**
  - Built for the EU context of 2004.
  - Africa: 0 mentions. Project finance: 0. Loans: 0. PPA: 3 mentions.
  - No offtaker credit, no transmission interface, no model.
- **Ratings:** not found.

**4. *Layman's Guidebook on How to Develop a Small Hydro Site* (2nd ed.)**
- **Author/publisher:** Celso Penche, for ESHA and the European Commission DG XVII (ALTENER programme) (snippet) [S8].
- **Year:** June 1998 (snippet) [S8]. **Pages:** 278 in the Italian edition I read [S9]. English page count not found. **Price:** free.
- **Audience:** laymen and non-specialist engineers producing a primary feasibility report (snippet) [S8].
- **TOC** (Italian edition) [S9]: chapters on water resources, electromechanical equipment, environmental impact, economic analysis (Ch. 8, including methods of economic evaluation) and administrative procedures (Ch. 9).
- **Does well:** The ancestor of #3. Thorough on technical screening.
- **Lacks:** Same gaps as #3, and older.
- **Ratings:** not found.

**5. *Financing of Private Hydropower Projects* (World Bank Discussion Paper No. 420)**
- **Author/publisher:** Chris Head, World Bank [S10 full text].
- **Year:** July 2000 [S10]. **Pages:** about 95 plus an annex of project profiles (TOC runs to p. 95+) [S10]. **Price:** free.
- **Audience:** practitioners and policymakers in developing countries (snippet) [S11].
- **TOC highlights** [S10]:
  - Issues in the Financing of Hydro Projects (hydro-specific problems)
  - Regulatory Background and Concession Arrangements (award of concessions, royalty arrangements)
  - The Offtake Contracts (PPAs for the candidate projects)
  - Financing Arrangements (equity, debt, public-sector support)
  - Project Implementation (contractor risk allocation)
  - Role of the Host Government and Utility
  - Cost Profile of Private Hydropower Projects
  - Credit Enhancement and Risk
  - The Solicitation Process
  - Models for Future Developments
  - Table 2: "Thermal vs. Hydro: Factors Affecting Bankability"
- **Does well:** Still the most finance-literate hydro-specific text found. Ten real projects in the Philippines, Lao PDR, Nepal, Turkey and Brazil [S10]. Strong on concessions, offtake contracts, credit enhancement and solicitation. Mentions: concession 159, guarantee 103, credit 61, PPA 56.
- **Lacks:**
  - Dated (2000).
  - No African projects.
  - No development budget (0 hits for development cost or budget).
  - No model, no checklist, no financial-close mechanics.
- **Ratings:** not found.

**6. *An Investor's Guide to Hydropower in Africa***
- **Author/publisher:** Addleshaw Goddard "with support from" IHA (IHA contributors listed). Country chapters by local firms [S12 full text].
- **Year:** PDF created May 2021 [S12]. **Pages:** 39 PDF pages (spreads; contents run to p. 68) [S12]. **Price:** free.
- **Audience:** host governments, private investors, funders and procuring entities. Main focus is the IPP model [S12].
- **TOC** [S12]: Key Legal Bankability and Risk Issues (procurement models: "World Bank" model, PPP/IPP, and others; hydrological risk), then country profiles for Cameroon, Ethiopia, Ghana, Malawi, Morocco, Mozambique, Nigeria, Rwanda, Uganda and Zambia.
- **Does well:** The only Africa-wide, hydro-specific bankability text found. Good on hydrological risk allocation in PPAs: deemed energy, deemed available capacity, hydrological floors and ceilings, and why parametric insurance is rare in Africa [S12]. Covers water rights and concessions country by country.
- **Lacks:**
  - Legal only.
  - Development budget: 0 hits. "Financial close": 1 hit.
  - No numbers on utility credit, no model, no case, no developer economics.
  - A marketing report, not a book.
- **Ratings:** not applicable.

**23. *How-to Guides* series** (Hydropower Sustainability Alliance, formerly IHA)
- **Year:** 12 guides, 2019–2025. Sedimentation 2019; most others 2024; climate mitigation and resilience 2025 [S13].
- **Price:** free PDFs (e.g. [S14]).
- **Content:** E&S assessment and management, resettlement, benefit sharing, indigenous peoples, labour, flows, biodiversity, infrastructure safety, communications, climate [S13]. The ESIA guide covers budgeting for E&S and engaging stakeholders across the project life cycle (snippet) [S15].
- **Does well:** The authoritative good-practice standard for E&S. Lenders will expect a developer to have aligned with it.
- **Lacks:** No guide on finance, contracts, PPAs or development economics [S13].

**25. *Directions in Hydropower: Scaling Up for Development*** (World Bank Water Working Note 21)
- **Year:** June 2009 (snippet) [S16]. **Price:** free.
- **Content:** World Bank strategy note on scaling up hydropower (snippet) [S16].
- **Assessment:** Policy context only. Not a practitioner competitor.

### 3.2 Hydro books (engineering or economics) that claim development or economics coverage

**7. *Hydropower Economics*, 2nd ed.** (International Series in Operations Research and Management Science, vol. 217)
- **Author/publisher:** Finn R. Førsund, Springer.
- **Year/pages:** 2015, xvi + 326 pp. (snippet) [S17]. **Price:** not found (page redirects to login) [S17].
- **Content:** A thorough revision of the 2007 edition with five new chapters (snippet) [S17]. The introduction centres on Norway's hydropower pricing history and public ownership [S18].
- **Does well:** Rigorous economics of hydro operation and pricing.
- **Lacks:** A Nordic, operations-research orientation. Not about project development or finance. No developer economics, no PPA, no model. Full TOC not seen.
- **Ratings:** not found.

**8. *Hydropower***
- **Author/publisher:** Paul Breeze, Academic Press (Elsevier).

- **Audience:** power generation planners, electrical engineers, students [S20].
- **TOC:** 16 chapters on resources, site selection, dam types, turbines, generators, small hydro and environment, ending with "Cost of Electricity Generation from Hydropower Plants" [S20].

- **Ratings:** not found.

**9. *Small Hydropower: Design and Analysis***

### 3.3 General project-finance books (compete for the financier reader)

**17. *Project Finance in Theory and Practice*, 4th ed.**
- **Author/publisher:** Stefano Gatti, Academic Press (Elsevier).
- **Year/pages:** 24 May 2023; 600 pp. [S34, S35]. **Price:** not shown.
- **Audience:** upper-level undergraduate and graduate students [S34].

Six cases: Cogeneration 1, Italy Water System, Odebrecht Norbe Drilling VIII/IX, Watercraft Capital, Viveracqua Hydrobond, Quezon Power. Appendix on the structure of the simulation model. Instructor resources include Excel files [S35].
- **Note:** One Elsevier page summary described a "hydroelectric" case [S34]. The verbatim TOC shows none: Viveracqua Hydrobond is a water-utility bond [S35].

- **Lacks:** Not hydro, not Africa. No development-stage economics.

- **Author/publisher:** E.R. Yescombe, Academic Press.

**24. *Green Banking*** (ed. Jörg Böttcher)
- **Publisher/year:** De Gruyter Oldenbourg, 2020 (snippet) [S24].
- **Content:** Developing, financing and executing utility-scale renewables, with financing-vehicle chapters on PV, onshore wind and offshore wind (snippet) [S24].
- **Relevance:** Hydro is not mentioned in the snippet. A German or European practitioner lens.

## 4. White-space analysis: what no book does well

These conclusions are analyst judgement, based on the evidence above.

1. **Development-stage economics.** None of the 25 titles gives a development budget by stage (desk study, prefeasibility, feasibility/ESIA, permitting, PPA, financial close), the probability of reaching close, the cost of at-risk capital, or how a developer's premium or development fee is valued and paid at close or on sell-down. IFC 2015 has phases and timelines but no money [S1]. Head 2000 has cost profiles but not development spend [S10]. "Development cost/budget" scored 0 hits in every full-text check.
2. **Quantified stage gates.** IFC gives a checklist [S1], and ESMAP gives a timeline-risk-CAPEX figure [S3]. Nobody turns them into **go/no-go gates with numeric thresholds** (e.g. hydrology record length, tariff headroom against utility avoided cost, DSCR at P90, land and resettlement exposure) tied to how much money is released at each gate.
3. **The African permitting, PPA and financing critical path.** Addleshaw Goddard/IHA lists country laws [S12] and Head covers concessions and solicitation [S10]. No title sequences, for an African IPP, the interlocking path of water-use permit and concession, ESIA approval, implementation agreement, grid connection agreement, PPA, and government support or PRG through to financial-close conditions precedent. No title gives a financial-close CP checklist ("financial close" has 0–1 hits in the hydro guides).
4. **Utility credit risk, quantified.** ESMAP mentions LEGs [S3] and CLDP covers credit enhancement in general [S44]. Neither is hydro-specific or numerical. No title shows how to size liquidity support (months of payment cover) against an African offtaker's arrears, or how hydrology-driven deemed-energy payments add to sovereign or utility exposure.
5. **Transmission interface.** Apart from GEIDCO's master plan for one basin [S30], no title addresses who builds and pays for the evacuation line, line-delay deemed-energy risk, or curtailment, which are recurrent African hydro IPP failure points.
6. **A hydro-specific integrated model with a worked African case.** Hydro books with spreadsheets are engineering-only [S22]. Books with financial models are not hydro [S35, S39, S43]. Commercial hydro templates come with no teaching text and no case [S45]. Nothing links hydrology (P50/P90, dry-year sequences), construction overrun and delay, the PPA's deemed-energy mechanics, DFI debt terms and fiscal or contingent-liability exposure in one documented model.
7. **The government/fiscal lens on hydro IPPs.** Yescombe & Farquharson cover PPP fiscal risk in general [S38]. No hydro title quantifies government contingent liabilities from take-or-pay, deemed energy, hydrological risk sharing and PRGs.
8. **Currency.** The only hydro-specific finance texts are from 2000 [S10], 2015 [S1], 2021 [S12] and 2024 [S3], and the 2024 one is a policy report. No book-length hydro finance text reflects 2023–2026 conditions: interest rates, Chinese lending retreat, climate-resilience requirements [S13] and pumped-storage and hybrid revenue stacks.

## 5. Harsh assessment: what Book 7 must contain to be clearly better

### 5.1 Must-haves (without these, the free IFC and World Bank guides are "good enough")

1. **A development economics chapter with numbers.** Stage-by-stage development budget ranges with sources, time and spend at each gate, probability-weighted development NPV, the developer's development premium or fee at financial close, and equity sell-down valuation. If these numbers are illustrative, say so, and give sourced benchmark ranges.
2. **A stage-gate decision framework.** Numeric gate criteria tied to spend release, delivered as a usable template (a gate scorecard), not prose.
3. **An African hydro IPP critical path.** A sequenced Gantt-style map from water rights and concession through ESIA, IA, PPA, grid connection, government support and lender DD to CPs. Include typical durations and the failure points, sourced from real African projects.
4. **A financial-close CP checklist** specific to hydro: hydrology DD, dam safety panel, resettlement action plan completion, EPC wrap or multi-contract interface, insurance, PRG or LC effectiveness.
5. **A PPA chapter on hydro mechanics,** beyond what Addleshaw Goddard/IHA already gives free: deemed energy, hydrology floors and ceilings, dry-year reserve accounts, grid-unavailability compensation, tariff indexation and currency. **It must quantify the cash effect in the model.**
6. **An offtaker-credit and liquidity-sizing method:** utility financial ratios, arrears history, LC and escrow and LEG sizing, and PRG triggers, linked to the model.
7. **A transmission-interface chapter** covering who builds the line, delay risk allocation and curtailment.
8. **A documented companion model** with hydrology scenarios (P50/P90, drought sequences), construction delay and overrun, DFI and blended debt, DSCR/LLCR sculpting, equity IRR including development premium, and a government contingent-liability view. Each output must trace to a worked chapter.
9. **A worked African case end to end.** A fictional case is acceptable if labelled as such, but it must be calibrated to sourced real benchmarks. Add short real-project boxes (e.g. Bujagali, Nachtigal, Ruzizi III, Kafue Gorge Lower, Bui; each needs sourcing before use).
10. **Recency:** 2024–2026 sources on cost of capital, DFI instruments and climate-resilience requirements [S13].

### 5.3 Claims that probably do hold up, if delivered (analyst judgement; verify before marketing)

- "The first book-length treatment of **hydropower development economics**: development budget, stage gates and development premium, with a companion model."
- "The first **hydro-specific integrated project-finance and fiscal-exposure model** documented in a book, with an end-to-end worked **African IPP** case."
- "The first to **quantify utility credit and liquidity support sizing** for African hydro IPPs inside the model."

**Commercial risk (analyst judgement).** The strongest competitors are **free** (IFC, ESMAP, Head, Addleshaw Goddard/IHA, CLDP, the How-to Guides). A paid book has to beat them on numbers, tools and the model, not on breadth or narrative. The repository's model (35 sheets) is the real differentiator. A book that is just narrative would be seen as a repackaging of IFC 2015 plus Addleshaw Goddard 2021.
