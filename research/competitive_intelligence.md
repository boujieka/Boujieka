# Competitive Intelligence: Bankable Hydro

**Research date:** 2026-10-03
**Product benchmarked:** Bankable Hydro, an integrated package of book, Excel bankability model, manual and course on structuring African hydropower without creating unsustainable public liabilities.

## Methodology

1. I ran web searches and opened product, publisher and institution pages directly where I could. Several marketplaces (eFinancialModels, Flevy, PPIAF, ESMAP, Springer, CNBC) returned HTTP 403 or bot-protection pages to the fetch tool. Facts from those come from search-result snippets only and are marked **(snippet)**.
2. Prices are stated exactly as displayed on the date of access, in the currency shown. Promotional prices are flagged because they change often. If no price was visible, the entry says **Price not publicly listed** (or "not visible on page accessed").
3. Overlap rating with Bankable Hydro: **High** means the product covers the core proposition (hydro + project finance + public-liability or fiscal lens); **Medium** means it covers 2 or more modules; **Low** means it is adjacent only.
4. Gap-matrix ratings: **Yes** if the dimension is a stated, explicit feature or chapter; **Partial** if it is mentioned or generic; **No** if nothing in the reviewed description suggests it. Ratings rest on the public descriptions cited, not on hands-on testing of each product. Where a product is free or open-access, ratings also draw on its published scope.
5. No proprietary text is reproduced. Descriptions are paraphrased.
6. Sections marked "(Analyst inference)" are my judgement.
7. Not verified in this pass: Eloquens hydro listings (none surfaced), Fimarc (no web presence surfaced), Duke/ESMAP hydropower course (none surfaced), Euromoney prices (course pages returned 404), and Hydropower Sustainability Standard assessment fees.

## E. Positioning conclusions (Analyst inference)

1. **The integration gap is the moat.** Each element of Bankable Hydro exists somewhere, often for free: hydro technical guidance (IFC), PF modelling (WSP, Mazars, LCT), PPA design (CLDP), fiscal risk (PFRAM), debt screening (LIC-DSF) and utility viability (UPBEAT, Kojima & Trimble). No reviewed product chains hydrology → tariff → utility ability to pay → contingent liability → debt-sustainability headroom in one model with pass/fail bankability gates.
2. **Free MDB tools are complements, not threats.** PFRAM and LIC-DSF are authoritative but sector-agnostic. Position Bankable Hydro as the hydro-specific front end that produces PFRAM-compatible and DSF-compatible outputs: fiscal payment schedules and contingent-liability values by scenario. Avoid presenting it as a replacement.
3. **Price band.** Templates sell at US$45–499. Generic PF courses run US$499 self-paced to US$3,200–£4,495 live. A book plus specialist model plus course can credibly sit between US$500 and US$3,000 per seat, with institutional or MoF licences above that. This is an inference from the benchmarks only and has not been demand-tested.
4. **Buyer segments that competitors underserve:** ministries of finance and PPP units (PFRAM users who lack sector models), African utilities and regulators (UPBEAT users who lack project links), and DFI and MIGA project teams that need a public-liability screen before guarantees. Commercial courses target bankers and analysts instead [S17][S20][S22].
5. **Credibility levers:** align the E&S gate with the Hydropower Sustainability Standard and HESG [S36][S37]; reuse benchmark cases (NT2, Lao EDL debt, Itaipu Annex C, Belo Monte assured energy) from `case_studies/international_benchmarks.md`; and cite PFRAM, DSF and UPBEAT data conventions.
6. **Risks:** MDBs could add sector modules to PFRAM, or Power Africa or CLDP could publish a hydro-specific handbook. Both would be free, so differentiation must rest on the integrated model, the gate logic and the training.
