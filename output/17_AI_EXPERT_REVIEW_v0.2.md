## Cover note: what this document is, and what it is not

**This is an AI-generated structured review.** It was produced by an AI model reading the chapter sources (`volumes/02-solar-home-systems/book/ch00_front.md` to `ch99_annexes.md`) from four simulated practitioner perspectives, one after the other: (1) CFO of a PAYGo solar company in East Africa; (2) credit officer of a bank or debt fund lending against PAYGo receivables; (3) impact investor or DFI investment officer; (4) financial modeller using MODEL 2.

**It is not a substitute for review by named practitioners.** The perspectives are simulated; no CFO, credit officer, DFI officer or modeller has read the book for this review. The review is not a legal opinion, an accounting opinion, a professional copyedit or a test of the workbook in Microsoft Excel. **Release gate B6 (practitioner review) stays open** until named people have reviewed the text and their comments have been logged and resolved. This log can serve as a starting checklist for them.

* Statements about the default workbook were checked against the recalculated file `scratchpad/lo/s10out/fm.xlsx`, opened with openpyxl in data only mode (cached values, no recalculation). Confirmed: 47 sheets; 38 integrity checks with master check OK; Base investor IRR 46.4%, MOIC 6.7x, peak equity USD 10.0m; Downside (22.9%), 0.3x, 44 breach months; Severe peak equity USD 46.6m, 48 breach months; every row of the 14.3 sensitivity table; Base collection rate 84.5% falling to 73.3%; default hazards, collection rates, repossession rates, advance rates, covenant thresholds and the ECL inputs; 2 of 23 readiness gates met, 13 critical gates, STOP on the DSCR test; tier burdens 13.0%, 13.2% and 11.3%. * Web searches were used only to check terminology and sector practice. Sites holding the primary documents (gogla.org, usaid.gov, afsic.net) were blocked by the network proxy, so **everything taken from the web is a secondary (search snippet) source** and is listed at the end. No claim in this log depends on a document that was not opened, except where the comment says so.

### Severity scale

Critical: wrong or misleading in a way that would change a decision. Major: a practitioner would object. Minor: a correction or clarification. Suggestion: an improvement.

**Result: 50 comments; 0 Critical, 17 Major, 24 Minor, 9 Suggestion.** By perspective: CFO 13, credit officer 17, impact or DFI 12, modeller 8. No Critical finding was raised: the arithmetic holds and the book is candid about its simplifications. The Major findings are mostly about (a) what the model's simplified accounting does to book equity and to some ratios the book reads as conclusions, (b) covenant mechanics in the model that a lender would read differently, and (c) missing practice points (advance rate sizing, consumer protection code, impact metrics).

## The ten most important fixes

1. **Quantify the booked allowance and its effects (R01).** The model's allowance runs at 50% to 54% of gross receivables against an indicative ECL of 15% to 17% at default inputs. Say so in Chapter 8, and flag that book equity, debt to book equity, the price to book exit and ROE all sit on that basis.
2. **Withdraw the conclusion drawn from ECL ÷ financing revenue (R02).** The 1.28x ratio is partly a product of the model's conventions and of the cash price versus premium split; test pricing against losses on the whole contract instead.
3. **Disclose that in Proxy mode PAR30 equals RaR and PAR90 is a fixed share of it (R18).** Three of the seven covenants are one test; the 25% and 18% limits cannot bind first.
4. **State that the model does not act on covenant breaches (R17).** Downside peak equity of USD 10.0m (default) and USD 9.0m (SolaraPay) assumes the facility survives 40 or more breach months; add a draw stop run.
5. **Use one consistent phrase on breaches (R20):** "no monthly covenant breach; the annual DSCR fails in Years 1 to 4". Several chapters still say "no covenant breach" for Base.
6. **Label APRs nominal and add effective rates (R05).** Tier 1 at 106% nominal is about 175% effective; Chapter 3 currently compares nominal with effective figures.
7. **Make the SolaraPay conditions consistent with Chapter 4 (R12, R38).** Condition 5 relies on longer tenors that Chapter 4 shows do not work; condition 2 caps Tiers 4 and 5 at their planned share, which is not a pilot cap.
8. **Explain how lenders size advance rates and fix the logic of 8.6 (R15, R16).** Derive an advance rate from stressed calibrated losses; compare advances with the net carrying amount of the eligible pool.
9. **Recast the 180 DPD default as a lenient model default (R14)** and show the effect of 90 and 120 DPD.
10. **Add the consumer protection and impact references a DFI expects (R30, R31),** naming the GOGLA Consumer Protection Code and an impact metrics framework, with pending source entries until the documents are read.

Next in line: IAS 21 terminology for the USD loan (R03), the over-general claim that currency and pricing beat volume "in this sector" (R36), the collection covenant evidence (R21), the equity cross check on the DCF (R45), and the shortening suggested in R40 and R41.
