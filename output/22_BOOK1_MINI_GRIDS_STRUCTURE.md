## 2. The idea that organises the book

Book 2 rests on one observation: a PAYGo company is a retailer, a utility like service provider and a lender at the same time.

Book 1 rests on a parallel one. **A mini grid developer is a small regulated utility, an infrastructure project and a demand building business at the same time.**

* **The utility** sells energy at a tariff it often does not set alone, to customers it must keep connected and paying.
* **The infrastructure project** spends most of its capital before the first kWh is sold, on assets that last 20 years or more, except for the batteries.
* **The demand builder** earns little until households and businesses use more power, so productive use and appliance finance are part of the business, not an add on.

The unit of analysis is **the site**, and above it **the portfolio of sites**. A single site rarely finances well on its own; the question for an investor is whether a platform can build sites at a falling cost, fill them with demand and fund the gap that tariffs leave.

Three questions run through every chapter:
1. Does the site's revenue cover its operating cost and the replacement of its batteries (operating sustainability)?
2. Who pays for the capital that revenue does not cover, and on what terms (the viability gap)?
3. What happens if the main grid arrives (the terminal risk)?

## 4. MODEL 1: structure

**Reused from the collection's shared engine (already tested in MODEL 2):**
* timeline;
* FX;
* financing;
* the three statements;
* checks and master check;
* scenarios and sensitivity;
* RBF engine;
* readiness gates;
* source register and calibration;
* dashboard;
* the Start and Contents sheets.

**Mini grid domain core (new):**

| Sheet | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Sites | One row per site or site batch: start date, connections planned, customer mix, capital cost, grant, region |
| Build_Schedule | Development, construction and commissioning timing; pipeline attrition |
| Demand | Connections ramp by customer class; kWh per connection by class and season; demand growth |
| Energy_Balance | Simplified monthly balance: PV energy, battery throughput, diesel fill, unserved energy, losses (design inputs come from the engineering study; no hourly dispatch) |
| Tariff | Tariff structure, regulatory cap, cost reflective tariff calculation, indexation and review dates |
| Opex_Assets | Operating cost by type; battery degradation and replacement; reserve account |
| Grid_Arrival | Scenario of grid arrival year per site; interconnection or transfer; compensation formula; terminal value |
| Site_Economics | Levelised cost, break even tariff, viability gap per connection, site IRR |
| Portfolio | Aggregation; overheads; equity and project IRR |

**Design choice for the author (time step).** Assets last 20 years or more.
* **Monthly for 20 years** gives 240 columns. It is consistent with MODEL 2 but heavy.
* **Quarterly for 20 years** gives 80 columns. It is enough for construction, ramp and debt service.
* **Annual** is too coarse for construction draws and the demand ramp.

Recommendation: quarterly for 20 years, with the same check and readiness logic as MODEL 2.
