# Evidence base and its limits

Every figure that appears on screen in these films comes from Chapter 1 of
*Bankable Is Not Enough*, and carries the qualification the chapter itself attaches
to it. This page exists so a facilitator can answer a challenge from the room
without guessing.

The general rule the films follow: **where the chapter footnotes a number as press
reporting rather than a primary document, the on-screen citation says so.**

## The denominator problem

The films state plainly that **the success rate of the MoU route is unknown**.
Databases record projects that reached financial close, are under construction or
are operating. They do not systematically record the MoUs that preceded them, still
less those that went nowhere. Governments rarely publish the MoUs they sign, and
developers have no reason to announce when one lapses.

No figure in these films should be read as a success rate for MoUs. The chapter does
not claim one, and neither do the films.

## Where the films depart from the chapter's layout

One deliberate departure, in the "who gains at signature" scene. The chapter's
Table 1.3 lists five actors: government, developer, utility, lenders and development
partners. The prose immediately after it names **two** parties as the ones who will
carry the risk — the utility *and the treasury*. The treasury is not a row in the
table.

Rather than resolve that silently, the films draw the treasury as a sixth, ghosted
card marked "Not at the table". The absence is the argument.
