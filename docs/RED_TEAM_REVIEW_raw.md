# RED-TEAM REVIEW: Bankable Hydro Integrated Bankability Model (raw)

Reviewer stance: adversarial DFI model auditor / energy-economics examiner.
Scope: `model/build_model.py` (source of truth), `model/Bankable_Hydro_Model.xlsx` (recalculated values), `model/snapshot_results.json` (29 runs), `tools/run_snapshots.py`, `manual/USER_MANUAL.md`, `book/BANKABLE_HYDRO_Manuscript.md`, `research/source_database.md`.
No repository file other than this one was modified. All tests were run on copies in the session scratchpad and recalculated with LibreOffice (`recalc.py`).

Notation: `name` = template name in build_model.py; `Sheet!Cn` = scalar cell; `row name (Sheet rN)` = time-series row; t = period (E..AR = t1..t40). Base = PPP structure 3, base case, P50, debt sculpted, backstop on (as the shipped file). "LOCK" = the runner's locked-debt base (`debt_mode=2`, `lock_m=499.525`).

## 2. Methodological critique (examiner / IMF-WB reviewer view)

1. **P90 definition.** A one-year P90 applied in every year of an 18-year tenor is not what lenders size on. A one-year P90 governs liquidity and DSRA sizing. Sculpting uses a 10-year (or tenor-length) P90, roughly P50·(1−z·CV/√n) under independence, plus a separate multi-year drought sequence. The model conflates the two. In practice the conflation is moot only because the gearing cap binds (M-04), which itself shows the "lender case" machinery is not exercised by the worked example.
2. **Normal approximation and asserted CV.** The CV of 12% is an input, not derived from the flow record. The monthly flow table has no inter-annual dimension at all. Energy is bounded above by installed capacity and design flow, so it is left-skewed. My simulation (annual lognormal CV 12% × monthly lognormal noise CV 20 to 40%) gives an empirical P90/P50 of 0.806 to 0.839 against the model's 0.846, and the gap grows with monthly variability. Gate 1's P90/P50 test is therefore a function of an assumption.
3. **Mean-flow (Jensen) bias.** Energy computed from long-term mean monthly flows overstates expected energy whenever flows are capped by `q_design` (May at 520 m³/s vs 480 capacity incl. e-flow). The same simulation shows a P50 bias of −2.0% / −3.5% / −5.5% for monthly CV 0.2 / 0.3 / 0.4. Every financial result inherits the bias. It is noted in the sheet but never quantified or stressed.
4. **Annual periodicity.** The model has no intra-year dispatch, no seasonal firmness, no peak/off-peak value, and no minimum-load curtailment beyond a single annual MW ratio (`evac_ratio`). Gate 2 (share of peak) and Gate 4 (evacuation) are therefore coarse. The proportional curtailment rule is not conservative for a plant with pondage, contrary to Ms 6.1: pondage lets the plant shift energy into hours with headroom, so the rule *overstates* curtailment. It is conservative for the IPP's revenue only if deemed energy is absent.
5. **Nominal LCOE at 10%.** The model levelises in nominal USD at a 10% "sponsor WACC" while citing IRENA's 12% *real* Africa WACC. The plant and system LCOE (111/134) cannot be compared with IRENA benchmarks or with real-terms tariffs. The tornado is mislabelled "real annuity".
6. **Same PPA tariff across all structures.** Structure comparisons hold the tariff fixed at USD 26/kW-mo + USD 25/MWh. The fiscal NPV ranking (public +416, hybrid −294) and equity IRRs (26% hybrid, 10% IPP) are then mechanical consequences of cost of capital, not of structure merit. A defensible comparison solves, for each structure, the tariff that meets that structure's hurdle (and lender DSCR). It then compares consumer cost (PV of PPA payments) plus taxpayer cost (consolidated fiscal NPV) on a common perimeter. The manuscript's "Rule 7" (tie grants to tariff) is right, but the model does not implement it.
7. **Fiscal perimeter.** The fiscal NPV takes the central-government view and excludes the state utility (C-03). For a vertically integrated, state-owned single buyer this is the wrong perimeter under IMF GFS/PFRAM practice: SOE quasi-fiscal flows are exactly the channel the book warns about. Transmission is on a cash basis (no loan financing option), so the peak-cash Gate 8 test is driven by the 3-year line-build profile (1.9% of revenue in 2029 to 2031, from `g_tx` 78.6 to 82.6/yr). Treating transmission as loan-financed would remove that "development gap".
8. **Probability-based expected loss.** The model takes annual probabilities × exposure × LGD and sums over 30 years with no survival weighting, no correlation between events (a PPA call, a debt call and termination share the same driver: utility distress), and no conditioning on the active scenario. Using 8% p.a. for a PPA call implies an expected 2.4 calls per concession, yet the exposure per call is capped at 12 months. The EL is neither a calibrated credit-risk measure nor consistent with the deterministic calls. Use either a scenario-weighted approach (probabilities on the snapshot scenarios) or PFRAM's stress-test logic.
9. **Utility model as a binding constraint.** "Maximum sustainable PPA payment = all free cash / 1.2" gives 100% of utility free cash to one IPP. It ignores utility network CAPEX (essential with 6% demand growth), taxes, other future IPPs, and the seniority of existing obligations. The waterfall puts the new PPA *junior* to existing debt service and all other supply: in the offtaker stress, the utility pays its other suppliers and lenders in full while running an unfunded −2.8bn/yr deficit. The modelled shortfall is thus partly an artefact of payment priority. A payment-priority input (PPA senior or pari passu with other supply) is needed.
10. **Demand projection.** Six segments compound at 5 to 10% for 40 years (domestic demand rises from 7.3 to 88.7 TWh, peak from 1.45 to 17.6 GW) with committed supply growing 1.5%. This guarantees absorption, kills the low-demand stress (H-03), and drives the phantom-supply problem (C-02). An examiner would ask for logistic or saturating growth, GDP-elasticity links to `gdp_g`, and a supply-expansion plan (least-cost plan) instead of a fixed `sup_g`.
11. **Deterministic, single-path stresses.** The model has no Monte Carlo over hydrology and FX, and no joint distribution between drought, FX and utility distress (they are correlated in SSA: drought → thermal imports → FX pressure → arrears; cf. Zambia 2024). The "Combined" scenario omits drought and the offtaker × FX interaction is additive. At minimum, a correlated "drought + FX + offtaker" case should be shown.
12. **Termination and guarantee mechanics** are simplified to the point of changing results (no subrogation, no cap, no equity value). These are not "documented simplifications" of second order: they decide who bears the loss in every scenario the book discusses.
