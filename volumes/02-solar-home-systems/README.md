# Volume 2 — Solar Home Systems: PAYGo Business & Financial Model

**Central question:** *Can PAYGo solar become profitable and financeable?*

**Core thesis:** a PAYGo SHS company is three businesses in one: a retailer, a utility-like service provider and a consumer-finance company. Its P&L can look profitable while it runs out of cash. That is why the model is built around **customer cohorts and the receivables book**, not around a simple sales P&L.

## Model v0.2: scope

**Product range: MTF capacity Tiers 1–5.** Tier labels follow the ESMAP Multi-Tier Framework capacity attribute only and are indicative (source register S15, to verify).

| Tier | Product (illustrative) | PV / battery | Tenor | Segment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pico solar kit | 10 Wp / 40 Wh | 12 m | Rural, first-time PAYGo |
| 2 | SHS with TV | 80 Wp / 300 Wh | 24 m | Rural / peri-urban households |
| 3 | Large SHS + DC fridge | 300 Wp / 1.5 kWh | 30 m | Peri-urban households, kiosks |
| 4 | Solar inverter + lithium | ~1.2 kWp / 5 kWh | 36 m | Urban households & SMEs with weak grid |
| 5 | Solar inverter + lithium | ~3 kWp / 10 kWh | 48 m | Upper-income households, SMEs, productive use |

**Investment-grade features**
- **Investment_Summary:** one page for an investment committee or lender.
- **Sheet index** on the Cover, with hyperlinks.
- **Glossary** of PAYGo/SHS terms, as implemented in the model.
- **Cohort engine per tier:** collections, active accounts, receivables at risk, recoveries.
- **Per-tier credit and recovery:** default hazard, partial payment, repossession rate and resale value.
- **RBF** by tier, with a verification lag.
- **FX pass-through** of local-currency depreciation into prices of new contracts.
- **Receivables facility:** borrowing base by tier (advance rates); drawn as needed to hold minimum cash (cash sweep); forced repayment if the borrowing base falls.
- **Lender covenants:**
  - monthly tests: trailing-3-month collection rate, receivables at risk, leverage, liquidity;
  - annual DSCR;
  - breach counts.
- **Valuation:**
  - DCF on unlevered free cash flow, with a normalised terminal cash flow (reinvests g × working capital);
  - exit value by EV/EBITDA or price/book;
  - investor IRR and MOIC in USD, with pro-rata participation in equity top-ups.
- **Unit economics by tier:** APR, expected loss, LTV/CAC, payback, unit IRR, unit NPV.
- **Static sensitivity table:** 12 cases, produced by the verified Python twin.
- **13 integrity checks** feeding a master check.

Accounting simplifications are listed in `engine/engine-spec.md` §4.

**What the illustrative case says (inference, to be tested with real data)**
- Equity returns are far more sensitive to **FX depreciation and pricing power** than to sales volume.
- **Tiers 4–5 drive most of the value.** Their LTV/CAC is 12–16x, against 3–5x for Tiers 1–2. They are also the least proven PAYGo segment, so their credit assumptions deserve the hardest scrutiny.
- **Tier 1's implied APR is about 106%.** Expect consumer-protection questions.
- The base collection rate (73–85%) is still above the sector figure of about 62% reported by ESMAP for 2023. Base is therefore an optimistic case.
