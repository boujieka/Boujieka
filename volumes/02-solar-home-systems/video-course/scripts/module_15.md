# Module 15. The investor view: due diligence and the investment memo

Duration: about 8 minutes. Book: Chapter 15. Model sheets: Credit_Input, Vintage_Input, Credit_Portfolio, Vintage_Dashboard, Valuation, Investment_Summary, Investment_Readiness, Covenants, Benchmark_Compare, Calibration, Source_Register. Templates and tools: T01 Investment committee memorandum, T02 Investor due diligence checklist, T06 Loan tape specification and data request, D5 Investment screening scorecard, D6 Investor returns calculator.

## Learning objectives
1. Organise a PAYGo diligence in eight workstreams, starting with credit and the data tape, and recognise the red flags that should pause a deal.
2. Value a business whose free cash flow is negative by design, and express returns in dollars.
3. Turn readiness gates and findings into a memo, conditions precedent and covenants.

## Scene 1. One asset, two contracts
On screen: Title card. One receivables book. Equity: residual claim. Lender: senior claim.

Every earlier module ends at the same decision: whether to invest or lend, and on what terms.

An equity investor in a PAYGo, or pay as you go, company buys a residual claim on a consumer credit book, a distribution network and a hardware margin. A receivables lender buys a senior claim on the same book, protected by an advance rate, eligibility criteria and covenants. The investor asks whether growth will compound into exit value. The lender asks whether, if growth stops tomorrow, the book will repay the facility.

Each needs the other's view. A covenant breach hands control of cash to the bank, and book equity is only as good as the loss allowance.

## Scene 2. Credit and the data tape come first
On screen: Eight workstreams. Credit and data tape highlighted. Commercial, operations, technology, finance, legal, ESG, management.

A full diligence has eight workstreams, and the overlaps are where problems hide.

Credit and the data tape is the core, and it should start first. The team takes an account level loan tape and the monthly portfolio and cohort data. It rebuilds the collection rate, receivables at risk, cohort curves and PAR30, the share of the book more than 30 days past due, and reconciles them to the ledger. The aim is to replace assumed behaviour with observed behaviour. In the SolaraPay case, a fictional company with synthetic history, recalibration on 24 months of history raised the Tier 1 to 3 default hazards by 30 per cent and cut the investor return from 33.1 to 29.0 per cent.

The commercial workstream produces the volume path and product mix. Scrutinise any shift towards larger systems, because mix moves value sharply on little evidence.

## Scene 3. Operations, platform and accounts
On screen: Agents: clawbacks, concentration, fraud. Platform: unlock tokens and audit trail. Accounts: IFRS 9 staging at 30 and 90 days past due.

Agents originate the credit. So diligence covers clawbacks on early default, concentration by agent and region, and controls against ghost sales and recycled devices. It also checks that repossession actually happens, because modelled recoveries need a real process.

The lockout platform is the collateral. Test who can issue unlock tokens, with what audit trail, and reconcile unlocks to payments. An unlock without full payment is a settlement, a goodwill gesture or a control failure.

Finance and accounting tests revenue recognition and the loss allowance. Under IFRS 9, the international accounting standard for financial instruments, check the staging. Stage 2 follows a significant increase in credit risk, presumed at 30 days past due. Stage 3 follows default, presumed at 90. An allowance that stays flat while PAR30 rises is a finding in its own right.

Legal work maps licences against the rules, confirmed with local counsel. Environmental, social and governance work covers payment burden and the conduct of collections. Management diligence asks whether the credit function can slow sales.

## Scene 4. The data request and the red flags
On screen: Template T06 data request. Red flags: buckets that do not reconcile, deposits in the collection rate, re ageing, unexplained unlocks, flat allowance, high recoveries, concentration, cases that need RBF.

Issue the data request on day one. The minimum is the account level tape, 24 months of portfolio data by tier in the structure of Credit_Input, cohort observations in the structure of Vintage_Input, ledger reconciliations, unlock logs and every financing and subsidy agreement. Template T06 sets this out.

Some findings should pause a deal until explained. Days past due buckets that do not add up to gross receivables. A collection rate that includes deposits without saying so. Restructurings that reset days past due, especially just before a covenant date. Unlocks without matching payment. A loss allowance that does not move with PAR30. And an investment case that clears the hurdle only with subsidy income, a richer mix of untested tiers or a generous exit multiple.

One flag outweighs the rest. A team that cannot produce its cohort data within two weeks is unlikely to be managing to it.

## Scene 5. Valuing a business with negative free cash flow
On screen: Terminal value formula on normalised cash flow. Illustration: 1.1 times 1.05, less 0.05 times 6.0, divided by 0.17, equals about 5.03 billion.

Free cash flow is EBITDA, meaning earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, less tax, capex and the increase in working capital. Here working capital is mostly the receivables book, so while it grows, free cash flow is negative and value sits in the terminal year.

The workbook reinvests only what the book needs to grow at the long run rate. Take an illustration. Year 5 EBITDA is one point five billion local currency units, less tax of three hundred million and capex of one hundred million, leaving one point one billion. Working capital is six billion, growth is 5 per cent and the discount rate is 22 per cent. One point one times 1.05 is 1.155. Take away 5 per cent of six, which is 0.3, and you have 0.855. Divided by 0.17, that gives a terminal value of about five point zero three billion. On raw Year 5 free cash flow it would have been negative.

Even normalised, the discounted cash flow, or DCF, understates a growth book. In the SolaraPay case it gives six point one million dollars at 22 per cent, below the eight million dollar pre money valuation.

## Scene 6. Exit, book value and dollar returns
On screen: Valuation sheet. Exit at 4.0 times EBITDA: IRR 46.4 falls to 33.9 per cent. Depreciation over five years: 5 per cent leaves 78.4 per cent, 12 per cent leaves 56.7 per cent, 25 per cent leaves 32.8 per cent.

The exit valuation applies a multiple of EBITDA and deducts net debt. In the default model, exiting at 4.0 times EBITDA instead of the base multiple lowers the investor internal rate of return, or IRR, from 46.4 to 33.9 per cent.

Price to book treats the company as a lender, so it is only as reliable as the loss allowance.

Dollar investors are judged in dollars. For SolaraPay, four million dollars at eight million pre money buys 33.3 per cent. A third of exit equity of forty two point nine million dollars is fourteen point three million, a multiple of about 3.6 times and an IRR of about 29.0 per cent. Currency deserves its own line in the memo. Over five years, depreciation of 5 per cent a year leaves 78.4 per cent of the value, 12 per cent leaves 56.7 per cent, and 25 per cent leaves 32.8 per cent.

## Scene 7. Gates, the memo and conditions
On screen: Investment_Readiness banner: "3/23 gates met. Decision: STOP (Failed test: No covenant breach in the active scenario)". Template T01, twelve sections, recommendation first.

The Investment_Readiness sheet lists 23 gates, some automatic and some set on external evidence. A manual gate counts only when the sheet records where the evidence is held and who signed it off. Thirteen gates are critical, and the sheet applies a fixed rule. STOP when a test fails: the master check, a covenant breach in any month or a year with the debt service coverage ratio below its minimum, or a tier with negative contribution. GO only when all 23 gates are met. CONDITIONAL GO when every critical gate is met, with the open gates as conditions. Otherwise STOP, because the evidence is incomplete. A STOP on incomplete evidence is not a verdict on the business, but a STOP on a failed test is a finding against the plan as modelled. A GO is not an investment recommendation. At default inputs two gates are met and the rule reads STOP on a failed test: no month breaches a covenant, but the annual DSCR sits below 1.20 times in Years 1 to 4. In the SolaraPay case four gates are met, and the rule reads STOP for the same reason.

The memo in template T01 follows twelve sections, with the recommendation and its conditions first. Show the DCF and exit values side by side.

Write changes as instruments, not hopes. Conditions precedent fix what must be true before money moves, such as a reconciled tape or an independent review of expected credit loss, or ECL. Covenants fix what must stay true. Prefer portfolio covenants to a debt service coverage test during growth, set them at levels the history supports, and add a cure period. An unmet gate is often a condition to attach.

## Scene 8. Benchmarks and their limits
On screen: Benchmark_Compare, Calibration and Source_Register. Grades A to D, status recorded separately. M-KOPA comparison withdrawn until the group's consolidated accounts are read.

Benchmarks are a sanity check, not a target. The workbook grades each source from A, for a primary official or audited document, to D, for unverified material, and records separately whether the claim has been verified. Calibration uses a reference only once its source is verified.

M-KOPA shows why that discipline matters. Two outlets reported FY2024 group revenue figures that differ by a factor of about 1.64. The only filing held is that of M-KOPA UK LIMITED, a subsidiary, which says nothing about the group's PAYGo business. So the comparison is withdrawn until the group's consolidated accounts are read, and neither revenue figure is used as a benchmark. The ESMAP Off-Grid Solar Market Trends Report 2024 is reported to give an average PAYGo collection rate of about 62 per cent for 2021 to 2023. It is pending the primary document, and it is a collection rate, not a repayment rate. The benchmark that matters most is the company's own cohort history.

## Scene 9. Recap and exercise
On screen: Recap. Credit first. Red flags pause the deal. Value on normalised cash flow and in dollars. Gates become conditions. Exercise: D6 and T01.

Start with credit and the data tape, and let the red flags stop a deal until they are explained. Value a growing book on normalised cash flow, read the DCF and exit values together, and translate returns into dollars. Turn every unmet readiness gate into a condition precedent, a covenant or an accepted risk.

Your exercise uses the D6 Investor returns calculator, which opens on the SolaraPay calibrated Base at 3.57 times and 29.0 per cent. Raise the depreciation rate from the Base path to the Downside rate of 12 per cent a year and record the new IRR. Then use the solver to find the highest pre money valuation that meets your fund's hurdle rate. Write both results into section 8 of the T01 memo, with one sentence on what the entry price assumes.
