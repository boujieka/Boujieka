THE MoU TRAP
Nigeria's 14 solar PPAs, 2016–2019
1
A BIG ANNOUNCEMENT (2016)

Nigeria's bulk electricity trader signed power purchase agreements (PPAs) with fourteen solar developers.

14 solar PPAs
50–100 MW each
1,125 MW total
11.5 US¢/kWh for 20 years
2
EARLY MOMENTUM

The buyer's acting head set a target of 18 months for first power. Ten of the fourteen developers paid development security bonds to show their commitment.

18 months to first power
10 / 14 developers paid bonds
3
THREE YEARS LATER (2019)

None of the fourteen projects reached financial close. Solar prices had fallen, and the government now wanted to cut the tariff to around 7.5 cents per kWh.

11.5→7.5 US¢/kWh
NO FINANCIAL CLOSE
4
KEY SUPPORTS NOT IN PLACE

The put and call option agreements that lenders expected, and the partial risk guarantees from the World Bank and the African Development Bank, were not concluded for most projects.

✗ Put & call options
✗ Partial risk guarantees
5
WHY IT STALLED
FACTOR 1

Tariff change

Government wanted to reduce the tariff to 7.5 ¢/kWh.

FACTOR 2

Missing support

Put and call options and guarantees not concluded.

FACTOR 3

System constraints

The grid could not readily absorb the power.

FACTOR 4

Fiscal concerns

Lenders were uneasy about long-term state obligations.

6
THE MoU TRAP

Projects stop at the moment when a signed commitment has to become a financeable one, and somebody has to answer the questions that nobody asked at signature.

MoU / PPA signed
Political commitment
Expectations created
Missing key tests
7
TWO POSSIBLE EXITS

Once signed, the commitment eventually leaves the trap by one of two exits.

EXIT 1 · Stall

The country loses time, money and options.

EXIT 2 · Close on weak terms

The country inherits a liability.

8
THE REAL COST

Whether it stalls or closes on weak terms, the MoU trap is costly.

1Lost time
2Wasted resources
3Fewer options for better projects
4Long-term public liabilities
9
THE WAY FORWARD

Before signing, apply two key tests:

1. THE LENDER'S TEST
Can the project repay its debt?
  • Realistic tariff and demand
  • Bankable risk allocation
  • Support instruments in place
2. THE STATE'S TEST
Can the power system and public finances carry the obligations for 20 years or more?
  • Grid and system readiness
  • Fiscal affordability
  • Value for money
  • Sustainable long-term impact
