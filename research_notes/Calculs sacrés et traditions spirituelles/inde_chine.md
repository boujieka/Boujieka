## Q1. What do primary sources attest, and when? (dating debates)

### Takeaway
The best-dated "sacred calculation" material is Indian ritual geometry (Śulba Sūtras, roughly 800–200 BCE, with the oldest c. 800–500 BCE per Plofker) and the Chinese sexagenary day count (Shang oracle bones, c. 1100 BCE; unbroken since at least 722 BCE). By contrast, the Luoshu as an explicit 3x3 magic square is only clearly attested by c. 80 CE, with an explicit example from 570 CE. The canonical Hetu/Luoshu dot diagrams come down only from Song-dynasty (10th–12th c. CE) versions.

### Cited Findings

**India: Śulba Sūtras (altar geometry)**
- Plofker dates the Baudhāyana, Mānava and Āpastamba Śulba Sūtras to roughly 800–500 BCE. She puts Kātyāyana after Pāṇini, probably in the mid-4th century BCE. Pingree's relative order is Baudhāyana, Āpastamba, then Kātyāyana and Mānava. — [Wikipedia, Shulba Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulba_Sutras)
- The language is late Vedic Sanskrit, which points to the 1st millennium BCE. Boyer gives a wide range from the 8th century BCE to the 2nd century CE. Sundararāja's commentary on Āpastamba dates to the late 15th century CE. — [Wikipedia, Shulba Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulba_Sutras)
- The "Pythagorean" statement in Baudhāyana 1.9 reads: "The diagonal of a square produces double the area [of the square]." The general rule for rectangles is also stated. Baudhāyana 1.13 lists rectangles with sides 3 & 4, 12 & 5, and 15 & 8. — [Wikipedia, Shulba Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulba_Sutras)
- Āpastamba's right-angle constructions use the triples (3,4,5), (5,12,13), (8,15,17) and (12,35,37). — [Wikipedia, Shulba Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulba_Sutras)
- For √2, Baudhāyana 2.12 gives the diagonal of a square as 1 + 1/3 + 1/(3·4) − 1/(3·4·34) = 577/408 ≈ 1.414216, correct to about 5 decimals. Bürk thought this implied awareness of irrationality; Heath said there was no real evidence of that. — [Wikipedia, Shulba Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulba_Sutras)
- On circle-squaring, Baudhāyana 2.9 turns a square into a circle and 2.10 turns a circle into a square ("the diameter is divided into eight parts…"). These imply π ≈ 3.088; the rule in 2.11 implies π ≈ 3.004. — [Wikipedia, Shulba Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulba_Sutras)
- Scholars disagree on origins. Seidenberg argued for a ritual origin of mathematics, possibly with a Mesopotamian source. Van der Waerden placed the authors before 600 BCE and independent of Greek geometry. Staal proposed a common ritual origin for Indian and Greek geometry. Boyer noted possible Mesopotamian influence. — [Wikipedia, Shulba Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulba_Sutras)

**India: Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa (ritual-calendar astronomy)**
- The extant text, attributed to Lagadha, is usually dated to the last centuries BCE, though its tradition may go back to c. 700–600 BCE. Some scholars date it to 1400–1200 BCE because of its winter-solstice position; Sastry and Kochhar proposed 1370–1150 BCE. Witzel puts it in the last centuries BCE on linguistic grounds and cites resemblances to Babylonian works. Chakraverti dates composition after 700 BCE, with the solstice data fitting c. 1400 BCE. Pingree dates the solstice to c. 1180 BCE but says its bearing on the text's date is unclear. — [Wikipedia, Vedanga Jyotisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanga_Jyotisha)
- Its calendar is a 5-year "yuga" beginning at new moon at Dhaniṣṭhā on the winter solstice. It has 62 months, 2 of them intercalary. One tithi is dropped every 61 days and an extra nakṣatra is added every 3,279 days. Months are amānta (ending at new moon). — [Wikipedia, Vedanga Jyotisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanga_Jyotisha)

**India: cosmic time cycles (yugas)**
- One mahāyuga (caturyuga) is 12,000 divine years, or 4,320,000 human years at 360 human years per divine year. Its four parts are Kṛta 1,728,000, Tretā 1,296,000, Dvāpara 864,000 and Kali 432,000 years, in the ratio 4:3:2:1. Each yuga has a dawn and a dusk of one-tenth its length. — [Wikipedia, Yuga cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_cycle)
- A kalpa (one day of Brahmā) is 1,000 mahāyugas, or 4.32 billion years. A manvantara is 71 mahāyugas, or 306,720,000 years. — [Wikipedia, Yuga cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_cycle)
- Primary sources are the Manusmṛti 1.67–71 (yuga lengths in divine years), Mahābhārata 12.231 (same durations, 12,000-year cycle), the Sūrya Siddhānta ch. 1 ("Twelve thousand of these divine years are denominated a Quadruple Age") and the Purāṇas, including the Bhāgavata. — [Wikipedia, Yuga cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_cycle)
- Hindu astronomers place the start of the Kali Yuga at midnight between 17 and 18 February 3102 BCE. One theory derives the yuga names from dice throws marked 4-3-2-1. — [Wikipedia, Yuga cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_cycle)

**India: kaṭapayādi letter-numerals**
- Rules: the consonant groups beginning with ka, ṭa, pa and ya give the digits 1–9. Vowels, ña and na give 0. In a conjunct only the last consonant counts. The digits are read in reverse order. — [Wikipedia, Katapayadi system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katapayadi_system)
- The oldest available evidence is Haridatta's *Grahacāraṇibandhana* (683 CE). Vararuci's *Candra-vākyāni*, traditionally 4th century CE, would put the origin in the early 1st millennium CE. Use is concentrated in South India, especially Kerala (e.g. Mādhava's sine table). — [Wikipedia, Katapayadi system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katapayadi_system)
- Later uses include the melakarta rāga numbering (e.g. Dhīraśaṅkarābharaṇam: dha=9, ra=2 → 92 → reversed 29) and chronograms. In the Nārāyaṇīyam, "āyurārogyasaukhyam" = 1712210, read as days since the start of the Kali Yuga, giving the date of completion. — [Wikipedia, Katapayadi system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katapayadi_system)

**India: Buddhist and Jain large numbers**
- Asaṃkhyeya ("incalculable") is given as 10^140 or, depending on the Avataṃsaka translation, 10^(5·2^103) (Buddhabhadra), 10^(7·2^103) (Śikṣānanda) or 10^(10·2^104) (Cleary; possible error, citation needed). Śākyamuni's practice is said to have lasted 4 asaṃkhyeya kalpas, Kassapa's 8 and Dīpaṅkara's 16. — [Wikipedia, Asaṃkhyeya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asa%E1%B9%83khyeya)
- Jain palyopama is defined by a thought experiment: an 8×8×8-mile pit packed with the hairs of a seven-day-old newborn, one hair removed every 100 years; the time to empty it is one palyopama. One sāgaropama is 10 quadrillion palyopamas. Śīrṣaprahelikā is called "the highest measurable number in Jainism." — [Wikipedia, Jain cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_cosmology)
  - Caution: that page gives the figures as "10^194" without derivation, and its rajju/rajlok conversion is internally inconsistent. Treat its numbers as unreliable until checked against a specialist source.
- The Jain time wheel has a descending half (avasarpiṇī) and an ascending half (utsarpiṇī), each of 6 aras. In the descending half the first ara lasts 400 trillion sāgaropamas and the last two 21,000 years each. — [Wikipedia, Jain cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_cosmology)

**China: Yijing**
- The *Zhou Yi* core text is dated "from about 1100 BC" (some say the 9th c. BCE). Plastromancy (turtle-shell crack divination, the context of the oldest Chinese writing) predates it by hundreds of years. The Yijing was fused with cosmology in the Han (206 BCE–220 CE). Jing Fang's method dates to 78–37 BCE, and Shao Yong to the 11th c. CE. — [Wikipedia, I Ching divination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Ching_divination); [Wikipedia, Binary number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_number)
- The SEP describes the Yijing as a Confucian classic with three textual layers, whose philosophy was "embedded in the practices of divination." — [SEP, Chinese Philosophy of Change (Yijing)](https://plato.stanford.edu/Entries/chinese-change/) (search-result summary only; full entry not read)

**China: Luoshu and Hetu**
- The early records (c. 650 BCE) are ambiguous and refer to a "river map." Sources "clearly start to refer to a magic square by 80 CE, and explicitly give an example of one since 570 CE" (citing Cammann and Swaney). — [Wikipedia, Luoshu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoshu_Square)
- Received Hetu/Luoshu diagrams go back only to the Song dynasty; according to Legge, the earliest versions are lost. The Song lineage runs from Chen Tuan to Liu Mu, with a separate branch to Shao Yong. Zhu Xi adopted Shao Yong's forms for his Yijing commentary. Liu Mu's two charts reverse the attribution used by Shao Yong. — [WebSearch summary of Wikipedia Luoshu / Yellow River Map pages](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River_Map)
- The Luoshu number arrangement matches the *taiyi jiugong zhanpan* (Great Unity Nine Palaces prognostication board), which that source dates to the Warring States. This would make the arrangement pre-Song. — [WebSearch summary, Yellow River Map/Luoshu pages](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoshu)
- Structure: every row, column and diagonal sums to 15, and opposite cells around the central 5 sum to 10. It is the unique normal magic square of order 3. 1 is at the bottom (north) and 9 at the top. — [Wikipedia, Luoshu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoshu_Square)

**China: Ganzhi sexagenary cycle and wuxing**
- The cycle is attested for recording days "from the earliest written records in China" (oracle bones, c. 1100 BCE). Almost all dates in the *Chunqiu* (722–481 BCE) use it, and recorded eclipses show the day count has been unbroken since then. — [Wikipedia, Sexagenary cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexagenary_cycle)
- Use for years began around the mid-3rd century BCE. The earliest documents are from Mawangdui tomb 3 (sealed 168 BCE), and year-counting became widespread in Western Han administration. 10 stems × 12 branches give 60 (the LCM), not 120; this is "traditionally explained" by pairing stems and branches of the same yin/yang polarity. — [Wikipedia, Sexagenary cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexagenary_cycle)
- Wuxing "was in use before but was formulated in the second or first century BCE during the Han dynasty." Mawangdui texts (before 168 BCE) link it to the Five Virtues. Zou Yan (c. 305–240 BCE) tied the succession of phases to dynastic change, and "From the Qin dynasty onward, most Chinese dynasties invoked" this succession to justify their rule. — [Wikipedia, Wuxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxing_(Chinese_philosophy))
- The generating cycle is Wood→Fire→Earth→Metal→Water; the overcoming cycle is Wood→Earth→Water→Fire→Metal. Correlations extend to colors, directions, seasons, planets, pitches, organs, tastes and emotions ("a metaphysics based on cosmic analogy"). Sivin (1987) proposed "five phases"; "five agents/transformations" is preferred over "elements." — [Wikipedia, Wuxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxing_(Chinese_philosophy))

### Inferences
- A safe chronology for the report: ritual geometry (India) and ritual day-counting (China) are early and firmly attested. Grand numerological systems (yuga arithmetic, wuxing correlations, Luoshu square, Hetu/Luoshu diagrams) are mostly codified late, between the Han and Song for China and the Epic/Purāṇic/Siddhānta period for India. Projecting them back to "high antiquity" is a traditional claim, not a historical finding.

### Gaps
- I did not reach Swetz's *Legacy of the Luoshu* or Cammann's 1960–61 articles directly. Several things often cited to them remain unchecked: the Da Dai Liji "Mingtang" chapter numbers (2-9-4 / 7-5-3 / 6-1-8), Zhen Luan's commentary on the *Shushu jiyi* (6th c., probably the "570 CE" source), the 1977 Fuyang (Shuanggudui) *taiyi jiugong* board from the tomb of the Marquis of Ruyin (c. 165 BCE), and Yang Hui's 1275 magic squares.
- The Xici (Great Commentary) passage on the "Great Expansion" (dayan) number 50/49 is the classical source of the yarrow method but was not checked in a primary source this session.
- Aryabhaṭa's equal-yuga scheme (Āryabhaṭīya, 499 CE) was not covered by the sources fetched.
- Dates for Hongfan (Shangshu) as the earliest list of five xing, and for Dong Zhongshu's *Chunqiu fanlu*, were not verified.

## Q2. What spiritual utility was claimed? (ritual efficacy, harmony with the cosmos, divination)

### Takeaway
In India, exact calculation was claimed to make ritual effective. An altar of the right shape and area got the sacrificer the boon he wanted, and correct timing (jyotiṣa) made the rite valid. In China, number was a means of reading and aligning with the patterns of Heaven: yarrow divination, the sexagenary calendar, wuxing succession legitimating dynasties, and Luoshu/bagua orientation in geomancy.

### Cited Findings
- The shape of an altar was tied to the gift the sacrificer sought; Plofker quotes "he who desires heaven is to construct a fire-altar in the form of a falcon" (śyenaciti). — [Wikipedia, Shulba Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shulba_Sutras)
- Yarrow procedure: of 50 stalks, one "is set aside at the beginning and takes no further part." The 49 are split into two piles, one stalk is taken out, and the piles are counted off by fours. The remainders are set aside, and this is done three times per line. Remainders score 2 (9 or 8) or 3 (5 or 4), and the sum is 6, 7, 8 or 9. Six lines are built this way. — [Wikipedia, I Ching divination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Ching_divination)
- Probabilities: yarrow gives 6 (old yin) 1/16, 7 (young yang) 5/16, 8 (young yin) 7/16, 9 (old yang) 3/16. Three coins give 2/16, 6/16, 6/16, 2/16. The coin method came into use "over a thousand years after the yarrow-stalk method." The two methods therefore differ in how often lines change: yang lines change more often than yin with yarrow (3/8 vs 1/8); with coins both change at 1/4. — [Wikipedia, I Ching divination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Ching_divination)
- The SEP describes trigrams and hexagrams as symbols of interacting yin and yang, within an organismic cosmos "without beginning or end" and the "three potencies" of heaven, earth and humankind. — [SEP, Chinese Philosophy of Change](https://plato.stanford.edu/Entries/chinese-change/) (search summary)
- Wuxing gave political legitimacy: dynasties claimed a phase, Heaven's sanction showed in signs matching the dynasty's ritual colors, and from the Qin on most dynasties invoked the theory. — [Wikipedia, Wuxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxing_(Chinese_philosophy))
- The Luoshu is "an important emblem in Feng Shui" and is used with the bagua in geomancy. In the Later Heaven arrangement, 1 and 9 correspond to Kǎn (water) and Lí (fire); in the Early Heaven arrangement, to Kūn and Qián. The page's claim that it was the basis for city, tomb and temple planning is tagged [citation needed]. — [Wikipedia, Luoshu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoshu_Square)
- Legend: in Yu's time, after sacrifices to the Luo river god, "a magical turtle emerged from the water with the curiously unnatural Lo Shu pattern on its shell" (Cammann 1960). — [Wikipedia, Lo Shu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo_Shu_Square)
- The Śrī Yantra is the central object of devotion in Śrīvidyā. Its geometry is 9 interlocking triangles (4 pointing up = Śiva, 5 pointing down = Śakti) forming 43 small triangles, a central bindu, rings of 8 and 16 lotus petals, and a square enclosure (bhūpura) with four gates. Each of its nine levels corresponds to a mudrā, a yoginī and an aspect of Tripurasundarī. It is worshipped with the 15-syllable Pañcadaśākṣarī mantra. — [Wikipedia, Sri Yantra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Yantra)
- Buddhist reading of 108 (Theravāda gloss): 6 senses × 3 feelings × 2 (internal/external) × 3 times = 108, "all the possible sensory states" (Bhante Gunaratana). In Japan, temple bells ring 108 times for the 108 bonnō (defilements), uncited on the page. The Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra has Mahāmati ask 108 questions. Tibetan malas usually have 108 beads (Gyatrul Rinpoche). — [Wikipedia, 108 (number)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/108_(number))
- Hindu uses of 108: 108 Upaniṣads in the Muktikā canon, 108 Divya Desams, 108-bead malas (Śaiva/Liṅgāyat), mostly uncited on the page (it carries a "needs more citations" banner, Oct 2025). — [Wikipedia, 108 (number)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/108_(number))
- Leibniz read binary theologically: he saw 1 and 0 as an image of *creatio ex nihilo* and told the Duke of Brunswick that creation "ex nihilo through God's almighty power" was hard to convey to pagans. — [Wikipedia, Binary number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_number)

### Inferences
- The two cultures had different "utility logics." Indian ritual mathematics was performative: exactness of form and time guaranteed the efficacy of the rite. Chinese cosmological number was mostly hermeneutic and political: numbers let one read change and align rulers with Heaven. Both put number at the junction of ritual and cosmos.
- Several standard explanations of 108 are combinatorial or calendrical (Buddhist 6×3×2×3; 27 nakṣatras × 4 pādas = 108, mentioned by Kak). That suggests 108 served as a "totality number" built up by multiplication, not a single measured constant.

### Gaps
- No source fetched documents muhūrta (traditionally 30 per day of about 48 minutes each) or the 27/28-nakṣatra lists with primary citations. These are standard but unverified here.
- I found no reliable source on 1008 (e.g. 1008 names, sahasranāma rounding). It needs a source.
- The SEP Yijing entry and Richard Smith's work on the Yijing and the imperial calendar (the Astronomical Bureau, the Mandate of Heaven and calendar promulgation) were not read. The imperial-calendar and Mandate of Heaven points cannot be cited from this session.

## Q3. Which popular claims are unverified or overstated?

### Takeaway
The main overstatements are: (1) 108 as an encoded sun–earth distance ratio; (2) a high-antiquity (Yu/Fuxi) origin for the Luoshu magic square and Hetu diagrams; (3) the Yijing as "invented binary" or as Leibniz's source; (4) extreme dates for the Śrī Yantra; (5) unsourced Jain or Buddhist "exact" magnitudes and some attributions of kaṭapayādi π verses.

### Cited Findings
- On 108 and the Sun: the modern mean Earth–Sun distance is about 107.5 solar diameters, and the Sun-to-Earth diameter ratio is about 109. Subhash Kak argues 108 may reflect this, possibly found by naked-eye comparison with a pole placed 108 heights away. He admits naked-eye accuracy is only 1–2%, that there is no evidence ancient texts knew the Sun's true size, and that the Vedic ṛṣis probably did not know the diameter ratio. — [Kak, arXiv 0902.4850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.4850); [Kak, arXiv 0903.3252](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.3252); [Kak, Time, Space and Structure (LSU)](https://www.ece.lsu.edu/kak/Time2%2Epdf)
- Ancient Indian distance estimates do not match the modern ratio. The Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa puts the heavens at 1,000 Earth diameters, implying the Sun at about 500. — [Kak, Early Theories on the Distance to the Sun, arXiv physics/9804021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/9804021)
- Kak also concedes that Sāyaṇa's speed-of-light-like value could be dismissed as coincidence unless a wider framework of ideas explains it. — [Kak, arXiv physics/9804020v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/9804020v2)
- On the antiquity of the Luoshu: there is ambiguity around 650 BCE, a clear magic square only by c. 80 CE, and an explicit example from 570 CE. — [Wikipedia, Luoshu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoshu_Square). The received Hetu/Luoshu diagrams are Song-era. — [Yellow River Map (search summary)](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River_Map)
- On the Yijing as binary: Shao Yong (1011–1077) arranged the hexagrams in a binary-like order but "did not intend his arrangement to be used mathematically." Leibniz's first binary treatise, "On the Binary Progression," dates to 1679, before his exchange with Bouvet (1700–01). He published the *Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire* in 1703. Thomas Harriot had worked out binary earlier. Bouvet argued in 1701 that the Yijing was a parallel invention of binary. That was the two men's own reading, not a neutral historical finding. — [Wikipedia, Binary number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_number); [Wikipedia, Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz)
- Piṅgala (c. 2nd c. BCE) used a binary-like scheme of short and long syllables for Sanskrit prosody, though not positional notation in the modern sense. — [Wikipedia, Binary number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_number)
- On the Śrī Yantra: the claim that the earliest yantras date to 11,000–10,000 BCE, and the attribution of the first drawings to Ādi Śaṅkara, are unsourced (citation-needed tag). Kak (2009) links the yantra to the Ṛgvedic Śrī Sūkta, which is a disputed interpretation. — [Wikipedia, Sri Yantra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Yantra)
- Some claims about 108 are themselves unsourced, e.g. "Sri Yantra contains 108 points" (cited only to a yoga institute) and the Japanese bell explanation. — [Wikipedia, 108 (number)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/108_(number))
- On kaṭapayādi π verses: the *Sadratnamālā* verse is said to encode π to 17 decimals in one place and 31 in another, so the Wikipedia page is inconsistent. One verse also breaks the reversed-digit rule. — [Wikipedia, Katapayadi system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katapayadi_system)
- Precession: Sri Yukteswar's 24,000-year yuga cycle (*The Holy Science*, 1894) is a modern reinterpretation, not an ancient one. It treats 432,000 as an error, puts the cycle's low point at 499 CE and ties the cycle to a precession period of about 24,000 years (versus the traditional 25,920). — [Wikipedia, Yuga cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_cycle)
- The Jain cosmology page's quantifications (palyopama "at least 10^194 years," śīrṣaprahelikā "10^194") are given without derivation, and a unit conversion on the same page is internally wrong. — [Wikipedia, Jain cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_cosmology)

### Inferences
- In the report, keep three separate tiers:
  - Verified: Śulba Sūtras √2 = 577/408 and the triples; the Manusmṛti/Sūrya Siddhānta yuga arithmetic as textual facts; the Luoshu sum of 15; yarrow probabilities 1/16, 5/16, 7/16, 3/16; ganzhi day count unbroken since 722 BCE; Leibniz binary 1679/1703.
  - Traditional interpretation: altar shape = boon; Yu's turtle and the Luoshu; Fuxi's trigrams; wuxing dynastic legitimacy; 108 as totality of defilements.
  - Unverifiable or speculative: 108 = Sun distance ratio; 10,000 BCE Śrī Yantra; Yijing as "the origin of binary"; high-antiquity Hetu/Luoshu diagrams.
- The 432 / 4,320,000 numbers are often linked by modern writers to precession (25,920 = 60 × 432). This is a modern numerological association, not attested in the ancient sources reviewed.

### Gaps
- No direct scholarly rebuttal of the 108 Sun-distance claim was found. The cautions come from its own proponent (Kak). A neutral astronomy-history source would strengthen the report.
- The claim that the "570 CE" explicit Luoshu is Zhen Luan's commentary, and the Fuyang board's exact date, need Swetz or Cammann to confirm.
- Kulaichev's and others' analyses of Śrī Yantra geometric precision (concurrency of lines, "optimal" constructions) were not found in the sources fetched.
- Lalitavistara's tallakṣaṇa (often given as 10^53) and the Abhidharmakośa kalpa similes (mustard-seed city, rock wiped by silk) were not verified.
