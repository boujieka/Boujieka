## 1. What do the primary sources (tablets, papyri, temple texts) actually attest?

### Takeaway
Mesopotamian cuneiform sources attest several things:
- a numerical ranking of the great gods (Anu 60, Enlil 50, Ea 40, Sin 30, Shamash 20, Ishtar 15, Adad 6);
- a huge celestial-omen corpus, *Enūma Anu Enlil* (68 or 70 tablets, about 6,500–7,000 omens);
- the astronomical compendium MUL.APIN, which divides the sky into "paths" of Anu, Enlil and Ea and uses an ideal 360-day year.

Egyptian sources attest several things:
- practical scribal mathematics (the Rhind Papyrus, c. 1550 BCE, copying a Twelfth-Dynasty original) with unit fractions, heqat sub-measures and *seked* slopes;
- a 365-day civil year made of 36 ten-day decans plus 5 extra days;
- funerary texts with 42 judges.

The sources do not attest a planetary seven-day week in Babylon. They also do not attest the golden ratio, or π as a design principle, in the pyramids.

### Cited Findings

**Mesopotamia: numbers of the gods**
- [VERIFIED/SEARCH-SNIPPET] The Metropolitan Museum essay "Mesopotamian Deities" (Ira Spar, 2009) gives the scheme Anu = 60, Enlil = 50, Ea = 40, Sin (moon) = 30, Shamash (sun) = 20, Ishtar = 15, Adad (storm) = 6. It dates the system to the 2nd millennium BCE, when "Babylonian theologians" ranked the major gods in a hierarchical numerical order. — [Met Museum, Mesopotamian Deities](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/mesopotamian-deities)
  - Caveat: the page returned HTTP 429 when fetched directly. The numbers above come from the search summary of that essay, and every result was the same essay. Please verify on the page.
- [VERIFIED, bibliographic] The standard primary-source edition is A. Livingstone, *Mystical and Mythological Explanatory Works of Assyrian and Babylonian Scholars* (Oxford: Clarendon, 1986). It contains a chapter of "expositions of mystical mathematics and philology" and one on "groups of deities and parts of the world". The publisher describes these texts as attempts by ancient scribe-scholars "to explain parts of their own culture". — [Eisenbrauns reprint page](https://www.eisenbrauns.org/books/titles/978-1-57506-133-7.html); [Birmingham research record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/mystical-and-mythological-explanatory-works-of-assyrian-and-babyl)
- [SEARCH-SNIPPET] A related article by W. G. Lambert, "Mystical numbers of major gods in the second millennium", appears in the same bibliographic catalogue. — [KEIBI Tübingen catalogue](https://keibi.uni-tuebingen.de/Record/KEI00064716)

**Mesopotamia: sexagesimal background**
- Anu's number is 60, the base of the sexagesimal system, so the highest god holds the "full" unit. The descending order (60, 50, 40, 30, 20, 15, 6) mirrors pantheon rank. — [Met Museum](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/mesopotamian-deities). The interpretive link to base 60 is my inference; see section 1 Inferences.

**Mesopotamia: Enūma Anu Enlil (EAE)**
- [VERIFIED] Size and date:
  - 68 or 70 tablets, depending on the recension, with an estimated 6,500–7,000 omens.
  - A prototype existed in the Old Babylonian period (1950–1595 BCE), and the canonical form was probably compiled in the Kassite period (1595–1157 BCE).
  - The latest datable copy was written in 194 BCE.
  - Less than half of the series has been published in modern English editions.
  — [Wikipedia, Enūma Anu Enlil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enuma_anu_enlil)
- [VERIFIED] Structure:
  - Tablets 1–13: first appearances of the moon and lunar haloes or crowns.
  - Tablet 14: a mathematical scheme for lunar visibility.
  - Tablets 15–22: lunar eclipses.
  - Tablets 23–29: the sun.
  - Tablets 30–39: solar eclipses.
  - Tablets 40–49: weather and earthquakes.
  - The last roughly 20 tablets: stars and planets.
  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enuma_anu_enlil)
- [VERIFIED] Form of the omens: they follow a protasis–apodosis pattern ("If … then …"). One example: "If the moon becomes visible on the first day: reliable speech; the land will be happy." Another: "If the moon at its appearance wears a crown: the king will reach the highest rank." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enuma_anu_enlil), citing State Archives of Assyria 8, report 10
- [SEARCH-SNIPPET] Lunar-eclipse omens use the date, the night watch and the lunar quadrant to say which region or city is affected. Planetary omens sometimes replace planet names with the names of fixed stars or constellations. Rochberg-Halton edited tablets 15–22 in *Aspects of Babylonian Celestial Divination* (1988). Two ancient catalogues of the series survive, one from Assur and one from Uruk; the Uruk catalogue gives the incipits of tablets 1–26. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enuma_anu_enlil); [BMCR 2005.06.29, review of Rochberg's *The Heavenly Writing*](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/?p=24478); [Cuneiform Commentaries Project, EAE 16–19](https://ccp.yale.edu/P461229)

**Mesopotamia: MUL.APIN**
- [VERIFIED] Date and copies: most scholars date the original compilation to about 1000 BCE. Astronomical dating of its star data (Schaefer; de Jong) points to about 1400–1100 BCE. Earlier proposals of about 2300 BCE (Papke, van der Waerden) were criticised by Hunger and Pingree. The earliest known copy dates to 686 BCE and the latest to about 300 BCE. — [Wikipedia, MUL.APIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MUL.APIN)
- [VERIFIED] Tablet 1 lists about 66 stars and constellations in three "paths":
  - the path of Enlil (north, 33 entries);
  - the path of Anu (equatorial band, about ±17°, 23 entries);
  - the path of Ea (south, 15 entries).
  The stars are seen as the "seats" of gods. — [Wikipedia, MUL.APIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MUL.APIN)
- [VERIFIED] Tablet 2 contains planetary visibility data, intercalation rules (one based on star risings, one on lunar position), shadow lengths, water-clock values and omens. The text uses an ideal year of 12 × 30-day months (360 days). — [Wikipedia, MUL.APIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MUL.APIN)

**Mesopotamia: the seven-day week**
- [VERIFIED] The planetary week (days named for Saturn, Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus) first appears in Italy in the second half of the 1st century BCE. In this view there is no evidence for a planetary week "in Egypt, Mesopotamia, the Hellenistic world, or anywhere further east" before that. Bultrighini and Stern call the link between the days and the seven celestial bodies "abstract" and "artificial", derived from the "Chaldean" scheme of hour-rulers. The earliest literary explanation of the order is in Vettius Valens (mid-2nd c. CE), followed by Cassius Dio. — [I. Bultrighini, FU Berlin Zodiac blog (2022)](https://blogs.fu-berlin.de/zodiacblog/?p=148), summarising Bultrighini & Stern, "The Seven-Day Week in the Roman Empire", in S. Stern (ed.), *Calendars in the Making* (Brill, 2021), pp. 10–79; [Bultrighini chapter, UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10115451/1/Bultrighini_Chapter%2010_LATEST%20copy%202.pdf)
- [SEARCH-SNIPPET] An older reference work states that the planetary week does not appear in cuneiform tablets. It says the Babylonians did not have a seven-day week, although some days of the month were marked as "evil days". — [Schaff-Herzog Encyclopedia, "Week"](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/encyc12/htm/iii.x.lxxvi.htm)
- This contradicts the popular claim that "most historians agree the seven-day week dates back to the Babylonians". — [Names of the days of the week (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_the_days_of_the_week). See section 3.

**Egypt: the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus (RMP)**
- [VERIFIED] Date and scribe: dated to regnal year 33 of the Hyksos king Apepi (about 1550 BCE). It is a copy of an older text from the reign of Amenemhat III (12th Dynasty), made by the scribe Ahmes (Ahmose). Most of it has been in the British Museum since 1865, with fragments in the Brooklyn Museum. — [Wikipedia, Rhind Mathematical Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhind_Mathematical_Papyrus)
- [VERIFIED] The title describes the content as "Accurate reckoning for inquiring into things, and the knowledge of all things, mysteries …". The language of "mysteries" is in the title, but the content is practical. — [Wikipedia, RMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhind_Mathematical_Papyrus)
- [VERIFIED] Contents:
  - a 2/n unit-fraction table and a 1–9/10 table;
  - 87 numbered problems plus four "B" items, covering arithmetic, linear equations, areas and volumes, bread and beer exchange problems, and the geometric progression of problem 79;
  - pyramid-slope (*seked*) problems 56–60;
  - an area of a circle equivalent to π ≈ 256/81 = (16/9)² ≈ 3.16 (problems 41, 48, 50);
  - problems 80–81, which convert heqat and hinu using the so-called Horus-eye fractions.
  — [Wikipedia, RMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhind_Mathematical_Papyrus)
- [VERIFIED] Rossi points out that the Egyptian circle rule ("take 1/9 from the diameter and square the rest") "had nothing to do with the ratio between circumference and diameter, now expressed by π". The Egyptians had no concept of π as a constant. — [C. Rossi, *Architecture and Mathematics in Ancient Egypt* (Cambridge UP, 2004), Part I](https://ia601604.us.archive.org/13/items/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt.pdf)

**Egypt: the heqat fractions ("Horus-eye fractions")**
- [VERIFIED] There are six signs for subdivisions of the heqat grain measure: 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32 and 1/64. Their link to the Eye of Horus was first proposed in 1911 by the Berlin Egyptologist Georg Möller ("Die Zeichen für die Bruchteile des Hohlmaßes und das Uzatauge", *ZÄS* 48: 99–101). — [J. Ritter, "Horus-eye fractions", *Encyclopedia of Ancient History* (Wiley-Blackwell, 2013), DOI 10.1002/9781444338386.wbeah21178](https://assets.super.so/e745dfd7-232e-47c1-a806-753f45b941ba/files/5682d6ef-2e08-4bbf-91d8-81f08143574f/ritter2012-2.pdf)
- [VERIFIED] The fractions are also used in the Ebers medical papyrus for ingredient proportions, written in hieratic. — [Wikipedia, Eye of Horus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_of_Horus) (search snippet). Full discussion in section 3.

**Egypt: the calendar, decans and the Sothic cycle**
- [VERIFIED] The 36 decans were star groups that rose in sequence through the night and acted as a sidereal "clock". The sequence began with Sirius (Sothis). 36 decans × 10 days = 360 days, and 5 added (epagomenal) days give the 365-day civil year. Decan lists appear on coffin lids from the First Intermediate Period (by the 9th/10th Dynasty). Later, in Hellenistic astrology, the decans were linked to planets and given other symbolic associations; the Dendera zodiac (about 50 BCE) shows this. The Greek name *dekanoi* means "tenths". — [Wikipedia, Decan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decan)
- [VERIFIED/SEARCH-SNIPPET] Sothic cycle: 1,461 Egyptian civil years of 365 days equal 1,460 Julian years of 365¼ days. The civil year had no leap day, so it drifted against the heliacal rising of Sirius and the seasons. Censorinus (*De die natali*, 238 CE) says the cycle renewed 100 years earlier. Astronomical calculation puts that heliacal rising at 20 July 139 CE (Julian), while Censorinus' text implies 21 July. — [Wikipedia, Sothic cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sothic_cycle)

**Egypt: 42 judges**
- [SEARCH-SNIPPET, popular source] In the Book of the Dead (spell 125), the dead person recites the "Negative Confession" before Osiris and 42 judges in the Hall of the Two Truths (Maat). There was no standard text of the confession. In the Papyrus of Ani the 42 nomes are not all represented and some appear twice, yet the number 42 is kept. — [World History Encyclopedia, "The Forty-Two Judges"](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/185/the-forty-two-judges/); [Wikipedia, Assessors of Maat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assessors_of_Maat)

**Egypt: proportions of the pyramids (primary evidence)**
- [VERIFIED] Pyramid slopes were defined by the *seked*: the horizontal run in palms and fingers for a rise of 1 cubit (7 palms; 1 palm = 4 fingers). The slope of Khufu's pyramid corresponds to a seked of 5½ palms, about 51°50′. The table in Rossi's book, after Lauer, gives about 51°50′35″ for Meidum (casing), Khufu and Neuserra. Other pyramids used other sekeds, for example 5 palms 1 finger, which gives the 3-4-5 triangle. — [Rossi 2004, ch. on proportions of pyramids, Table 8](https://ia601604.us.archive.org/13/items/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt.pdf); [UF, Dimensions of the Great Pyramid (PDF)](https://web.mae.ufl.edu/uhk/DIMENSIONS-GREAT-PYRAMID.pdf) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- The divine numbers seem to grow out of sexagesimal arithmetic. Anu holds 60, the base unit, and each lower rank holds a smaller "round" sexagesimal number. Ea's 40 is two-thirds of 60. Ishtar's 15 is half of her father Sin's 30. This last relationship is an often-repeated interpretation; I could not confirm it in a source I read.
- MUL.APIN's 360-day ideal year and the Egyptian 36 × 10 decan scheme both show a schematic, "ideal" calendar sitting alongside an observed reality that needed correction: intercalation in Babylon, and 5 epagomenal days in Egypt.
- The Rhind title ("knowledge of all things, mysteries") is the main textual basis for "sacred mathematics" readings. The problems themselves concern rations, grain, land and building.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the Met essay directly (HTTP 429) or read Livingstone 1986 or Lambert's article. Two things remain unverified: which tablet attests the divine numbers (often cited as a Neo-Assyrian list), and whether other numbers are attested (e.g. Marduk, Nabu, Nergal, or variants such as Ea 40 vs. Enlil 50).
- I found no fetched source for the Venus Tablet of Ammisaduqa (EAE tablet 63), the substitute-king ritual (*šar pūḫi*), or the Babylonian "evil days" (7th, 14th, 19th, 21st, 28th of the month). They are well known in the literature but were not checked here.
- I found no fetched scholarly source on the Ennead of Heliopolis (9 gods) or on the Egyptian symbolism of 3 ("plurality"), 4 (cardinal points) and 7. Do not cite these without a source.
- I did not check the Moscow Mathematical Papyrus (truncated-pyramid volume) or temple building texts (Edfu, "stretching of the cord").

## 2. What was the religious or spiritual function (divination, cosmic order, ritual timing)?

### Takeaway
In Mesopotamia, numbers and calculation served divination and royal decision-making. Celestial signs were read as messages from the gods ("heavenly writing"). Court scholars reported omens to the Assyrian king, and some scholarly texts encoded gods with numbers. In Egypt, calculation mainly served administration and timekeeping. The calendar and the decans regulated ritual and night hours, and Maat (cosmic order and justice) framed the use of measure and the judgment of the dead. Number speculation linking measures to gods arose later, in priestly circles, distinct from the accounting scribes.

### Cited Findings

**Mesopotamia**
- [VERIFIED] *Enūma Anu Enlil* was the main reference for scholars in the Neo-Assyrian king's entourage when they wrote regular astrological reports. More than 500 such reports are published in *State Archives of Assyria* 8. Most quote the relevant omens, and many add short explanations for the king. — [Wikipedia, EAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enuma_anu_enlil)
- [SEARCH-SNIPPET] Francesca Rochberg's *The Heavenly Writing: Divination, Horoscopy, and Astronomy in Mesopotamian Culture* (Cambridge UP, 2004) has a chapter on the scribes of EAE. Its title reflects the Mesopotamian idea that the sky was a form of writing by the gods. Her later book *In the Path of the Moon* (Brill, 2010) collects her studies on celestial divination and its legacy. — [Cambridge frontmatter](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805217/16611/frontmatter/9780521716611_frontmatter.pdf); [BMCR 2005.06.29](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/?p=24478)
- [VERIFIED] MUL.APIN places the stars on the "paths" of Anu, Enlil and Ea and treats them as divine "seats". Its second tablet includes omens alongside calendar and intercalation rules, so astronomy, calendar and divination appear in a single text. — [Wikipedia, MUL.APIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MUL.APIN)
- [VERIFIED] Livingstone's corpus of "mystical" explanatory works shows scribe-scholars using numbers and wordplay to explain and correlate gods, cult objects and parts of the world. This is learned speculation within a scholarly elite. — [Eisenbrauns](https://www.eisenbrauns.org/books/titles/978-1-57506-133-7.html)
- [VERIFIED] Function of the god numbers: they encoded the gods' hierarchical rank. Deities could also be represented by symbols or emblems. — [Met Museum](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/mesopotamian-deities)

**Egypt**
- [VERIFIED] The decans marked the hours of the night and the 10-day "weeks". The heliacal rising of Sirius marked the start of the year and was associated with the Nile flood. — [Wikipedia, Decan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decan)
- [VERIFIED] Ritter on the Horus-eye link: from the 2nd millennium BCE there grew up "in priestly circles, quite distinct from the accounting scribes who used metrological signs in their professional work, an interest in numerological speculation, associating certain metrological values with gods or parts of the land of Egypt". This went as far as, "at least by the early centuries of the common era, an imagined relation between capacity measures and the Eye of Horus". His source is J. Osing, *Hieratische Papyri aus Tebtunis I* (1998). So the sacred reading of measures is real, but it is late and priestly. — [Ritter 2013](https://assets.super.so/e745dfd7-232e-47c1-a806-753f45b941ba/files/5682d6ef-2e08-4bbf-91d8-81f08143574f/ritter2012-2.pdf)
- [SEARCH-SNIPPET] Judgment of the dead: the heart was weighed against the feather of Maat in the Hall of the Two Truths, before Osiris and the 42 judges. A popular explanation says each judge stood for a nome. That explanation is framed as a hypothesis ("it is thought"), not as an established fact. — [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/185/the-forty-two-judges/)

### Inferences
- In Mesopotamia, divination rested on a correlation between heavenly and earthly events, read as signs and not as causes, according to Rochberg's framework of "heavenly writing". The growing need to predict eclipses and planetary phenomena for omen purposes probably helped drive the mathematical astronomy of the late period. This is a widespread scholarly view, but I did not verify it in a fetched text.
- In Egypt, the link between measure and Maat (just measure as part of cosmic order) is plausible, given the confession formulas and the emphasis on honest measures. I did not verify it with a specific primary text in this session.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the Ritual of the Substitute King, namburbi rituals (apotropaic), or hemerologies (lucky and unlucky days). These are key for ritual timing in Mesopotamia.
- I found no verified source on Egyptian temple rituals for the hours (the "Hour priests", *imy-wnwt*), or on Late-Period calendars of lucky and unlucky days (Cairo Calendar).
- I found no fetched source specifically on Maat and metrology, such as royal cubit rods with inscriptions or "false measure" formulas in the Negative Confession.

## 3. Which popular claims are modern or esoteric inventions, or contested by specialists?

### Takeaway
Several popular claims are contested or rejected by specialists:
- the Horus-eye fraction myth, which goes back to Möller in 1911 and was challenged by Ritter in 2002;
- a Babylonian origin of the planetary week, which Bultrighini and Stern (2021) reject;
- π or the golden ratio as design principles of the Great Pyramid, which Rossi rejects and attributes to 19th-century pyramidology and to modern concepts read back into the past;
- 4241 BCE as the "start" of the Egyptian calendar, from Eduard Meyer, now discredited.

### Cited Findings

**Horus-eye fractions**
- [VERIFIED] Ritter (2013), summarising his 2002 paper "Closing the Eye of Horus: the rise and fall of 'Horus-eye fractions'" (in J. M. Steele and A. Imhausen, eds., *Under One Sky*, Münster, pp. 297–323), makes four points:
  - The original hieratic (cursive) metrological signs, which originated in the 3rd millennium BCE, "had no pictographic (hieroglyphic) equivalents".
  - Hieroglyphic versions were invented later, mainly in the New Kingdom. They fluctuated, and they were often replaced by ordinary fraction signs.
  - Metrological signs belong to a different system from phonetic writing, which is consistent with Mesopotamian proto-cuneiform metrology (Nissen, Damerow & Englund, 1993).
  - The tie to the Eye of Horus appears only in late priestly speculation (by the early centuries CE).
  — [Ritter 2013](https://assets.super.so/e745dfd7-232e-47c1-a806-753f45b941ba/files/5682d6ef-2e08-4bbf-91d8-81f08143574f/ritter2012-2.pdf)
- [SEARCH-SNIPPET] Popular retellings say Seth tore Horus' eye into six pieces that Thoth restored. They often add that the fractions sum to 63/64, with the "missing 1/64" supplied by Thoth's magic. These are later retellings and not core ancient texts. The "missing 1/64" story in particular has no source that I found. Wikipedia notes that the long-held Egyptological belief "has been challenged". — [Wikipedia, Eye of Horus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_of_Horus); [MathWorld, Eye of Horus Fraction](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/EyeofHorusFraction.html)
- Status: [ESOTERIC/MODERN] for the claim that "the Egyptians wrote fractions with pieces of the Eye of Horus from the beginning". [TRADITIONAL, late-antique] for a priestly association between capacity measures and the Eye, which is attested at least by the Roman period.

**The seven-day planetary week as Babylonian**
- [VERIFIED] Bultrighini and Stern (2021) find no evidence of a planetary week before late-1st-century-BCE Italy. They argue it is a Roman-era construction; the "Chaldean" hour-ruler scheme is Hellenistic astrological doctrine, not cuneiform. — [FU Berlin blog](https://blogs.fu-berlin.de/zodiacblog/?p=148)
- Contradicted by popular sources claiming that "most historians agree" the week is Babylonian. — [Wikipedia, Names of the days of the week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_the_days_of_the_week). The Babylonian role is better described as an indirect one: planetary omens and celestial divination fed into Hellenistic astrology. — [Bultrighini chapter (UCL)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10115451/1/Bultrighini_Chapter%2010_LATEST%20copy%202.pdf)
- Status: [CONTESTED] for the claim that the seven planetary days are Babylonian.

**π and the golden ratio in the Great Pyramid**
- [VERIFIED] Rossi on the golden section: "there is no direct evidence in any ancient Egyptian written mathematical source of any arithmetic calculation or geometrical construction which could be classified as the Golden Section". The first explicit references are in Euclid, *Elements* VI, Def. 2 (3rd c. BCE). Convergence towards φ "and φ itself as a number, do not fit with the extant Middle Kingdom mathematical sources". She does not exclude that Greeks brought it to Egypt in the Ptolemaic period. — [Rossi 2004, pp. 67–68](https://ia601604.us.archive.org/13/items/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt.pdf); see also C. Rossi & C. A. Tout, "Were the Fibonacci Series and the Golden Section known in ancient Egypt?", *Historia Mathematica* 29 (2002), 101–13; [Times Higher Education report](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/36269)
- [VERIFIED] Rossi on esoteric readings in general: theories reading pyramid geometry "in terms of mythological or esoteric significance" are "mainly based on incorrect starting points, inappropriate connections and unjustified interpretations". They "are representative of the modern culture which generated them, rather than of the ancient culture to which they refer". Herz-Fischler showed that some are "true products of Victorian Britain". — [Rossi 2004, p. 201](https://ia601604.us.archive.org/13/items/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt.pdf)
- [VERIFIED] Origins of the pyramidological claims:
  - John Taylor, *The Great Pyramid: Why Was It Built? & Who Built It?* (1859), claimed it was built by the "Children of Israel" as a record of "the Measure of the Earth" and a universal standard.
  - Charles Piazzi Smyth, Astronomer Royal for Scotland (*Our Inheritance in the Great Pyramid*, 1864; *Life and Work at the Great Pyramid*, 1867), invented the "pyramid inch" and "sacred cubit" and discarded the royal cubit.
  - Moreux and Barbarin derived astronomical constants and "key-dates of the history of mankind". Leonard Cottrell called the pyramidologists "pyramidiots".
  — [Rossi 2004, pp. 201–202](https://ia601604.us.archive.org/13/items/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt.pdf)
- [VERIFIED] Rossi's positive explanation: Borchardt (*Gegen die Zahlenmystik an der Grossen Pyramide bei Gise*, 1922), Lauer and Gay Robins showed that pyramid geometry can be explained with Egyptian tools, namely simple ratios and the seked, "there is no need to invoke concepts for which we have no evidence that they were known". Robins and Shute rebutted Legon's claims about irrational numbers (*Discussions in Egyptology* 16 and 18). — [Rossi 2004, p. 202](https://ia601604.us.archive.org/13/items/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt.pdf)
- [VERIFIED] Herodotus: the golden-ratio theory leans on a disputed reading of Herodotus. Herodotus (*Hist.* II.126) actually says that Khufu's pyramid had a base equal to its height, which corresponds to neither φ nor π. A Springer paper in the *Nexus Network Journal* (2014) calls the Herodotus basis an "erroneous interpretation". It still notes that the measured dimensions come close to φ, without proving intent. — [Rossi 2004, p. 70 n. 41](https://ia601604.us.archive.org/13/items/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt.pdf); [Nexus Network Journal, DOI 10.1007/s00004-014-0193-9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00004-014-0193-9) (abstract only; paywalled)
- [VERIFIED] The π approximation in the Rhind Papyrus is (16/9)² ≈ 3.16, not 22/7 or 3.1416. The Egyptian circle rule is not a ratio of circumference to diameter. — [Wikipedia, RMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhind_Mathematical_Papyrus); [Rossi 2004](https://ia601604.us.archive.org/13/items/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt/ArchitectureAndMathematicsInAncientEgypt.pdf)
- Why both "π" and "φ" appear in Khufu's pyramid:
  - A seked of 5½ palms gives a slope whose ratio of half-base to height is 5.5/7. So the ratio of base perimeter to height is 4 × 2 × 5.5/7 = 44/7 ≈ 2 × 3.1429. This produces a "22/7 ≈ π" ratio automatically. The same slope also gives an apothem/half-base ratio of about 1.62, close to φ.
  - Hence the near-coincidences follow from choosing a simple seked; they do not require knowledge of π or φ.
  - This is my calculation, based on the seked definition. It is consistent with Rossi's argument, but this precise wording is not hers. Cite it as an inference.
- Status: [ESOTERIC/MODERN] for π or φ as intended design principles; [VERIFIED] for the seked of 5½ palms.

**The Sothic "start date" of 4241 BCE**
- [VERIFIED/SEARCH-SNIPPET] Eduard Meyer used Censorinus' date of 139 CE to place the creation of the civil calendar in 4241 BCE ("the first date in history"). Recent scholarship has discredited this. Leo Depuydt calls the assumption that the calendar began with a coincidence of Sirius and New Year "unsubstantiated". — [Wikipedia, Sothic cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sothic_cycle); [Depuydt essay (Hungaricana)](https://library.hungaricana.hu/hu/view/ORSZ_SZEP_Bmhbas_01_Supplement/?pg=87); [Meyer's Sothic hypothesis (UCA repository)](https://repositorio.uca.edu.ar/bitstream/123456789/11812/1/hipotesis-sotiaca-eduard-meyer.pdf)
- Status: [DISCREDITED] for the 4241 BCE date.

**The 42 judges and the 42 nomes**
- [SEARCH-SNIPPET] The equation "42 judges = 42 nomes" is a hypothesis. The number of nomes varied over time, and the lists do not map neatly onto the judges. — [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/185/the-forty-two-judges/)
- Modern "42 Laws of Maat" or "42 Ideals of Maat" lists, found on New Age sites, are positive paraphrases of the Negative Confession. They are not an ancient law code. — [Example of a modern list (Obsidian publish)](https://publish.obsidian.md/findingtruth/Documents/42+Laws+of+Ma'at). This is my classification, based on the fact that the ancient text is a list of denials in BCE spell 125.
- Status: [TRADITIONAL/HYPOTHESIS] for the 42 judges as nomes; [MODERN] for the "42 Laws of Maat".

### Inferences
- The scholarly consensus is not that "the ancients had no sacred numbers". Real but late and elite speculation existed: Babylonian god numbers and mystical expositions, and Egyptian priestly numerology of the Late and Roman periods. Modern esotericism often projects it back onto earlier eras, such as the Old Kingdom pyramids and the 3rd-millennium metrology.
- Mesopotamian divine numbers (from the 2nd millennium BCE) are better attested in primary sources than most Egyptian number-symbolism claims that circulate in popular literature.
