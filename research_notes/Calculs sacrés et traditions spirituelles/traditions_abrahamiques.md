# Sacred Calculation in the Abrahamic Traditions (Judaism, Christianity, Islam)

Scope: antiquity through the medieval period, with notes on modern derivatives (Bible codes, Code 19). Each section separates **verified facts** (sourced), **traditional interpretations** (what practitioners claimed), and **unverifiable claims**. Research note: the Firecrawl tool was out of credits, so the research used WebSearch/WebFetch. Several sources are Wikipedia summaries that point to primary or scholarly works. Where a Wikipedia article itself flags weak sourcing, that is noted.

## Q1. What methods are attested, from when, and in which texts?

### Takeaway
Hebrew gematria is first named in rabbinic literature, as rule 29 of the 32 hermeneutic rules attributed to R. Eliezer b. Jose ha-Gelili (c. 200 CE). Its techniques grew from simple letter-sums into the many value systems of medieval Kabbalah. The oldest claimed name-number link is Assyrian: Sargon II (8th c. BCE). Christian use is visible in Revelation 13:18 (666/616) and the Epistle of Barnabas. The Islamic abjad system (letters 1–1000) came before positional numerals. The letter-science of al-Būnī (d. 1225) dates to the early 13th century, but the famous *Shams al-maʿārif al-kubrā* is largely a 17th-century product.

### Cited Findings

**Judaism: earliest attestations**
- Assyrian precedent (verified fact, primary-source translation cited): an 8th-century BCE inscription of Sargon II says "the king built the wall of Khorsabad 16,283 cubits long to correspond with the numerical value of his name." Wikipedia cites Luckenbill, *Ancient Records of Assyria and Babylonia*. — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
  - Caveat: how the 16,283 value is computed from "Sargon" is not explained in the source, and the Jewish Encyclopedia article does not mention this claim. Treat it as an Assyrian name-number correspondence, not Hebrew gematria.
- Hebrew letters used as numerals first appear in 78 BCE. Greek Milesian alphabetic numerals appear in 6th-century BCE vase graffiti. Aristotle attributed isopsephy to the Pythagoreans. — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
- A Pompeii graffito (before 79 CE) reads "I love the girl whose name is phi mu epsilon (545)". This is a secular Greek isopsephy. — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
- Etymology: commonly derived from Greek *geometria*. The article's lead also gives Greek *grammateia* via Aramaic. The article contradicts itself here. — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
- Rabbinic origin: "The term first occurs in literature in the twenty-ninth of the thirty-two hermeneutic rules" of R. Eliezer b. R. Jose the Galilean (c. 200). Some texts count permutative gematria as a separate 30th rule. Symbolic numbers fall under the 27th rule ("ke-neged"). — [Jewish Encyclopedia, "Gematria" (Schechter & Levias)](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)
- The Jewish Encyclopedia says the Hebrew Bible has no clear numerical gematria. It does have permutative examples: Jer. 25:26 and 51:1 ("Sheshach" = Babel by atbash). — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)

**Judaism: methods**
- *Mispar hechrechi* (standard value, 1–400 for the 22 letters); *mispar gadol* (final forms 500–900); *mispar katan* (zeros dropped); *otiyot be-milui* (value of each letter's spelled-out name); also *mispar boneh*, *kidmi*, *prati*, *katan mispari* (digit reduction). — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
- Substitution ciphers (temurah family): *atbash* swaps first and last letters (aleph↔tav, bet↔shin…). *Albam* splits the alphabet into two halves of 11 and swaps across them. — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
- Rabbinic-era techniques listed by the Jewish Encyclopedia:
  - normal, "minor" (cyclical) and "external" values
  - letter permutation
  - interchange of homorganic letters
  - adding or dropping vowel letters
  - counting only parts of words, or reading them by **notarikon** (initials or abbreviations)
  - *al tiqre* (re-reading a word)
  - adding the number of words
  — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)
- In Kabbalah, the Jewish Encyclopedia counts about 22 kinds of numerical value (square, cube, "integration," "quaternion" etc.). It notes that the early kabbalists fixed nine main values. — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)
- Wikipedia defines *notarikon* and *temurah* only briefly ("Kabbalistic method of deriving a word" / "method in Kabbalah"). No detailed source was retrieved. — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)

**Judaism: classic examples (with text references)**
- *Yayin* (wine, יין) = *sod* (secret, סוד) = 70; BT Sanhedrin 38a ("wine enters, secret goes out"). — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
- *Chai* (חי, "alive") = 18, the basis of 18 as a lucky number. This is popular custom; the source gives no ancient text for it. — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
- *HaSatan* (השטן) = 364. BT Yoma 20a reads this as Satan having power on 364 days but not on Yom Kippur. — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
- Gen. 14:14: Abraham's 318 men = "Eliezer" (BT Nedarim 32a). — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)
- Other rabbinic examples:
  - Esth. 3:11: Haman's money and gallows both = 165 (Esther Rabbah)
  - Prov. 22:20: 606, plus the 7 Noahide laws = 613 (Tanhuma)
  - Deut. 32:1–6: initial letters = 345 = "Moses"
  — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)

**Judaism: Sefer Yetzirah, Zohar, Lurianic Kabbalah**
- *Sefer Yetzirah* describes creation through "32 paths of wisdom": 10 sefirot (numbers) plus 22 letters. The letters are 3 "mothers" (Aleph, Mem, Shin), 7 "doubles" (Bet, Gimel, Dalet, Kaph, Pe, Resh, Taw) and 12 "simples". — [Wikipedia, Sefer Yetzirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefer_Yetzirah)
- Dating is disputed:
  - 2nd c. BCE (Reitzenstein)
  - c. 2nd c. CE (Benton)
  - 3rd–4th c. (Jewish Encyclopedia)
  - 9th c. in an Islamic setting (Wasserstrom)
  - Most scholars place it in the Talmudic period. Tradition attributes it to Abraham.
  — [Wikipedia, Sefer Yetzirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefer_Yetzirah)
- Recensions: a short version (about 1,300 words; basis of the 1562 Mantua editio princeps) and a long version (about 2,500 words). Commentaries: Saadia Gaon (10th c.) and Shabbetai Donnolo on the long version; Dunash ibn Tamim / Jacob ben Nissim on the short version. BT Sanhedrin 65b tells of R. Hanina and R. Hoshaiah studying a "Sefer Yetzirah" on Sabbath eves. — [Wikipedia, Sefer Yetzirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefer_Yetzirah)
  - Note: the source gives no exact date for Saadia's commentary (it is often given as 931). That date is unverified here.
- *Zohar*: Moses de León (c. 1240–1305) circulated it in Spain as the teaching of Shimon bar Yochai (2nd c.). Most modern scholars, following Scholem's conclusion (reached by 1938), hold that de León wrote it, c. 1280–1286. Scholem's evidence: Aramaic grammatical errors, Spanish and Arabic traces, and ignorance of the Land of Israel. Some scholars propose multiple medieval authors. Isaac of Acre reports that de León's widow said he wrote it himself. — [Wikipedia, Zohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohar)
- Lurianic Kabbalah: Isaac Luria (1534–1572) taught in Safed after Cordovero. Hayyim Vital's *Etz Hayyim* is the main text. Core concepts:
  - *tzimtzum* (divine contraction)
  - *shevirah* (shattering of the vessels)
  - *partzufim*
  - *tikkun*
  - *kavanot*: meditations on divine letter permutations during prayer; "Every word and syllable of the prescribed prayers contain hidden names of God."
  — [Wikipedia, Lurianic Kabbalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurianic_Kabbalah)
- Abraham Abulafia (b. Zaragoza 1240, d. after 1291) founded "prophetic Kabbalah," which he also called the "Kabbalah of names." His method combined letter permutations, breathing, head movements and visualization, and he studied the consonants of the Tetragrammaton. He applied gematria to words in Greek, Latin, Italian, Arabic, Tatar and Basque. Works include *Chayei ha-Olam ha-Ba* (1280) and *Or ha-Sekhel*. — [Wikipedia, Abraham Abulafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Abulafia)

**Christianity**
- Revelation 13:18: 666 in most manuscripts. Papyrus 115 (Oxyrhynchus; the oldest manuscript of Rev. 13) and Codex Ephraemi Rescriptus read 616. — [Wikipedia, Number of the beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_of_the_beast)
- Nero gematria (scholarly consensus): Hebrew נרון קסר (*Nrwn Qsr*) = 666. The Latin-style spelling without the final nun, נרו קסר, = 616, which likely explains the variant (Hillers 1963; Cory 2006). — [Wikipedia, Number of the beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_of_the_beast)
- Irenaeus (*Adv. Haer.* 5.30) knew of 616 but defended 666, citing those who had seen John "face to face". He proposed the solutions Evanthas, Teitan and Lateinos, calling Lateinos "very probable". — [Wikipedia, Number of the beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_of_the_beast)
- Epistle of Barnabas 9:6–7 reads Abraham's 318 servants (Gen. 14:14) as pointing to Jesus. This is the same verse the rabbis read as "Eliezer". — [Wikipedia, Gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)
- John 21:11, the 153 fish:
  - **Augustine**: 153 is the triangular number of 17 (1+2+…+17), and 17 = 10 (commandments, Law) + 7 (gifts of the Spirit, grace).
  - **Jerome** (via Cornelius a Lapide): naturalists counted 153 kinds of fish, so the catch means all kinds of people.
  - **Cyril** (via Lapide): 100 = the fullness of the Gentiles, 50 = the Jews, 3 = the Trinity.
  - **Rupert / Maldonatus**: 100 married, 50 widows, 3 virgins.
  - Skeptical modern view: D.A. Carson ("he has hidden it well") and the *Dictionary of Biblical Imagery* ("No symbolic significance… has received widespread support").
  — [Wikipedia, Miraculous catch of fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraculous_catch_of_fish)
  - The Jerome–Oppian link and the Ezekiel 47 (En-gedi/En-eglaim) gematria reading (J.A. Emerton) were not confirmed in sources retrieved.
- Augustine, *City of God* 11.30: creation took six days because 6 is a perfect number (its divisors 1+2+3 = 6). He cites Wisdom 11:20 ("thou hast ordered all things in measure and number and weight"). Parallels: *De Genesi ad litteram* 4.2 and *De Trinitate* 4.7. — [Augustine, City of God 11.30 (BKV, Univ. Fribourg)](https://bkv.unifr.ch/en/works/cpl-313/versions/the-city-of-god/divisions/331); [Logos Library text](https://logoslibrary.org/augustine/city/1130.html)

**Christianity: computus (Easter dating)**
- Key dates:
  - **c. 277**: Anatolius of Laodicea proposes a 19-year (Metonic) cycle. The Alexandrian method became authoritative only in the late 4th century.
  - **325, Nicaea**: gave no explicit computation rules. It directed the East to celebrate Easter with Rome and Alexandria.
  - **457**: Victorius of Aquitaine's 532-year table, which had errors and was used in Gaul and Spain until the late 8th century.
  - **525**: Dionysius Exiguus' table (532–616), in which he also introduced the AD era.
  - **630**: Synod of Magh-Lene; the southern Irish adopt the Dionysian tables.
  - **664**: Synod of Whitby; Northumbria adopts them.
  - **725**: Bede's *De temporum ratione* fully explains Dionysian reckoning and notes the equinox drifting from 21 March.
  - **1582**: Gregorian reform.
  — [Wikipedia, Computus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computus)
- The older British Isles tables used an 84-year cycle, which made full moons fall progressively early. The cycle is also associated with Augustalis in Rome (late 3rd c.). — [Wikipedia, Computus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computus)

**Islam: abjad, ʿilm al-ḥurūf, magic squares**
- Abjad orders:
  - **Mashriqi (Eastern)**: ا ب ج د ه و ز ح ط ي ك ل م ن س ع ف ص ق ر ش ت ث خ ذ ض ظ غ. Mnemonic: *abjad hawwaz ḥuṭṭī kalaman saʿfaṣ qarashat thakhadh ḍaẓagh*.
  - **Maghribi (Western)**: differs in the places of س/ش, ص/ض and ظ/غ.
  — [Wikipedia, Abjad numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abjad_numerals)
- Values, Mashriqi (Maghribi in brackets where different):
  - Units: ا1 ب2 ج3 د4 ه5 و6 ز7 ح8 ط9
  - Tens: ي10 ك20 ل30 م40 ن50 س60 [ص] ع70 ف80 ص90 [ض]
  - Hundreds and above: ق100 ر200 ش300 [س] ت400 ث500 خ600 ذ700 ض800 [ظ] ظ900 [غ] غ1000 [ش]
  - Persian additions: پ2 چ3 ژ7 گ20
  — [Wikipedia, Abjad numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abjad_numerals)
- Abjad numerals were used in the Arab world before positional numerals were adopted (8th c.), citing Chrisomalis, *Numerical Notation* (2010). The orders follow the 22-letter Semitic sequence, then extend past taw (400) with the six additional Arabic letters. Main scholarly source: M.C.A. Macdonald (1986). The article carries a "needs more citations" banner. — [Wikipedia, Abjad numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abjad_numerals)
- *Ḥisāb al-jummal*: letter-value numerology. The Basmala = **786**; *Allāh* = 66. — [Wikipedia, Abjad numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abjad_numerals)
- Al-Būnī (d. 1225; from Būna/Annaba, active in Ayyubid Egypt; contemporary of Ibn ʿArabī) wrote on the esoteric value of letters and on the 99 names. Around 1200 he showed how to construct magic squares by a bordering technique, though he may not have invented it. He also built 4×4 Latin squares from the letters of a divine name. — [Wikipedia, Ahmad al-Buni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_al-Buni)
- Noah Gardiner, "Forbidden Knowledge? Notes on the Production, Transmission, and Reception of the Major Works of Aḥmad al-Būnī" (*Journal of Arabic and Islamic Studies* 12, 2012):
  - The *Shams al-maʿārif al-kubrā* is "in significant ways a product of the early 11th/17th century".
  - Teacher-lineages attributed to al-Būnī in that work were plagiarized from ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Bisṭāmī (15th c.).
  - Five works can be attributed to al-Būnī most securely.
  — [Gardiner, JAIS 12 (PDF)](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/jais/volume/docs/vol12/v12_04_Gardiner_081-143.pdf); [JAIS article page](https://journals.uio.no/JAIS/article/view/4618)
- Further key studies on al-Būnī's letter magic: Pierre Lory, "La magie des lettres dans le *Shams al-maʿārif*" (1987), and J.-C. Coulon's thesis on the *corpus bunianum*. Coulon (2022) is cited for doubts about authorship. — [Wikipedia, Ahmad al-Buni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_al-Buni); search snippets
- Matthew Melvin-Koushki calls lettrism (*ʿilm al-ḥurūf*) "Hebrew kabbalah's coeval Arabic twin" and the most Islamic of the occult sciences, because it focuses on the Qurʾān and the Imams. Ibn Turka of Isfahan (d. 835/1432) developed a formal lettrist metaphysics. Lettrism also flourished in the Timurid, Aqquyunlu and Safavid periods. — [Melvin-Koushki, "Islamic Occult" (Brown Univ. PDF)](https://library.brown.edu/create/performing-objects/wp-content/uploads/sites/83/2021/06/Melvin-Koushki-Islamic-Occult-WM.pdf); [Melvin-Koushki, "Of Islamic Grammatology", Al-ʿUṣūr al-Wusṭā](https://journals.library.columbia.edu/index.php/alusur/article/view/7018)

**Islam: ritual time and direction**
- The *muwaqqit* was a mosque-employed astronomer who calculated prayer times, built instruments and wrote treatises. By the 13th century this was a widespread institution. — [Religious calculations with the astrolabe (Cambridge HPS)](https://www.sites.hps.cam.ac.uk/starry/isaslabereligion.html); [Khalili entry, Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers (McGill)](https://islamsci.mcgill.ca/RASI/BEA/Khalili_BEA.htm)
- Shams al-Dīn al-Khalīlī (muwaqqit, Umayyad Mosque, Damascus, c. 1365) compiled prayer-time tables for the latitude of Damascus and a universal qibla table. Determining the qibla is "one of the most complicated problems of medieval Islamic trigonometry". David King judged the table highly accurate. — [Khalili BEA (McGill)](https://islamsci.mcgill.ca/RASI/BEA/Khalili_BEA.htm)
- David King's studies (*Astronomy in the Service of Islam*, Variorum 1993; *In Synchrony with the Heavens*; *The Call of the Muezzin*, Brill 2004) cover the lunar calendar, the times of the five prayers, and the qibla. — [Routledge/Variorum listing](https://www.routledge.com/products/isbn/9780860783572); [Khalili BEA](https://islamsci.mcgill.ca/RASI/BEA/Khalili_BEA.htm)

### Inferences
- Alphanumeric calculation is shared across Greek, Hebrew and Arabic because each alphabet doubled as a numeral system. Gematria, isopsephy and *ḥisāb al-jummal* are parallel developments of the same affordance, not separate inventions. Acevedo (2020), *Alphanumeric Cosmology from Greek into Arabic*, is cited on this transmission in the Wikipedia Gematria bibliography.
- The Islamic case uses "sacred calculation" in two distinct senses that should be kept apart:
  - **exact-science calculation**: computus-like work on prayer times, qibla and the calendar, which was mainstream and mosque-institutionalized.
  - **esoteric letter-number calculation**: *ʿilm al-ḥurūf*, *wafq*, which was contested.
  Christianity's computus is the counterpart of the first sense.

### Gaps
- The method behind Sargon's 16,283 value was not found.
- No source was retrieved for the full Baraita of 32 Middot text. It is sometimes attributed to a later, possibly Geonic, compilation, and its date was not verified.
- Chronograms (*tārīkh*, *māddat al-tārīkh*): the abjad article did not cover them. No sourced Persian, Ottoman or Mughal examples were retrieved.
- The South Asian practice of writing "786" in place of the Basmala: only the 786 value is sourced. The custom itself (on letterheads, shop signs) and the ʿulamāʾ debates over it were not sourced.
- Hijri calendar facts were not verified in this session: lunar year of about 354/355 days, era starting 622 CE under ʿUmar, crescent sighting versus calculation debates. Neither were the standard definitions of prayer times by shadow length and solar depression.
- The Ezekiel 47 / 153 gematria (Emerton 1958) and the Jerome–Oppian source were not verified.
- No sourced passage was retrieved for Bede beyond *De temporum ratione*; his *De temporibus* (703) is unverified. *De Trinitate* number passages are known only via cross-reference (4.7). The Christian symbolism of 3, 7, 12 and 40 was not specifically sourced in this session.
- Moshe Idel's and Scholem's specific works (e.g., Idel, *The Mystical Experience in Abraham Abulafia*) are known only as bibliography entries and were not read. The specific milui names (72/63/45/52) used in Lurianic kavanot were not sourced.

## Q2. What spiritual/religious utility did practitioners claim?

### Takeaway
Practitioners in all three traditions claimed four kinds of use: (1) **hermeneutic** (finding hidden links in scripture, or serving as a mnemonic); (2) **cosmological** (creation through numbers and letters); (3) **contemplative/theurgic** (meditation on divine names to reach prophecy or to "repair" the divine); and (4) **practical-protective** (amulets, talismans, magic squares). Exact-science calculation (computus, prayer times, qibla) served correct ritual observance.

### Cited Findings
- Talmudists may have treated gematria only as an *asmakhta*, a supporting hint or mnemonic, and not as a basis for law. — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)
- *Sefer Yetzirah*: God created the world through the 10 sefirot and 22 letters ("32 paths of wisdom"). — [Wikipedia, Sefer Yetzirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefer_Yetzirah)
- Abulafia's aim was a "prophetic" state, unitive experience and revelation, reached through letter permutation of divine names together with breathing and visualization. His handbooks were more influential than his commentaries. — [Wikipedia, Abraham Abulafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Abulafia)
- Luria's kavanot: meditating on the hidden divine names in each word of prayer, which contributes to *tikkun* (liberating divine sparks). — [Wikipedia, Lurianic Kabbalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurianic_Kabbalah)
- Augustine: number reveals the perfection of God's work. Six days signify perfection, not divine need of time (Wis 11:20, "measure, number and weight"). — [City of God 11.30](https://bkv.unifr.ch/en/works/cpl-313/versions/the-city-of-god/divisions/331)
- Patristic allegory of the 153 fish signified the universality of the Church, or law plus grace. — [Wikipedia, Miraculous catch of fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraculous_catch_of_fish)
- Computus served to celebrate Easter on the same day across Churches; this was Nicaea's concern. — [Wikipedia, Computus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computus)
- Al-Būnī's works treat the magical and devotional uses of the 99 Names of God and letter-based magic squares (*wafq*). Later reception, often through the pseudo-Būnian *Shams*, spread talismanic practice across West Africa, the Maghrib and Southeast Asia (Wikipedia cites Martin, Cornell, Sanni, van Bruinessen). — [Wikipedia, Ahmad al-Buni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_al-Buni)
- Lettrism as metaphysics: Ibn Turka's "metaphysics of writing", and philosophical lettrism in Mamluk–Timurid–Aqquyunlu thought (Melvin-Koushki). — [Al-ʿUṣūr al-Wusṭā](https://journals.library.columbia.edu/index.php/alusur/article/view/7018)
- Prayer-time and qibla tables served the correct performance of the five daily prayers toward the Kaʿba. — [Khalili BEA](https://islamsci.mcgill.ca/RASI/BEA/Khalili_BEA.htm)

### Inferences
- The claimed uses run along a spectrum from "mnemonic/homiletic" (rabbinic *asmakhta*, patristic allegory) to "operative/theurgic" (Abulafia, Luria, al-Būnī). How orthodox authorities reacted tracks this spectrum closely (see Q3).

### Gaps
- The specific magical uses of Hebrew gematria in amulets (e.g., Sefer Raziel, Shimmush Tehillim) were not sourced.
- Ibn ʿArabī's lettrist doctrine (*Futūḥāt*, ch. 2, "science of letters"; trans. Gril) is cited only as a reference. Its content was not read.

## Q3. What orthodox/critical positions existed within each tradition?

### Takeaway
Each tradition had internal critics:
- **Judaism**: from Ibn Ezra onward, rationalists belittled gematria, and the Talmud itself treated it as non-binding. Abulafia was attacked by Solomon ibn Adret, and the Zohar's antiquity was contested (Delmedigo, Emden).
- **Christianity**: allegorical numerology was mainstream patristic practice. Modern exegetes are skeptical of symbolic readings (e.g., the 153 fish).
- **Islam**: Ibn Khaldūn and Ibn Taymiyya condemned letter-science as sorcery (*siḥr*), despite its religious trappings.

### Cited Findings
- Outside kabbalistic circles, starting with Ibn Ezra, "the value of gemaṭria is spoken of more or less derogatorily". Critics named: Joel Särkes, Leon of Modena, Milsahagi, Zweifel. — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)
- The Talmudists may have treated gematria only as an *asmakhta*, a supporting hint and not a legal source. — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)
- Around 1285 the Palermo community appealed to Solomon ben Adret (Rashba) of Barcelona, who wrote against Abulafia. This was a major reason Abulafian Kabbalah was excluded from Spanish schools. — [Wikipedia, Abraham Abulafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Abulafia)
- Zohar authenticity was contested:
  - **Elia del Medigo** (c. 1458–1493), *Beḥinat ha-Dat*: the Talmud would mention it; it names later rabbis.
  - **Jacob Emden** (d. 1776), *Mitpaḥat Sefarim*: anachronisms such as Crusades, the Portuguese word "esnoga" and vowel-point mysticism.
  — [Wikipedia, Zohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohar)
- Ibn Khaldūn (d. 808/1406) gave a "stern admonition that, despite its religious trappings, the occult science of letters was in reality a form of sorcery (siḥr)". Ibn Taymiyya made a similar charge against al-Būnī and others. — [Gardiner, JAIS 12](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/jais/volume/docs/vol12/v12_04_Gardiner_081-143.pdf) (as summarized in search results)
- The title of Gardiner's article ("Forbidden Knowledge?") reflects how the al-Būnī corpus was received: restricted and esoteric, and controversial. — [JAIS article page](https://journals.uio.no/JAIS/article/view/4618)
- Modern Christian scholarly skepticism about the symbolism of 153: Carson; *Dictionary of Biblical Imagery*. — [Wikipedia, Miraculous catch of fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraculous_catch_of_fish)
- Irenaeus warned against the 616 reading and was cautious about naming the Antichrist (he offered several candidate names). — [Wikipedia, Number of the beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_of_the_beast)

### Inferences
- The orthodox line is drawn differently in each tradition. In Judaism the question was mainly legal authority: gematria could not establish halakha. In Islam it was mainly theological and legal: letter-magic was seen as *siḥr* that compromises *tawḥīd*. The exact sciences of time and direction (computus, *ʿilm al-mīqāt*) were not condemned in either Christianity or Islam.

### Gaps

## Q4. Which modern claims (Bible codes, Code 19) are statistically refuted?

### Takeaway
The Torah/Bible code (equidistant letter sequences) was published by Witztum, Rips and Rosenberg (WRR) in *Statistical Science* in 1994. McKay, Bar-Natan, Bar-Hillel and Kalai (*Statistical Science* 14(2), 1999, pp. 150–173) refuted it: the result reflects experimenters' choices, and comparable "codes" appear in *War and Peace*. Rashad Khalifa's "Code 19" (1974) depends on selective counting, chosen orthographic variants and the excision of Q 9:128–129. It has never been independently validated, and mainstream Muslim scholars reject it.

### Cited Findings

**Bible code**
- The ELS technique reads letters at fixed skips. Precedents: Bachya ben Asher (13th c.) described an ELS, and Michael Ber Weissmandl later searched for them by hand. — [Wikipedia, Bible code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_code)
- WRR 1994 in *Statistical Science* claimed that the names and dates of famous rabbis were encoded in Genesis. The journal presented the paper as a "challenging puzzle". — [Wikipedia, Bible code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_code)
- McKay, Bar-Natan, Bar-Hillel and Kalai (1999), "Solving the Bible Code Puzzle", *Statistical Science* 14(2): 150–173. Conclusions:
  - WRR's case is "fatally defective".
  - The result "merely reflects the choices made in designing the experiment and collecting the data".
  - New experiments failed to detect the phenomenon.
  — [Paper PDF (Univ. Toronto)](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/drorbn/Codes/StatSci.pdf); [ANU copy](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/dilugim/StatSci/MBBK.pdf); [ANU research portal](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/solving-the-bible-code-puzzle/)
- The War and Peace test: the first 78,064 letters of the Hebrew *War and Peace* were run with a tuned list of rabbis' appellations, giving a comparable "success". Witztum disputed this. — [Chance News, "There are Codes in War and Peace Too"](https://chance.dartmouth.edu/chance_news/full_text/6.12/CodesinWarPeace.html); [ANU StatSci page](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/codes/statsci/)
- The journal's editor later agreed the puzzle appeared solved. Defenders: Harold Gans, the WRR authors, Robert Haralick. Robert Aumann found no confirmation but did not rule the codes out. — [Wikipedia, Bible code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_code)
- Drosnin's *The Bible Code* (1997) popularized predictive claims. Rips and Gans disavowed them, and predictions about Arafat and Libya failed. Masoretic manuscripts vary in letter count, which undermines any exact-letter-skip theory. — [Wikipedia, Bible code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_code)

**Code 19**
- Khalifa (Egyptian-American biochemist) began analysing the Qurʾānic disconnected letters in 1969 and published *Miracle of the Quran* (1973). In 1974 he claimed a 19-based code, presented in *The Computer Speaks*. His key verse was Q 74:30 ("Over it are nineteen"). — [Wikipedia, Quran code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_code)
- Claimed counts:
  - The Basmala has 19 letters.
  - *ism* 19×, *Allāh* 2698 (19×142), *raḥmān* 57 (19×3), *raḥīm* 114 (19×6).
  - 114 suras = 19×6.
  - 6346 verses = 19×334.
  — [Wikipedia, Quran code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_code)
- Khalifa declared Q 9:128–129 human interpolations, citing Bukhari 7425 (Zayd found them with only one witness, Khuzayma al-Anṣārī). With those verses included, *Allāh* = 2699 and *raḥīm* = 115, neither divisible by 19. The excision is therefore needed to keep the code working. — [Wikipedia, Quran code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_code); [Wikipedia, Rashad Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashad_Khalifa)
- Methodological critiques:
  - He chose orthographic variants whose counts fit 19.
  - For Sura 68 he proposed spelling the letter Nūn out as nūn-wāw-nūn to reach 133 (19×7) instead of 132. No manuscript has this spelling.
  - For 7:69 he chose a sīn-spelling of *basṭatan* from the Samarkand codex.
  - The code is applied mainly to the initialled suras.
  - No independent validation exists.
  — [Wikipedia, Quran code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_code)
- Bilal Philips (1987) called the "miracle 19" a hoax based on falsified data, misreadings and grammatical inconsistencies. Martin Gardner discussed it in *Scientific American* (1980) and *Skeptical Inquirer* (1997). Most Muslims reject Khalifa's interpretation. — [Wikipedia, Quran code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_code); [Wikipedia, Rashad Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashad_Khalifa)
- Caveat on sourcing: the Wikipedia "Quran code" article carries a banner warning of possible undue weight to fringe views, and its pro-code counts come from Edip Yüksel's *Nineteen*. — [Wikipedia, Quran code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_code)

### Inferences
- Both modern claims share the same structural flaw. The researchers had many free choices (which spellings, which names, which text edition, which verses count), and those choices inflate apparent significance. This is what statisticians call "researcher degrees of freedom" or the look-elsewhere effect. In both cases the text had to be adjusted, by choosing appellations (WRR) or excising verses (Khalifa), for the pattern to hold.
- These modern claims differ from medieval practice. Medieval gematria and *jummal* were mostly presented as hermeneutic, devotional or operative. The modern claims present themselves as *statistical proof* of divine authorship, which makes them empirically testable, and they failed the test.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed statistical paper specifically testing Code 19 was retrieved. The refutations found are methodological and theological, not formal statistical tests.
- The source for Khalifa's assassination (Tucson, 1990) was not retrieved. The Quran code article does not mention it, so it is left unverified here.
- Witztum's reply to the 1999 paper and Haralick's later work were not read in detail.
