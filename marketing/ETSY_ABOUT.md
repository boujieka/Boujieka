## 2. Etsy shop, "About" section (story)

**Story headline (EN)**

Mini-grid finance models, built from twenty years of practice in Africa

**Story (EN, version A)**
```
I am Emmanuel Boujieka Kamga, a climate finance and energy-access specialist working from Yaoundé and Brazzaville.

Over twenty years and 21 African markets, I have prepared climate fund proposals, appraised power and off-grid transactions, and designed results-based and viability-gap instruments for national mini-grid programmes. That work includes a portfolio of 129 mini-grids in Togo and a technical assistance programme in Guinea covering feasibility studies for 47 mini-grids and the competitive procurement of 57. In total I have co-structured more than USD 1 billion of renewable energy and infrastructure finance.

On almost every file, the same question came back. The project makes sense for the community, but the tariff people can pay does not cover the cost of the kWh. How much subsidy closes that gap, in what form, and can the project still repay its debt? Generic solar models do not answer it, so I built these tools: for developers preparing a bankable application, and for fund managers allocating RBF and grants across a pipeline.

Each model is an unlocked Excel file with readable formulas, an English and a French version, and a PDF manual. My background is engineering (Master's in mechanical engineering) and finance (MBA, HEC Montréal; Certificate of Mastery in Project Finance and PPPs, New York Institute of Finance).

If a model does not fit your programme's rules, send me a message.
```

## What not to write

* No current mandate (UNDP, Mwinda Energy Access Fund) in any marketing text.
* No logo of any institution, and no wording that suggests an institution endorses or uses the models.
* No figure that the CV does not support. The USD 30 million of RBF approved is a current investment committee decision: keep it out of marketing.
* No phone numbers, email addresses or references on Etsy: buyers contact you through Etsy messages.
